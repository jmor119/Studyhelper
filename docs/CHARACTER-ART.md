# Character art

Students collect characters by levelling up: a mystery egg per level (see `packages/shared/src/rewards.ts`).
The NZ crew are hand-written SVGs in `apps/web/public/characters/<id>.svg`. Robots (every 5th level) are
generated by `apps/web/src/components/rewards/robot.ts` from a 6-hex-digit seed.

To add a character: add it to `CHARACTERS` in `rewards.ts` (with a true, kid-friendly fact), draw the SVG
following the rules below, and run `npx vitest run scripts/characters.test.ts`, which checks every file.
Look at it at 32 px on a coloured circle before you commit it.

## The reference character
`apps/web/public/characters/kiwi.svg`. Copy its conventions exactly:
outline colour and width, eye construction, cheek blush, the darker shade crescent on the lower right of
the body and the light highlight stroke on the upper left. Every character must look like it belongs in
the same set as this kiwi.

## Hard rules (the app relies on these)
1. `<svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 128 128">` — no width/height attributes.
2. Transparent background. No full-canvas background shape. The app puts each character on a
   coloured circle.
3. The picture is shown CROPPED TO A CIRCLE (centre 64,64, radius 64). Keep everything important
   inside radius 58. Nothing may extend past radius 62 except a tiny tip.
4. Allowed elements only: svg, g, path, circle, ellipse, rect, polygon, polyline, line, defs,
   linearGradient, radialGradient, stop, clipPath. NOT allowed: text, image, use, script, style,
   foreignObject, filter, mask, animate, any href/xlink:href, any on* attribute, CSS classes.
5. ≤ 6 KB per file (aim for 2–4 KB). Round coordinates to integers or 1 decimal place.
6. If you use gradient/clipPath ids, prefix them with the character id (e.g. `id="golden-moa-glow"`).

## Style (match the kiwi)
- Chibi proportions: big head (or head-and-body blob for round birds), small limbs, friendly expression.
  Full body or head-and-shoulders, whatever reads best in a circle. Facing the viewer or three-quarter view.
- Outline: `stroke="#1f2937"`, `stroke-width="3"` on main shapes (2–2.5 for small details),
  `stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"`.
- Eyes: filled `#1f2937` circles r≈6.5–7.5, a white highlight r≈2.6–2.8 up-right and a tiny white dot
  r≈1.2 low-left. Species with famous eye colours (ruru = yellow, hoiho = yellow band) may add a
  coloured iris ring around the dark pupil. Goggles/helmets may cover eyes only if the face still reads.
- Cheeks: `#f9a8b8` ellipses rx 6 ry 4, opacity .6.
- Shading: flat fills; one darker tone of the body colour as a crescent on the lower right; one lighter
  highlight stroke on the upper left. Gradients only for special effects (glow, lava, galaxy, gold).
- Colours: true to the species but bright and friendly. Avoid muddy colours.
- Costumes and props must be recognisable at 32 px. Big simple shapes beat tiny detail.

## Content rules
- Kid-friendly. No realistic weapons (no guns, no blades). A knight can have a shield and a wooden
  practice sword; a ninja can have a mask and headband, no blades; a pirate can have a hat, eyepatch, map
  or telescope.
- No text, numbers, letters, flags, logos or real brands (a rugby jersey is plain black, no fern logo).
- Respect te ao Māori: no tā moko, no hei-tiki or pounamu carvings, no taniwha, no sacred objects.
  Use macrons in names (the app shows the names; you only make the art).
- Accurate species features (see your list). Look closely at what makes each animal recognisable.
