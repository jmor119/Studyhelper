## 2. Senior qualifications: what replaces NCEA, and what it means for this student

Source: Tāhūrangi "Further details confirmed for new senior secondary qualifications replacing NCEA" (16 May 2026, archived), plus the Ministry media info sheet (16 May 2026, PDF). **[OFFICIAL-FINAL]**

**Structure**

- **Foundational Award**: literacy and numeracy, benchmarked at Year 11. Introduced **2028**, when **NCEA Level 1 is removed**.
- **NZCE (New Zealand Certificate of Education)**: Year 12, from **2029**. Replaces NCEA Level 2.
- **NZACE (New Zealand Advanced Certificate of Education)**: Year 13, from **2030**. Replaces NCEA Level 3.

**Requirements**

- **To be awarded NZCE or NZACE:** pass at least **3 subjects** at that year level or above, **and** hold the Foundational Award. The certificate shows the number of subjects passed (e.g. "NZCE 4").
- **Grading:** a six-point scale, **A+, A, B, C, D, E**. A **C or above** is a pass.
- **Assessment:** every subject mixes coursework with **at least one exam**, usually 3–4 assessments per subject per year. Students are assessed on the whole subject, and assessment is nationally consistent.
- **Compulsory Year 11 subjects from 2028:** **English (or Te Reo Rangatira), Mathematics (or Pāngarau), Science (or Pūtaiao).**
- **Years 12–13:** no compulsory subjects, but schools must enrol students in at least 5.
- **Endorsements:** two new endorsements, needing at least 5 subjects passed with a set number of high grades (example given: three A grades for "Distinction"). Names and criteria are not finalised.
- **Foundational Award options:**
  - It is typically sat in Year 11, but can be sat at any time.
  - It can also be gained by passing English or Mathematics in Year 12 or 13.
  - **Transitional arrangement:** for two years (the first two cohorts taking NZCE/NZACE), the **current NCEA literacy and numeracy co-requisite** can be used to gain the Foundational Award. The certificate will show which route was used.
- **Still to be decided:** reassessment rules, grade aggregation, detailed transitional rules, and **NZQA's review of University Entrance**.

**What this means for this student (Year 9 in 2026)** [OFFICIAL-FINAL facts; implications INFERRED]

- **First cohort for everything.** The student is in the first cohort for the new Year 11 curriculum (2028), the Foundational Award with no NCEA Level 1 (2028), NZCE (2029) and NZACE (2030). The Ministry said this explicitly about the 2026 Year 9 cohort in October 2025.
- **Year 11 core subjects.** Year 11 English, Maths and Science will be compulsory. Phase 5 Science for Year 11 is a single "Science" subject, splitting into Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Earth & Space Science and Agricultural & Horticultural Science in Years 12–13 (Tāhūrangi "NZC – Science Phase 5 (Years 11–13)", proposed). Strong Years 9–10 maths and science foundations therefore matter directly.
- **Literacy and numeracy in Years 9–10.**
  - The current co-requisite assessments (Reading, Writing, Numeracy) run twice a year, around May and September.
  - Most students first attempt them in **Year 10**: in 2025, 75% of enrolled Year 10 students sat at least one (NZQA Insights paper, June 2026).
  - Schools say students can sit them from Year 9 **[SECONDARY]**.
  - Because this student's cohort is covered by the transitional rule, **passing the current co-requisite (likely in Year 10, 2027) should count toward the Foundational Award**. The app should include numeracy co-requisite practice for Year 10 **[INFERRED: confirm the operational rules once NZQA publishes them]**.
- **Exam preparation.** Every subject will include at least one exam, so exam-style practice (timed, whole-subject) is worth building in from Years 9–10 **[INFERRED]**.

- **Status: [OFFICIAL-FINAL].** The page states this content has been part of the official policy statement for maths since 1 January 2026.

### 3.1 Structure

- **6 strands:** Number, Algebra, Measurement, Geometry, Statistics, Probability.
- **Inside each strand:**
  - **Elements**: sub-topics, e.g. "Number structures and operations", "Financial mathematics".
  - Each element has two columns: **Knowledge** (facts, concepts, principles and theories to teach) and **Practices** (skills, strategies and applications to teach).
  - Each column is split into **During Year 9** and **During Year 10**. Some cells span both years.
- **Not used any more:** "Understand / Know / Do" and "progress outcomes" from earlier drafts. Years 9–10 is a year-by-year teaching sequence.
- **Purpose ("big ideas").** Maths and statistics are presented as connecting quantity, space, time, data and uncertainty through abstraction, visualisation and symbols; as building logical reasoning and critical thinking; and as telling what is probable from what is possible.
- **Mathematical and statistical processes** underpin all teaching: investigating, representing and connecting situations; generalising, explaining and justifying findings.
- **Years 9–10 emphasis** (condensed from the official introduction):
  - proportional reasoning applied to quantities, measurements and shapes, including right-angled triangles
  - generalising with tables, equations and graphs
  - area, perimeter and volume for circles and prisms
  - data visualisations to explain patterns, trends and variation
  - chance
- **Changes from the draft:**
  - Algorithmic Thinking was removed; it now sits in Technology.
  - Statistics and probability were restructured; the statistical enquiry cycle is no longer the organising structure.
  - Statistics and probability content was reduced after feedback. (Maths infosheet, 20 Oct 2025; "What you told us – Maths Years 9–10", Oct 2025.)
- **No trigonometry.** Trigonometry (sin/cos/tan) does not appear in the Phase 4 text; similarity and Pythagoras do. **[INFERRED from the text: it is absent]**

#### Strand: PROBABILITY

**Element: Experimental and theoretical probability** (all cells span both years)

- **Knowledge:**
  - Some situations can only be explored by experiment (e.g. tossing an irregular 3D object).
  - Repeated sets of trials vary.
  - Law of Large Numbers.
  - Lists, tables, two-way tables and tree diagrams for finding all outcomes.
  - Joint events can be dependent or independent.
  - You can't simply add the probabilities of joint events (double counting).
  - Mutually exclusive events.
  - Experimental probability = relative frequency.
- **Practices:**
  - Run chance experiments, including digital simulations with many trials.
  - List the sample space.
  - Compare experimental results (at least 30 trials) with theoretical probability and explain the difference.
  - Run at least 100 trials to demonstrate the Law of Large Numbers.
  - Display the distribution of outcomes.
  - Calculate probability estimates.

### 3.4 Assessment expectations (maths) [OFFICIAL-FINAL]

- Teachers report progress against the year-level teaching sequence using five common progress descriptors: **Emerging, Developing, Consolidating, Proficient, Exceeding**.
  - Consolidating, Proficient and Exceeding count as "on track".
  - Teachers judge each strand, then make an overall judgement.
- The mandatory twice-yearly maths tools (SMART, PATs, or e-asTTle in 2026 only) apply to **Years 3–8**, not Year 9.
- There is no national Year 9–10 maths exam.
- The app could mirror the five descriptors for self-assessment. **[INFERRED]**

## 4. Science: topic map

### 4.1 What officially applies in Year 9 (2026): 2007 NZC Science, Levels 4–5 [OFFICIAL-FINAL]

**Source:** "The Science 2007 learning area" on Tāhūrangi, https://newzealandcurriculum.tahurangi.education.govt.nz/the-new-zealand-curriculum---science/5637209897.p
- Read live on 25 Oct 2026. The science landing page says Years 0–10 can use it until the end of 2026.

**Structure:** **Nature of Science** is the core strand, required learning to Year 10. Its sub-strands are Understanding about science, Investigating in science, Communicating in science, and Participating and contributing. Four context strands sit alongside it:
- **Living World**: life processes, ecology, evolution.
- **Planet Earth and Beyond**: Earth systems, interacting systems, astronomical systems.
- **Physical World**: physical inquiry and physics concepts, using physics.
- **Material World**: properties and changes of matter, structure of matter, chemistry and society.

Over Years 1–10, programmes are expected to cover all four context strands.

**Which level is Year 9?** The 2007 levels overlap across year groups. Schools commonly treat Year 9 as Level 4 moving into Level 5; for example, Burnside's 2026 Year 9 maths booklet says its course is based on Level 4 and extends to Levels 5–6. **[INFERRED / school practice]** I did not re-verify the official 2007 level-to-year diagram.

### 4.2 The refreshed Science Years 0–10 (final Aug 2026; required for Years 9–10 from 2027): structure [OFFICIAL-PROPOSED / OFFICIAL-DRAFT]

**Sources:**
- Science Phases 1–4 page (archived 15 Aug 2026): purpose statement and glossary only. The curriculum itself is in the PDF, which I did not obtain.
- "What you told us – Science Years 0–10" (Aug 2026).
- "Science Learning Area Years 0–10" draft infosheet (28 Oct 2025).
- Consultation Feedback Analysis (Aug 2026).

**Purpose (condensed, final).** Science is an evidence-based discipline that builds knowledge through observation, prediction, testing and analysis. It develops curiosity and critical thinking, and informed decisions on scientific issues. It prepares students for the Years 11–13 science subjects.

**Strands and elements** (from the draft infosheet; year spans as drafted, **final spans not confirmed**):

- **Physical Science**
  - Materials (Years 1–10): properties; states of matter; density; thermal conductivity; mixtures, solutions and separation; solubility and concentration.
  - Chemical Reactions (Years 9–10): signs of reaction; acids, bases, metals and carbonates; pH and neutralisation; combustion; displacement; reaction rates (temperature, catalysts).
  - Matter Interactions and Energy (Years 3–10): conduction, convection and radiation; energy transfer and efficiency; electricity (static, current, voltage, resistance, series and parallel); waves (sound and light: frequency, amplitude, reflection, refraction, EM spectrum).
  - Motion and Forces (Years 2–9 in the draft): forces, friction, Newton's laws, contact and non-contact forces, pressure, buoyancy and drag, simple machines, motion graphs, force diagrams.
  - Earth Systems (Years 4–10): water cycle; atmosphere; rocks, minerals, fossils, soil; Earth's spheres; carbon cycle and human impact.
  - Earth in Space (Years 3–10): Earth–Sun, Moon phases, Matariki; Solar System; universe structure, Big Bang, light years and AU.
- **Biological Science**
  - Organism Diversity (Years 1–10): classification; life cycles; cells; inheritance and DNA; variation; natural selection.
  - Body Systems (Years 1–10): organ systems; respiration, photosynthesis, diffusion; homeostasis, hormones, nervous system; reproduction and puberty.
  - Ecosystems (Years 1–10): food webs; ecosystem dynamics; nutrient cycles; human impact; ngā tohu o te taiao; sustainability.

**Nature of Science** is no longer a stand-alone strand; it is embedded in "scientific practices" linked to the knowledge. Notable scientists, including Māori and NZ scientists, appear in the teaching sequence.

**What changed in the final version (Aug 2026)** [OFFICIAL-FINAL summary of changes]:
- **Conceptual focus statements** were added to connect knowledge and practices across years.
- A practice framework organised around **investigation, evaluation, communication and application** was made explicit (stated for Years 0–8).
- Notes now mark what is *not* expected at each level.
- An indicative materials and equipment list was added.
- Content was moved **into Phase 4 (Years 9–10)**: advanced cell organelles, DNA and genetics; atomic theory, chemical bonding and **balanced chemical equations**; acceleration and **Newton's laws**.
- The report says some too-advanced concepts were simplified or moved to later phases, including Year 11, but does not list which Phase 4 items moved.

**At risk of having changed in the final version** [OFFICIAL-DRAFT + feedback; outcome unknown]. The August 2026 feedback analysis records many submitters asking for these draft Phase 4 items to be removed or deferred:
- homeostasis and hormonal regulation
- active transport
- advanced genetics and protein expression
- disease and immunity mechanisms
- reaction rates
- displacement reactions
- detailed atomic and bonding concepts
- the Big Bang, cosmic microwave background, red shift and expanding universe
- "oxidising agents"
- extensive wave physics

**Treat these Year 10 topics as provisional until checked against the final PDF.**

### 4.3 Year 9 science topic map: refreshed curriculum (draft-aligned) [OFFICIAL-DRAFT]

**Sources:**
- Ministry Year 9 Science Learning Kit, https://newzealandcurriculum.tahurangi.education.govt.nz/year-9-science-learning-kit/5637316326.p. Files: annual plan (11 Feb 2026); Unit 9.1 Motion & Forces unit plan (17 Feb 2026) and lessons; 9.2 Matter & Chemical Reactions lessons (24 Apr 2026). The kit page says it is aligned to the draft curriculum.
- education.govt.nz Parent Portal "Science in Year 9" (published 15 Oct 2025; now marked as under review because science has been fully updated).

**Use this for app content in 2026–27 with a "draft-aligned" flag; re-verify against the final PDF.**

**Ministry exemplar Year 9 annual plan (sequence):**
- **Term 1:** Motion & Forces.
- **Term 2:** Matter (elements, molecules, compounds; periodic table), then Chemical Reactions.
- **Term 3:** Earth Systems with Ecosystems, then Organism Diversity.
- **Term 4:** Organism Diversity, then Body Systems.

**Physical Science**

- **Motion and Forces.** This is the only element where draft knowledge and practice statements were available word-for-word (in the 9.1 unit plan); condensed here.
  - *Knowledge:*
    - Newton's first law: net force; balanced forces mean rest or constant velocity; unbalanced forces change motion; inertia depends on mass.
    - Newton's second law: F = ma, force in newtons. No breakdown into base units is required.
    - Newton's third law: action–reaction pairs.
    - Friction, including air and water resistance.
    - Equal and opposite forces on a rigid body cancel; free-body diagrams give the net force.
    - Velocity; acceleration as rate of change of velocity.
    - Distance–time and velocity–time graphs.
    - Upthrust decides floating or sinking.
    - Isaac Newton named as the scientist.
  - *Practices:*
    - Measure and compare forces.
    - Draw free-body diagrams and identify unbalanced forces.
    - Calculate F, m or a, and net force.
    - Predict and measure the effect of resistance forces in air, water and on surfaces.
    - Average vs instantaneous velocity from graphs or tables.
    - Independent, dependent and control variables.
  - *Kit lesson sequence (23 lessons + 4 practicals):*
    - Distance–time graphs; speed = d/t and unit conversion; average and instantaneous speed.
    - Velocity and acceleration (a = Δv/t); speed–time graphs.
    - Contact and non-contact forces; Newton's first law and force diagrams; F = ma; inertia; net force; friction.
    - Mass vs weight (gravity on other planets); drag and terminal velocity; projectiles (simulation).
    - Newton's third law; locomotion.
    - Practicals: rolling objects; friction simulation; parachutes; fan carts.
  - *From the parent guide:* pressure in fluids (atmospheric pressure falls with altitude; liquid pressure rises with depth).
  - In the kit, fluids and pressure are deferred to the Earth Systems unit.
- **Matter.** Kit 9.2 lessons:
  - particle model and changes of state
  - atoms and elements
  - molecular substances; molecular formulas (counting atoms)
  - metals vs non-metals
  - salt (ionic) lattices
  - pure substances vs mixtures
  - periodic table: organisation and history
  - Group 1 and 2 metals; Group 17 halogens; Group 18 noble gases

  The parent guide adds: element names ↔ symbols; position on the table predicts metal or non-metal; ratios of atoms in formulas (H₂O).
- **Chemical Reactions.** Kit 9.2 lessons continue:
  - chemical reactions and **word equations**
  - signs of a chemical reaction
  - exothermic and endothermic reactions
  - combustion; acid–base reactions
  - reactions in living things
  - industrial chemistry
  - Practicals: modelling matter; properties of substances; reactivity of metals; observing chemical change; acids and bases.

  The parent guide also lists oxidation–reduction examples such as rusting.
- **Earth Systems.** From the parent guide:
  - cycling of matter and energy between atmosphere, lithosphere/geosphere, biosphere and hydrosphere
  - water and carbon cycles, and their effect on climate and clean water
  - why the tropics are hotter (latitude)
  - ocean convection currents and climate

**Biological Science** (parent guide; kit units not yet published for these)

- **Organism Diversity:**
  - DNA in the nucleus; genes code for proteins
  - genes + environment → phenotype
  - many kinds of protein (enzymes, hormones, antibodies, hair)
  - single-gene vs many-gene traits
  - continuous vs discontinuous variation
- **Body Systems:**
  - mammalian circulatory system (heart, vessels, blood) and what blood transports
  - diffusion, osmosis and active transport across membranes
  - surface-area-to-volume ratio
  - links between respiratory, digestive and other life processes
  - plant xylem and phloem
- **Ecosystems:**
  - levels of organisation
  - biotic and abiotic factors and their effects on populations
  - mātauranga Māori observation of environmental patterns (planting, harvest, fishing)
  - carbon, nitrogen and water cycling; storage in forests and oceans

**Note on electricity and Earth in Space:** neither appears in the Year 9 parent guide or the kit plan. Their Phase 4 year placement is **not confirmed**. The draft infosheet lists electricity under Matter Interactions & Energy (Years 3–10) without giving a year.
