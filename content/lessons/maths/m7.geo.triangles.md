Triangles are strong shapes, so builders use them in bridges, roofs and power pylons. In this lesson you will name triangles by their angles and by their sides, and use angle facts to find missing angles.

## Naming triangles by their angles

Every triangle has three **interior angles**: the angles inside it. Look at the biggest angle to name the triangle.

- An **acute triangle** has three acute angles. They are all less than 90°.
- A **right-angled triangle** has one right angle: exactly 90°. A small square marks the right angle.
- An **obtuse triangle** has one obtuse angle, between 90° and 180°.

## Naming triangles by their sides

Matching tick marks on sides show which sides are the same length.

- An **equilateral** triangle has three equal sides.
- An **isosceles** triangle has at least two equal sides.
- A **scalene** triangle has no equal sides.

## The angle sum of a triangle

### Example

Two angles of a triangle are 48° and 75°. Add them: $48° + 75° = 123°$. The third angle is $180° - 123° = 57°$.

> **Tip:** Check your answer by adding all three angles. You should get exactly 180°: $48° + 75° + 57° = 180°$ ✓

## Isosceles and equilateral triangles

In an isosceles triangle, the two **base angles** are equal. They are the angles opposite the equal sides.

```figure
{"svg":"<svg xmlns=\"http://www.w3.org/2000/svg\" viewBox=\"0 0 236 244\" width=\"236\" height=\"244\" font-family=\"Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif\" font-size=\"14\"><rect width=\"100%\" height=\"100%\" fill=\"#ffffff\"/><polygon points=\"56,204 179.75,204 117.87,34\" fill=\"#dbeafe\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"2\"/><path d=\"M 80 204 A 24 24 0 0 0 64.21 181.45\" fill=\"none\" stroke=\"#2563eb\" stroke-width=\"1.8\"/><text x=\"92.08\" y=\"178.73\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"14\" fill=\"#2563eb\">70°</text><path d=\"M 171.54 181.45 A 24 24 0 0 0 155.75 204\" fill=\"none\" stroke=\"#2563eb\" stroke-width=\"1.8\"/><text x=\"143.67\" y=\"178.73\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"14\" fill=\"#2563eb\">70°</text><path d=\"M 109.67 56.55 A 24 24 0 0 0 126.08 56.55\" fill=\"none\" stroke=\"#2563eb\" stroke-width=\"1.8\"/><text x=\"117.87\" y=\"72\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"14\" fill=\"#2563eb\">40°</text><line x1=\"154.45\" y1=\"116.95\" x2=\"143.17\" y2=\"121.05\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"1.5\"/><line x1=\"81.3\" y1=\"116.95\" x2=\"92.58\" y2=\"121.05\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"1.5\"/><text x=\"227.75\" y=\"234\" text-anchor=\"end\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"11\" fill=\"#6b7280\">Not to scale</text></svg>","alt":"An isosceles triangle with two equal sloping sides. The two base angles are both 70° and the top angle is 40°."}
```

### Example

The angle between the equal sides is 40°. The two base angles share what is left: $180° - 40° = 140°$. So each base angle is $140° \div 2 = 70°$.

In an **equilateral** triangle all three angles are equal, so each one is $180° \div 3 = 60°$.

> **Watch out:** Angles on a straight line also add up to 180°. If a side of a triangle is extended, use this to find the angle inside the triangle first.

## Angle equations

Sometimes the angles are written using a letter, like $x$. Add the angles, make the total equal 180, and solve the equation.

```figure
{"svg":"<svg xmlns=\"http://www.w3.org/2000/svg\" viewBox=\"0 0 372 221\" width=\"372\" height=\"221\" font-family=\"Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif\" font-size=\"14\"><rect width=\"100%\" height=\"100%\" fill=\"#ffffff\"/><polygon points=\"56,180.97 316,180.97 231.15,34\" fill=\"#dbeafe\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"2\"/><path d=\"M 80 180.97 A 24 24 0 0 0 74.39 165.54\" fill=\"none\" stroke=\"#2563eb\" stroke-width=\"1.8\"/><text x=\"93.56\" y=\"167.3\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"14\" fill=\"#2563eb\">x°</text><path d=\"M 304 160.18 A 24 24 0 0 0 292 180.97\" fill=\"none\" stroke=\"#2563eb\" stroke-width=\"1.8\"/><text x=\"260.57\" y=\"148.96\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"14\" fill=\"#2563eb\">(x + 20)°</text><path d=\"M 212.76 49.43 A 24 24 0 0 0 243.15 54.78\" fill=\"none\" stroke=\"#2563eb\" stroke-width=\"1.8\"/><text x=\"224.22\" y=\"73.28\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"14\" fill=\"#2563eb\">2x°</text><text x=\"364\" y=\"210.97\" text-anchor=\"end\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"11\" fill=\"#6b7280\">Not to scale</text></svg>","alt":"A triangle with angles labelled x degrees, x plus 20 degrees, and 2x degrees."}
```

### Example

$x + (x + 20) + 2x = 180$

Collect like terms: $4x + 20 = 180$

Subtract 20 from both sides: $4x = 160$

Divide both sides by 4: $x = 40$

So the angles are 40°, 60° and 80°. Check: $40° + 60° + 80° = 180°$ ✓

> **Putting it together:**
> - Name a triangle by its angles: acute, right-angled or obtuse.
> - Name a triangle by its sides: equilateral, isosceles or scalene.
> - The angles in a triangle add up to 180°.
> - The base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal. Every angle in an equilateral triangle is 60°.
