Money is maths you use every day: at the dairy, the school canteen and the supermarket. In this lesson you will work out totals and change, round cash payments the New Zealand way, round to the nearest cent, and find sale prices.

## Totals and change

The **total** is all the prices added together. **Change** is the money you get back when you pay with more than the total.

Money has two decimal places. \$4.50 means 4 dollars and 50 cents.

### Example

Mere buys a pie for \$4.80 and a juice for \$2.70.

Total: $4.80 + 2.70 = 7.50$, so the total is \$7.50.

She pays with a \$10 note. Change: $10.00 - 7.50 = 2.50$, so she gets \$2.50.

Shopkeepers often find change by **counting on** from the total to the note.

## Cash rounding

New Zealand has no 1c, 2c or 5c coins. The smallest coin is 10c. So when you pay with **cash**, the total is rounded to the nearest 10 cents. This is called **cash rounding**.

- 1, 2, 3 or 4 cents: round **down**. \$4.33 becomes \$4.30.
- 6, 7, 8 or 9 cents: round **up**. \$4.37 becomes \$4.40.
- 5 cents: the shop decides which way to round. Many shops round down.

Only the **total** is rounded, not each item. If you pay by card (EFTPOS), you pay the exact amount.

### Example

Sione's shopping comes to \$18.64, and he pays with a \$20 note. \$18.64 ends in 4 cents, so it rounds down to \$18.60. His change is $20.00 - 18.60 = 1.40$, so \$1.40.

## Rounding to the nearest cent

Sometimes a money calculation gives more than two decimal places. Money answers are rounded to two decimal places, which is the nearest cent.

### Example

Three friends share \$10 equally. $10 \div 3 = 3.333\ldots$

Look at the third decimal place. It is 3, which is less than 5, so round down. Each person gets \$3.33.

The price of one item is called the **unit price**. A pack of 6 muesli bars costs \$5, so one bar costs $5 \div 6 = 0.833\ldots$, which is about \$0.83.

> **Tip:** To find the better buy, compare the price of **one** item in each pack, not the price of the whole pack. The lower unit price is better value.

## Discounts and sale prices

A **discount** is an amount taken off the price. The **sale price** is the price after the discount.

Discounts are often a **percentage**. Use 10% to find the others.

| To find | Do this |
|---|---|
| 10% | Divide by 10 |
| 20%, 30%, 40% | Find 10%, then multiply by 2, 3 or 4 |
| 5% | Find half of 10% |
| 25% | Divide by 4 |
| 50% | Divide by 2 |

### Example

A \$60 hoodie has 30% off. 10% of \$60 is \$6, so 30% is $3 \times 6 = 18$, a discount of \$18.

Sale price: $60 - 18 = 42$, so \$42.

Another way: with 30% off you pay 70%. 70% of \$60 is $7 \times 6 = 42$, so \$42.

## Putting it together

A **budget** is a plan for how you will spend your money. Before you buy, check that the total fits your budget.

### Example

Hana has \$50. She buys a \$36 board game with 25% off, and 3 drinks at \$2.40 each. She pays cash.

- Board game: 25% of \$36 is $36 \div 4 = 9$, so it costs $36 - 9 = 27$, which is \$27.
- Drinks: $3 \times 2.40 = 7.20$, so \$7.20.
- Total: $27.00 + 7.20 = 34.20$. It already ends in 0 cents, so cash rounding doesn't change it.
- Change: $50.00 - 34.20 = 15.80$, so \$15.80.

> **Tip:** Line up the decimal points when you add or subtract money, and write answers with two decimal places: \$3.50, not \$3.5.

> **Tip:** Check your change: the total plus the change should equal the money you paid.
