When you collect data, you end up with lots of numbers. The mean and the range are two quick ways to sum them up: where the middle is, and how spread out they are.

## Data varies

**Data** is information we collect, such as heights, scores or times. Each number in a set of data is a **value**.

When you ask a statistical question, like "How long does it take students in our class to get to school?", the answers **vary**. People live in different places and travel in different ways. That is normal! Statistics helps us make sense of answers that vary.

### Example

Hana asked 6 friends how many minutes it takes them to get to school: 5, 12, 8, 20, 10 and 5. The answers are not all the same, but we can still describe them. We can find their **centre** (a typical value) and their **spread** (how far apart they are).

**Range = highest value − lowest value**

## The mean

The **mean** is one kind of **average**. It tells you about the **centre** of the data.

**Mean = total of all the values ÷ number of values**

### Example

Ruby’s netball team scored 4, 7, 9, 12 and 8 goals in 5 games.

Total: $4 + 7 + 9 + 12 + 8 = 40$

Mean: $40 \div 5 = 8$ goals per game

## Decimals and measured data

The mean doesn’t have to be a whole number, and it doesn’t have to be one of the values. This often happens with measured data, like times, lengths and masses.

### Example

Three students ran 100 m in 14.2 s, 15.1 s and 13.9 s.

Total: $14.2 + 15.1 + 13.9 = 43.2$ s

Mean: $43.2 \div 3 = 14.4$ s

Range: $15.1 - 13.9 = 1.2$ s

> **Tip:** Always count how many values there are. A value of 0 still counts!

```check

  "solution": "$2.5 + 3.1 + 3.7 = 9.3$ kg, and $9.3 \\div 3 = 3.1$. The mean mass is **3.1 kg**."
}
```

## Comparing groups

The mean and the range help you compare groups.

### Example

| | Scores | Mean | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aroha | 7, 8, 8, 9 | 8 | 2 |
| Losa | 3, 12, 6, 11 | 8 | 9 |

Both have a mean of 8, so they score the same on average. Aroha’s range is much smaller, so her scores are more **consistent**: they stay close together.

**Working backwards:** if you know the mean, you can find the total. If 4 scores have a mean of 8, they must add up to $4 \times 8 = 32$.
