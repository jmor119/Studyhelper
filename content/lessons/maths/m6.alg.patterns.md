Patterns are all around us: chairs around tables, fence posts, and money saved each week. In this lesson you will continue growing patterns, describe them with a rule in words, use tables, and predict terms far along a pattern.

## Growing patterns

A **pattern** is a list of numbers or shapes that follows a rule. Each number in the pattern is a **term**. Where a term sits in the list is its **position**.

$$3, \; 7, \; 11, \; 15, \; \ldots$$

- The term in position 1 is 3. The term in position 2 is 7.
- Each term is 4 more than the one before. The amount it changes by each time is the **constant**. Here the constant is 4.

### Example

What is the next term in $3, 7, 11, 15, \ldots$?

The pattern adds 4 each time, so the next term is $15 + 4 = 19$.

Some patterns go down. $50, 45, 40, 35, \ldots$ takes away 5 each time, so the next term is 30.

## Rules in words

A **rule** tells you how to make the pattern. A rule in words has two parts:

1. the number to **start** with (the term in position 1), and
2. how much to **add** or **subtract** each time (the constant).

### Example

The pattern $2, 8, 14, 20, \ldots$ has the rule: **start with 2 and add 6 each time.**

> **Watch out:** Don't swap the numbers. "Start with 6 and add 2 each time" makes $6, 8, 10, 12, \ldots$, which is a different pattern.

## Tables of values

A **table of values** puts each position next to its term. It helps you see the rule.

| Position | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Term | 5 | 8 | 11 | 14 | 17 |

The terms go up by 3 each time, and position 1 has 5. Rule: start with 5 and add 3 each time.

### Example

Make a table for the rule **start with 10 and add 5 each time.**

| Position | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Term | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |

Position 1 is the starting number. Then add 5 for each new position.

## Predicting and checking

A **conjecture** is a prediction you think is true, but still need to check.

To find a term far along a pattern, you don't need to write every term. Count the jumps.

### Example

Find the 20th term of $3, 7, 11, 15, \ldots$

- From the 1st term to the 20th term there are **19 jumps** of 4.
- $3 + 19 \times 4 = 3 + 76 = 79$.

> **Watch out:** Count the jumps, not the terms. The 20th term is 19 jumps after the 1st term.

### Checking a conjecture

Mere says, "The 10th term must be double the 5th term." Let's check with $3, 7, 11, 15, \ldots$

- 5th term: $3 + 4 \times 4 = 19$.
- 10th term: $3 + 9 \times 4 = 39$.
- Double 19 is 38, not 39. Mere's conjecture is **false** for this pattern.

## Putting it together

- Find the **constant** first: how much is added or subtracted each time?
- A rule in words says what to **start** with and what to **add** or **subtract** each time.
- A **table of values** helps you see the rule and keep track.
- For far terms, count the jumps: the 20th term is the 1st term plus 19 jumps.
- Test every conjecture. Check it on terms you already know.

### Example

At a whānau dinner, 1 table seats 6 people, 2 tables pushed together seat 10, and 3 tables seat 14. How many people can sit at 8 tables?

Rule: start with 6 and add 4 each time. From 1 table to 8 tables there are 7 jumps: $6 + 7 \times 4 = 34$ people.
