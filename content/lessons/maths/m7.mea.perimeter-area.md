Perimeter and area are everywhere: fencing a garden, carpeting a room or painting a wall. Formulas make them quick to work out. In this lesson you will use formulas for squares, rectangles and triangles, and for shapes made from them.

## Perimeter and area

The **perimeter** is the total distance around the outside of a shape. It is a length, so it is measured in units like cm or m.

The **area** is the amount of flat space inside a shape. Area is two-dimensional, so it is measured in **square units**, such as square centimetres (cm²) or square metres (m²). 1 cm² is the area of a square that is 1 cm long and 1 cm wide.

## Formulas for squares and rectangles

A **formula** is a rule written with letters. Here $l$ is the length and $w$ is the width.

| Shape | Perimeter | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Square | $P = 4l$ | $A = l^2 = l \times l$ |
| Rectangle | $P = 2(l + w)$ | $A = lw = l \times w$ |

### Example 1

A rectangular garden is 8 m long and 5 m wide.

- Perimeter: $P = 2(8 + 5) = 2 \times 13 = 26$ m
- Area: $A = 8 \times 5 = 40$ m²

### Example 2

A square tile has sides of 9 cm. Its perimeter is $P = 4 \times 9 = 36$ cm. Its area is $A = 9^2 = 9 \times 9 = 81$ cm².

## Working backwards

Sometimes you know the perimeter or the area, and you need a side. Use the formula backwards, with **inverse operations**.

- **A square from its perimeter:** divide by 4. If $P = 28$ cm, each side is $28 \div 4 = 7$ cm.
- **A rectangle from its perimeter:** halve the perimeter, then take away the length. If $P = 30$ m and $l = 9$ m, then $l + w = 15$, so $w = 15 - 9 = 6$ m.
- **A rectangle from its area:** divide by the length. If $A = 48$ cm² and $l = 8$ cm, then $w = 48 \div 8 = 6$ cm.
- **A square from its area:** find the number that multiplies by itself to give the area. If $A = 64$ m², then $l = \sqrt{64} = 8$ m.

> **Tip:** Check by putting your answer back into the formula. For the second example: $2(9 + 6) = 30$ ✓

## Area of a triangle

A triangle is half of a rectangle with the same base and height.


So the area of a triangle is $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$. Here $b$ is the **base** and $h$ is the **perpendicular height**. The perpendicular height meets the base at a right angle. It is not the slanted side.

### Example

A triangle has a base of 10 cm and a perpendicular height of 7 cm.

$A = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 7 = \frac{1}{2} \times 70 = 35$ cm²

### Working backwards

A triangle has an area of 24 m² and a height of 6 m. Base × height is double the area, so $b \times 6 = 48$. That means $b = 48 \div 6 = 8$ m.

## Composite shapes

A **composite shape** is made from simpler shapes. To find its area, **decompose** it: split it into rectangles and triangles, find each area, then add them.

### Example

Split the shape along the dashed line.

- Rectangle A: $6 \times 8 = 48$ m²
- Rectangle B: $4 \times 3 = 12$ m²
- Total area: $48 + 12 = 60$ m²

The perimeter only uses the outside edges: $10 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 8 = 36$ m. Don't add the dashed line!

If a side is not labelled, look at the sides opposite it. Here the two top lengths, 6 m and 4 m, add up to the bottom length, 10 m.

> **Putting it together:**
> - Perimeter: add up the outside edges. Units like cm or m.
> - Area: use a formula, or split the shape up. Units like cm² or m².
> - For a triangle, always use the perpendicular height.
