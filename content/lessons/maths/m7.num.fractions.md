Fractions show parts of a whole, like $\frac{3}{4}$ of a pizza or $\frac{2}{5}$ of the class. In this lesson you will simplify fractions, change between mixed numbers and improper fractions, compare fractions, and add and subtract them.

## Equivalent fractions and simplest form

In $\frac{3}{4}$, the **numerator** (3) tells you how many parts you have. The **denominator** (4) tells you how many equal parts make the whole.

**Equivalent fractions** have the same value. They sit at the same point on the number line. To make one, multiply the top and the bottom by the same number:

$\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 2} = \frac{6}{8}$

A fraction is in **simplest form** when the numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1. To simplify, divide the top and bottom by their highest common factor (HCF).

### Example
Simplify $\frac{18}{24}$. The HCF of 18 and 24 is 6, so $\frac{18}{24} = \frac{18 \div 6}{24 \div 6} = \frac{3}{4}$.

## Mixed numbers and improper fractions

An **improper fraction** has a numerator bigger than its denominator, like $\frac{11}{4}$. A **mixed number** has a whole number and a fraction, like $2\frac{3}{4}$. They can show the same amount.

**Improper to mixed:** divide. $11 \div 4 = 2$ remainder 3, so $\frac{11}{4} = 2\frac{3}{4}$.

**Mixed to improper:** each whole is $\frac{4}{4}$, so 2 wholes are $\frac{8}{4}$. Add the $\frac{3}{4}$ to get $\frac{11}{4}$. A quick way is whole × denominator + numerator: $2 \times 4 + 3 = 11$.

### Example
$3\frac{2}{5} = \frac{3 \times 5 + 2}{5} = \frac{17}{5}$

## Comparing and ordering fractions

When fractions have the same denominator, compare the numerators: $\frac{5}{8}$ is bigger than $\frac{3}{8}$.

When the denominators are different, change the fractions so they have a **common denominator**. Use a number that all the denominators go into.

### Example
Which is bigger, $\frac{2}{3}$ or $\frac{3}{5}$? Both 3 and 5 go into 15.

$\frac{2}{3} = \frac{10}{15}$ and $\frac{3}{5} = \frac{9}{15}$, so $\frac{2}{3}$ is bigger.

> **Watch out:** A bigger denominator means *smaller* pieces. $\frac{1}{8}$ is smaller than $\frac{1}{6}$, because the whole is cut into more pieces.

## Adding and subtracting fractions

You can only add or subtract fractions when they have the same denominator. Change them to a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators. The denominator stays the same.

### Example
$\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$

**Mixed numbers:** change them to improper fractions first.

$2\frac{1}{2} - 1\frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{2} - \frac{5}{3} = \frac{15}{6} - \frac{10}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$

Always give your answer in simplest form. Change an improper answer to a mixed number if the question asks.

> **Watch out:** Never add the denominators. $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}$ is not $\frac{2}{7}$.

## Fractions as relationships, and putting it together

A fraction can describe how two amounts are related. If there are 3 boys for every 4 girls, each small group has 7 students. So $\frac{3}{7}$ of the class are boys and $\frac{4}{7}$ are girls.

> **Watch out:** $\frac{3}{4}$ compares boys with girls, not boys with the whole class.

**Checklist:**
- Simplify every answer: check whether the top and bottom still share a factor.
- To compare, add or subtract, find a common denominator first.
- Change mixed numbers to improper fractions before you subtract.
- Sense-check: adding should make a bigger number, and subtracting a smaller one.
