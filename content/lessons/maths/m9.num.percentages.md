Sales, test marks, pay rises and population changes are all described with percentages. This lesson covers finding a percentage of an amount, writing one number as a percentage of another, percentage increases and decreases, and working backwards to find the original amount.

## Percentages and fractions of an amount

**Percent** means "out of 100", so 35% of something is $\frac{35}{100}$ of it.

**Without a calculator**, build percentages from easy ones:

- 50% is a half, 25% is a quarter, 10% is a tenth (÷ 10), 1% is a hundredth (÷ 100).
- 5% is half of 10%, and 20% is double 10%.

### Example

35% of 80: 10% is 8, so 30% is 24. 5% is 4. So 35% is $24 + 4 = 28$.

**With a calculator**, change the percentage to a decimal and multiply: 35% of 80 $= 0.35 \times 80 = 28$.

**A fraction of an amount:** divide by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator. $\frac{3}{8}$ of 96: $96 \div 8 = 12$, then $12 \times 3 = 36$.

## One number as a percentage of another

To write one number as a percentage of another:

$$\text{percentage} = \frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \times 100$$

### Example

Aroha scored 18 out of 24 in a test. $\frac{18}{24} \times 100 = 75$, so she scored 75%.

As a fraction in simplest form, her score was $\frac{18}{24} = \frac{3}{4}$.

> **Watch out:** Both amounts must be in the **same units**. To write 45 cents as a percentage of \$3, change \$3 to 300 cents first: $\frac{45}{300} \times 100 = 15\%$.

Percentages make it easy to compare. 34 out of 40 (85%) is a better score than 42 out of 50 (84%), even though 42 is more marks.

## Increase and decrease with multipliers

The original amount is 100%. A percentage change gives a new percentage of the original, and you can write that as a decimal **multiplier**:

| Change | New amount is | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| **increase** by 20% | 120% | × 1.2 |
| **decrease** by 20% | 80% | × 0.8 |
| increase by 7% | 107% | × 1.07 |
| decrease by 35% | 65% | × 0.65 |

### Example

A school roll of 840 increases by 5%. The new roll is $840 \times 1.05 = 882$.

**Percentage change:** find the change, then divide by the **original** amount.

A rugby ball's price goes from \$40 to \$46. The change is \$6, and $\frac{6}{40} \times 100 = 15\%$, so it is a 15% increase.

> **Tip:** A 10% increase followed by a 10% decrease does **not** take you back to the start: $1.1 \times 0.9 = 0.99$, so you end up 1% lower.

## Finding the whole (the original amount)

Sometimes you know a **part** and the percentage it represents, and you need the whole.

### Example

20% of a number is 30. What is the number?

- 20% is 30, so 10% is 15.
- 100% is $15 \times 10 = 150$.

Or divide by the multiplier: $30 \div 0.2 = 150$.

**After an increase or decrease**, the new amount is a percentage of the **original amount**.

A bike costs \$96 after a 20% increase. The new price is 120% of the original, so divide by 1.2: $96 \div 1.2 = 80$. The original price was \$80.

> **Watch out:** Don't take 20% off \$96. That gives \$76.80, which is wrong, because the 20% was a percentage of the *original* price, not the new one. Check: 20% of \$80 is \$16, and $80 + 16 = 96$ ✓.

## Exam tips

- **Write the multiplier down** before you calculate: +15% is × 1.15, −15% is × 0.85.
- **Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100.** Always divide by the original amount.
- **Same units** before writing one amount as a percentage of another.
- **Working backwards?** Divide by the multiplier. Never take the percentage off the new amount.
- **Check your answer.** Apply the percentage to your answer and see if you get back to the number in the question.
