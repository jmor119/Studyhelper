Will it rain at the beach on Saturday? Will your team win? **Probability** is the maths of chance. In this lesson you will describe how likely things are, list all the outcomes, and work out probabilities as fractions.

## How likely is it?

**Chance** is how likely something is to happen. We use these words:

- **Impossible:** it cannot happen. Rolling a 7 on a normal die.
- **Unlikely:** it probably won't happen. Rolling a 6.
- **Even chance:** it is just as likely to happen as not. A coin landing on heads.
- **Likely:** it probably will happen. Rolling a number bigger than 1.
- **Certain:** it must happen. Rolling a number less than 7.

A **probability** is a number from 0 to 1. We can show it on a probability line.

## Outcomes and sample spaces

These words help us talk about chance:

- A **trial** is one go, like one roll of a die.
- An **outcome** is one possible result, like rolling a 4.
- The **sample space** is the list of every possible outcome. For a die it is 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.
- An **event** is a group of outcomes, like "rolling an even number": 2, 4, 6.
- An **experiment** is doing trials to see what happens.

### Example

A spinner has 4 equal sections coloured red, blue, red and green. The sample space is **red, blue, green**. List each outcome once, even if a colour appears twice on the spinner.

> **Tip:** Work **systematically**. Go in order (1, 2, 3, … or around the spinner) so you don't miss an outcome.

## Working out a probability

When every outcome is **equally likely**, you can work out a probability with a fraction:

$$P(\text{event}) = \frac{\text{number of ways it can happen}}{\text{total number of outcomes}}$$

### Example

A bag has 3 red and 5 blue counters. Aroha takes one without looking.

- There are $3 + 5 = 8$ counters altogether. That goes on the bottom.
- 3 of them are red. That goes on the top.
- $P(\text{red}) = \frac{3}{8}$

### Example

Rolling a die, $P(\text{even number}) = \frac{3}{6}$, because 2, 4 and 6 are 3 of the 6 outcomes. In simplest form that is $\frac{1}{2}$.

> **Watch out:** The bottom number is the **total**. For the bag above, $P(\text{red})$ is not $\frac{3}{5}$.

## Spinners and adding to 1

If a spinner has equal sections, count the sections. If the sections are different sizes, use the fraction of the spinner each colour covers.


- Red covers half the spinner, so $P(\text{red}) = \frac{1}{2}$.
- Blue and yellow each cover a quarter: $P(\text{blue}) = \frac{1}{4}$ and $P(\text{yellow}) = \frac{1}{4}$.

The spinner must land on some colour, so **the probabilities of all the outcomes add up to 1**:

$$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{4} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{4}{4} = 1$$

### Example

A spinner has only red, blue and green parts. $P(\text{red}) = \frac{1}{2}$ and $P(\text{blue}) = \frac{1}{8}$. What is $P(\text{green})$?

$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{8} = \frac{4}{8} + \frac{1}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$, so $P(\text{green}) = \frac{8}{8} - \frac{5}{8} = \frac{3}{8}$.

## Putting it together

- Use the chance words: impossible, unlikely, even chance, likely, certain.
- Probabilities go from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).
- List the sample space carefully, with each outcome once.
- $P(\text{event})$ = number of ways it can happen ÷ total number of outcomes.
- The probabilities of all the outcomes add up to 1. Use this to check your answers.

> **Watch out:** More sections does not always mean more likely. On a spinner, two small sections can cover less than one big section. Look at how much of the spinner each colour covers.
