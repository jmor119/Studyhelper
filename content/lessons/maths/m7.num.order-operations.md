When a calculation has more than one operation, everyone has to work it out in the same order. If we didn't agree, two people could get two different answers to the same question. This lesson teaches you that order, called **GEMA**, and three properties that make calculating easier.

## Why we need an order

An **expression** is a set of numbers joined by operations, like $3 + 4 \times 2$. To **evaluate** an expression means to work out its value.

Ana and Tama both evaluate $3 + 4 \times 2$.

- Ana adds first: $3 + 4 = 7$, then $7 \times 2 = 14$.
- Tama multiplies first: $4 \times 2 = 8$, then $3 + 8 = 11$.

They can't both be right. So everyone uses the same rules, called the **order of operations**. The rules say multiply before you add, so Tama is right. The answer is 11.

Think of $3 + 4 \times 2$ as "3, plus four lots of 2". The four lots of 2 belong together, so you work them out first.

> **Tip:** Reading from left to right is not always the right order. Look at the whole expression before you start.

| Step | Stands for | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **G** | Grouping | Work out anything in **brackets** first. |
| **E** | Exponents | Next, work out squares and cubes, like $4^2$ and $2^3$. |
| **M** | Multiplicative | Then do × and ÷, from left to right. |
| **A** | Additive | Last, do + and −, from left to right. |

× and ÷ are partners. Neither one beats the other, so do them in the order they appear, starting from the left. + and − are partners in the same way.

### Example

$20 - 12 \div 4 + 3 \times 2$

$= 20 - 3 + 6$ (M: the ÷ and the × first)

$= 17 + 6$ (A: left to right)

$= 23$

> **Watch out:** $18 \div 3 \times 2$ is $6 \times 2 = 12$. It is not $18 \div 6$, because × and ÷ go from left to right.

## Brackets and exponents

**Brackets** group numbers together. Whatever is inside brackets is worked out first, even if it is an addition.

An **exponent** is the small raised number. It tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself.

- $4^2 = 4 \times 4 = 16$. We say "4 **squared**".
- $2^3 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$. We say "2 **cubed**".

### Example

$2 \times (3 + 4)^2 - 10$

$= 2 \times 7^2 - 10$ (G: brackets)

$= 2 \times 49 - 10$ (E: exponent)

$= 98 - 10$ (M: multiply)

$= 88$ (A: subtract)

> **Watch out:** $(2 + 3)^2 = 5^2 = 25$, but $2^2 + 3^2 = 4 + 9 = 13$. The brackets change the answer.

## Properties that make maths easier

Some rules let you change a calculation to make it easier, without changing the answer.

- The **commutative** property: you can swap the order when you add or multiply. $7 + 5 = 5 + 7$ and $4 \times 9 = 9 \times 4$.
- The **associative** property: when you only add, or only multiply, you can group the numbers any way you like. $4 \times 17 \times 25 = (4 \times 25) \times 17 = 100 \times 17 = 1700$.
- The **distributive** property: when you multiply a bracket, multiply every part inside it. $6 \times (20 + 3) = 6 \times 20 + 6 \times 3 = 120 + 18 = 138$.

The distributive property is great for mental maths. To work out $7 \times 98$, think of 98 as $100 - 2$:

$7 \times 98 = 7 \times 100 - 7 \times 2 = 700 - 14 = 686$

> **Watch out:** Swapping doesn't work for − or ÷. $10 - 4 = 6$, but $4 - 10$ is not 6.

## Putting it together

- Write a new line for each step, and copy down the parts you haven't used yet.
- Say GEMA in your head: brackets, then exponents, then × and ÷, then + and −.
- × and ÷ are partners, and + and − are partners. Partners go from left to right.
- Square brackets [ ] work just like round ones. With brackets inside brackets, start with the innermost ones.
- Use the properties to make a calculation easier, like pairing $25 \times 4 = 100$.

### Example

$[2 + (5 - 3)^2] \times 3$

$= [2 + 2^2] \times 3$ (the inner brackets first)

$= [2 + 4] \times 3$ (the exponent, still inside the brackets)

$= 6 \times 3$ (finish the brackets)

$= 18$
