Ratios compare amounts: mixing paint, sharing money or scaling a recipe. Rates compare quantities measured in different units, like price per kilogram or kilometres per hour. Both come up all the time in everyday maths.

## What a ratio means

A **ratio** compares two or more quantities of the same kind. If a bag has 2 red counters for every 3 blue counters, the ratio of red to blue is $2 : 3$.

- **Order matters.** Blue to red is $3 : 2$.
- A ratio is usually **part : part**. To get a **part : whole** fraction, add the parts to find the whole. There are $2 + 3 = 5$ parts, so $\frac{2}{5}$ of the counters are red and $\frac{3}{5}$ are blue.

> **Watch out:** The ratio $2 : 3$ does not mean $\frac{2}{3}$ of the counters are red. Red is 2 parts out of 5, so $\frac{2}{5}$ of them are red.

## Simplifying and equivalent ratios

**Equivalent ratios** are made by multiplying or dividing every part by the same number: $2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 10 : 15$.

A ratio is in **simplest form** when the parts are whole numbers with no common factor except 1. Divide by the highest common factor:

$18 : 24 = 3 : 4$ (dividing both by 6)

**Units must match.** For 40 cm : 2 m, change 2 m to 200 cm. Then $40 : 200 = 1 : 5$.

**Decimals or fractions:** multiply to get whole numbers first.

- $1.5 : 2.5 = 15 : 25 = 3 : 5$
- $\frac{1}{2} : \frac{2}{3}$: multiply both by 6 to get $3 : 4$

**Missing values:** $3 : 5 = 12 : \square$. The 3 has been multiplied by 4, so multiply the 5 by 4 too. The missing value is 20.

## Sharing in a ratio

To **share** an amount in a ratio:

1. Add the parts to find the total number of shares.
2. Divide the amount by that total to find one share.
3. Multiply by each part.

### Example

Mere and Kai share \$60 in the ratio $2 : 3$.

1. Total shares: $2 + 3 = 5$
2. One share: $60 \div 5 = 12$
3. Mere gets $2 \times 12 = 24$, so \$24. Kai gets $3 \times 12 = 36$, so \$36.

Check: $24 + 36 = 60$ ✓

**If you know one part, not the total:** the ratio of girls to boys in a kapa haka group is $4 : 3$, and there are 24 girls. So 4 parts = 24, one part = 6, and there are $3 \times 6 = 18$ boys.

## Rates

A **rate** compares two quantities measured in **different units**, such as \$3.50 per kilogram, 80 km/h or 12 litres per minute. **Per** means "for every" or "for each".

A **unit rate** is the amount for one unit. To find it, divide:

- \$12.60 for 3 kg of apples: $12.60 \div 3 = 4.20$, so the unit rate is \$4.20 per kg.

**Using a rate:** a car uses 7 L of petrol per 100 km. For 350 km it uses $3.5 \times 7 = 24.5$ L.

**Equivalent rates** describe the same rate in different units: 90 km/h $= 90 \div 60 = 1.5$ km per minute.

**Best buys:** compare unit prices. A 2 L bottle of milk for \$4.40 costs \$2.20 per litre. A 3 L bottle for \$6.30 costs \$2.10 per litre. The 3 L bottle is better value.

## Exam tips

- **Read the order.** "Boys to girls" and "girls to boys" are different ratios.
- **Part : part or part : whole?** If a question asks for a fraction of the total, add the parts first.
- **Same units** before simplifying a ratio.
- **Sharing:** add the parts, find one share, then multiply. Check that your shares add up to the total.
- **Rates:** always write the units, e.g. \$4.20 per kg or 1.5 km/min. To compare value, compare the price for the same amount.
