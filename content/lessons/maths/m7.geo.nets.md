A **3D shape** is a solid shape with length, width and height. Boxes, tents and dice are all 3D shapes. In this lesson you will describe prisms and pyramids, count their parts, and draw and check nets.

## Faces, edges and vertices

- A **face** is a flat surface of a 3D shape.
- An **edge** is a line where two faces meet.
- A **vertex** is a corner, where edges meet. The plural of vertex is **vertices**.

### Example

A cube has 6 faces, and they are all squares.

It has 4 edges around the top, 4 around the bottom and 4 going up the sides: 12 edges.

It has 4 corners on the top and 4 on the bottom: 8 vertices.

## Prisms

A **prism** has two identical ends joined by rectangles. If you slice straight through a prism, parallel to its ends, the slice is the same shape as the ends, all the way along. This slice is called the **cross-section**. A prism is named after the shape of its ends.

```figure
{"svg":"<svg xmlns=\"http://www.w3.org/2000/svg\" viewBox=\"0 0 320 215\" width=\"320\" height=\"215\" font-family=\"Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif\" font-size=\"14\"><rect width=\"100%\" height=\"100%\" fill=\"#ffffff\"/><polygon points=\"230.6,54.66 69.65,65.61 179.75,104.74\" fill=\"#dbeafe\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"2\"/><polygon points=\"69.65,175.61 179.75,214.74 179.75,104.74 69.65,65.61\" fill=\"#bfdbfe\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"2\"/><polygon points=\"179.75,214.74 230.6,164.66 230.6,54.66 179.75,104.74\" fill=\"#bfdbfe\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"2\"/><line x1=\"230.6\" y1=\"164.66\" x2=\"69.65\" y2=\"175.61\" stroke=\"#6b7280\" stroke-width=\"1.4\" stroke-dasharray=\"5 4\"/></svg>","alt":"A triangular prism standing on one of its triangle ends, with hidden edges dashed.","caption":"A triangular prism: two triangle ends joined by three rectangles."}
```

For a prism whose ends have $n$ sides:

- faces: $n + 2$ (the 2 ends, plus $n$ rectangles)
- edges: $3n$ ($n$ around each end, plus $n$ joining the ends)
- vertices: $2n$ ($n$ on each end)

### Example

A triangular prism has ends with 3 sides. It has $3 + 2 = 5$ faces, $3 \times 3 = 9$ edges and $2 \times 3 = 6$ vertices. A tent and a wedge of cheese both have this shape.

## Pyramids

A **pyramid** has one base, and triangle faces that meet at a single point at the top, called the apex. A pyramid is named after the shape of its base.

An arrow points to the apex at the top."}

For a pyramid whose base has $n$ sides:

- faces: $n + 1$ (the base, plus $n$ triangles)
- edges: $2n$ ($n$ around the base, plus $n$ going up to the apex)
- vertices: $n + 1$ ($n$ on the base, plus the apex)

### Example

A square-based pyramid has $4 + 1 = 5$ faces, $2 \times 4 = 8$ edges and $4 + 1 = 5$ vertices.

> **Watch out:** A prism has two ends that match. A pyramid has only one base.

## Nets

A **net** is a flat shape that folds up to make a 3D shape. A net has one shape for every face.

- A prism net has a strip of rectangles, with one end shape on each side of the strip.
- A pyramid net has the base in the middle, with a triangle on each of its sides.

### Example: the net of a triangular prism

1. Draw three rectangles side by side in a strip. They will wrap around to make the sides.
2. Draw one triangle on the top edge of a rectangle.
3. Draw the other triangle on the bottom edge of a rectangle.
4. Check: 5 shapes for 5 faces ✓

> **Watch out:** If both end shapes are on the same side of the strip, they fold onto the same end. The other end is left open.

## Cube nets: will it fold?

There are 11 different nets of a cube. To check whether six squares will fold into a cube:

- Imagine one square as the base, and fold the others up around it.
- Two squares must never land on the same face.
- A net with a 2 by 2 block of squares never works.

### Example

In this net, $A$, $C$ and $F$ are in a straight line, so $A$ and $F$ are opposite each other. In the row $B$, $C$, $D$, $E$, the faces $B$ and $D$ are opposite, and so are $C$ and $E$.

> **Tip:** On a dice, opposite faces always add up to 7: 1 and 6, 2 and 5, 3 and 4.
