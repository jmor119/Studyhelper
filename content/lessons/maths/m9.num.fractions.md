Fractions are everywhere in maths: in ratios, probability, algebra and measurement. This lesson covers simplifying fractions, mixed numbers, and all four operations, including *why* dividing by a fraction works the way it does.

## Equivalent fractions and simplest form

In $\frac{3}{4}$, the **numerator** (3) is how many parts you have and the **denominator** (4) is how many equal parts the whole is split into.

**Equivalent** fractions have the same value. Make one by multiplying the top and the bottom by the same number. That is the same as multiplying by a form of 1, such as $\frac{2}{2}$ or $\frac{5}{5}$:

$\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3 \times 5}{4 \times 5} = \frac{15}{20}$

A fraction is in **simplest form** when the numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1. To simplify, divide the top and bottom by their highest common factor (HCF).

### Example

Simplify $\frac{18}{24}$. The HCF of 18 and 24 is 6, so $\frac{18}{24} = \frac{18 \div 6}{24 \div 6} = \frac{3}{4}$.

> **Tip:** You can simplify in steps (÷2, then ÷3). Just keep going until nothing else divides into both.

## Improper fractions and mixed numbers

An **improper fraction** has a numerator bigger than (or equal to) its denominator, like $\frac{11}{4}$. A **mixed number** is a whole number with a proper fraction, like $2\frac{3}{4}$.

**Improper → mixed:** divide. $11 \div 4 = 2$ remainder 3, so $\frac{11}{4} = 2\frac{3}{4}$.

**Mixed → improper:** multiply the whole number by the denominator, then add the numerator.

$3\frac{2}{5} = \frac{3 \times 5 + 2}{5} = \frac{17}{5}$

Each whole is $\frac{5}{5}$, so 3 wholes are $\frac{15}{5}$, plus $\frac{2}{5}$ more.

> **Tip:** Change mixed numbers to improper fractions before you multiply or divide. In algebra, improper fractions are usually easier to work with.

## Adding and subtracting fractions

You can only add or subtract fractions with the **same denominator**. Use the lowest common multiple (LCM) of the denominators, convert both fractions, then add or subtract the numerators. The denominator stays the same.

### Example

$\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{8}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$

**Mixed numbers:** change them to improper fractions first.

$2\frac{1}{2} - 1\frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{2} - \frac{5}{3} = \frac{15}{6} - \frac{10}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$

**Negative answers:** $\frac{1}{4} - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{12} - \frac{8}{12} = -\frac{5}{12}$. Write the negative sign in front of the fraction (or on the numerator). The denominator should always be a positive whole number.

> **Watch out:** Never add the denominators. $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3}$ is $\frac{5}{6}$, not $\frac{2}{5}$.

## Multiplying fractions

Multiply the numerators, and multiply the denominators. No common denominator is needed.

$\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{9} = \frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 9} = \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}$

You can **cancel** common factors before multiplying to keep the numbers small: the 3 cancels with the 9, and the 2 cancels with the 4.

"**Of**" means multiply: $\frac{2}{5}$ of 30 $= \frac{2}{5} \times 30 = 12$. A quick way is to divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator: $30 \div 5 = 6$, then $6 \times 2 = 12$.

**Mixed numbers:** change them to improper fractions first.

$1\frac{1}{2} \times 2\frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{8}{3} = \frac{24}{6} = 4$

> **Watch out:** Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes a number *smaller*. That's why half of 30 is less than 30.

## Dividing by a fraction

**Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.** Here is why.

$3 \div \frac{1}{4}$ asks "how many quarters fit into 3?" Each whole holds 4 quarters, so 3 wholes hold $3 \times 4 = 12$. So $3 \div \frac{1}{4} = 3 \times 4 = 12$.

The same works for any fraction. $\frac{2}{3}$ and $\frac{3}{2}$ are reciprocals ($\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = 1$). Dividing by $\frac{2}{3}$ and multiplying by $\frac{3}{2}$ both undo multiplying by $\frac{2}{3}$, so they always give the same answer.

**Method:** keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, and flip the second fraction.

### Example

$\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{3}{8} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{8}{3} = \frac{24}{12} = 2$

Check: two lots of $\frac{3}{8}$ make $\frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}$ ✓

## Exam tips

- **Simplest form, every time.** Check whether the numerator and denominator still share a factor.
- **Write negative fractions properly:** $-\frac{3}{4}$ (or $\frac{-3}{4}$), never $\frac{3}{-4}$. The denominator should be a positive whole number.
- **Mixed numbers:** change them to improper fractions for ×, ÷ and harder + and − questions. Change back at the end if the question asks for a mixed number.
- **Common denominators are only for + and −.** Don't waste time finding one for × or ÷.
- **Sense-check.** Dividing by a fraction less than 1 should make the answer bigger; multiplying by one should make it smaller.
