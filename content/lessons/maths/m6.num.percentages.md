Percentages are everywhere: a 25% off sale, a phone battery at 60%, or a test score of 85%. In this lesson you will switch between fractions, decimals and percentages, find percentages of numbers, and use percentages to compare.

## What is a percentage?

**Per cent** means "out of 100". The sign % means per cent. A **percentage** is a number out of 100.

Hundredths, fractions out of 100 and percentages are all the same thing:

$$0.35 = \frac{35}{100} = 35\%$$

### Example
$0.31 = \frac{31}{100} = 31\%$, and $8\% = \frac{8}{100} = 0.08$.

> **Tip:** To change a decimal to a percentage, multiply by 100. To change a percentage to a decimal, divide by 100.

## Equivalents to know

These fractions, decimals and percentages are **equivalent**: they have the same value. Learn them off by heart.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| $\frac{1}{2}$ | $0.5$ | 50% |
| $\frac{1}{4}$ | $0.25$ | 25% |
| $\frac{3}{4}$ | $0.75$ | 75% |
| $\frac{1}{5}$ | $0.2$ | 20% |
| $\frac{2}{5}$ | $0.4$ | 40% |
| $\frac{3}{5}$ | $0.6$ | 60% |
| $\frac{4}{5}$ | $0.8$ | 80% |
| $\frac{1}{10}$ | $0.1$ | 10% |

For any fraction, make an equivalent fraction out of 100: $\frac{3}{5} = \frac{3 \times 20}{5 \times 20} = \frac{60}{100} = 60\%$.

> **Watch out:** $0.7$ is 7 tenths, which is 70 hundredths. So $0.7 = 70\%$, not 7%.

## Finding a percentage of a number

Use what you know about fractions.

| Percentage | Same as | How to find it |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | $\frac{1}{2}$ | halve |
| 25% | $\frac{1}{4}$ | halve, then halve again |
| 75% | $\frac{3}{4}$ | find 25%, then × 3 |
| 10% | $\frac{1}{10}$ | ÷ 10 |
| 20% | $\frac{1}{5}$ | find 10%, then double |
| 1% | $\frac{1}{100}$ | ÷ 100 |

### Example
75% of 48. 25% is $48 \div 4 = 12$, so 75% is $3 \times 12 = 36$.

### Example
20% of 35. 20% is one fifth: $35 \div 5 = 7$.

### Example
1% of 600 $= 600 \div 100 = 6$.

## Finding the whole, and comparing

**Finding the whole.** The **whole** is 100%. If you know a percentage of it, work out how many lots make 100%.

75% is 3 lots of 25%, so 25% is $24 \div 3 = 8$. 100% is 4 lots of 25%: $4 \times 8 = 32$.

**Comparing.** Percentages help you **compare** amounts that come from different totals. Each is changed into a **proportion** out of 100.

### Example
Aroha got 17 out of 20 in a test. Tama got 21 out of 25 in a different test. Who did better?

- Aroha: $\frac{17}{20} = \frac{85}{100} = 85\%$
- Tama: $\frac{21}{25} = \frac{84}{100} = 84\%$

Tama got more marks, but Aroha did slightly better.

## Putting it together

- **Per cent means out of 100.** $0.31 = \frac{31}{100} = 31\%$.
- **Learn the equivalents** for halves, quarters, fifths and tenths.
- **Percentages of numbers:** 50% halve, 25% halve twice, 10% divide by 10, 1% divide by 100. Build 20% and 75% from these.
- **Finding the whole:** find one "lot" first, then build up to 100%.
- **Comparing:** turn each score into a percentage before you decide which is bigger.
- **Check:** 50% of a number is half of it, so 75% must be more than half and 25% less than half.
