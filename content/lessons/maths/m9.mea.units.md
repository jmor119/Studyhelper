Every measurement needs a number **and** a unit. "The track is 5" means nothing, but "the track is 5 km" does. In this lesson you'll convert between metric units, use the big and small metric prefixes, work with units such as cm² and km/h, handle time (which counts in 60s, not 10s), and decide how precise an answer should be.

## Metric prefixes and sensible units

The **metric** system builds every unit from a base unit (metre, gram, litre, second, watt) plus a **prefix** that multiplies it by a power of ten.

| Prefix | Symbol | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| tera | T | × 1 000 000 000 000 ($10^{12}$) | terawatt (TW) |
| giga | G | × 1 000 000 000 ($10^{9}$) | gigawatt (GW) |
| mega | M | × 1 000 000 ($10^{6}$) | megawatt (MW) |
| kilo | k | × 1000 ($10^{3}$) | kilometre (km) |
| centi | c | ÷ 100 ($10^{-2}$) | centimetre (cm) |
| milli | m | ÷ 1000 ($10^{-3}$) | millimetre (mm) |
| micro | μ | ÷ 1 000 000 ($10^{-6}$) | micrometre (μm) |
| nano | n | ÷ 1 000 000 000 ($10^{-9}$) | nanosecond (ns) |

Choose a unit that gives a sensible-sized number. Measure the length of a desk in centimetres, the distance from Hamilton to Tauranga in kilometres, the mass of a kiwifruit in grams, and the mass of a truck in tonnes. A hydro power station produces megawatts, and a bacterium is a few micrometres long.

> **Watch out:** Capitals matter. **M** (mega) is a million times bigger, but **m** (milli) is a thousand times smaller.

## Converting units

To **convert**, find how many small units make one big unit, then decide whether to multiply or divide.

- Changing to a **smaller** unit → you need **more** of them → **multiply**.
- Changing to a **bigger** unit → you need **fewer** of them → **divide**.

| Length | Mass | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 km = 1000 m | 1 t = 1000 kg | 1 L = 1000 mL |
| 1 m = 100 cm | 1 kg = 1000 g | 1 mL = 1 cm³ |
| 1 cm = 10 mm | 1 g = 1000 mg | |

### Example

Convert 2.45 km to metres. Metres are smaller, so multiply: $2.45 \times 1000 = 2450$ m.

Convert 350 mL to litres. Litres are bigger, so divide: $350 \div 1000 = 0.35$ L.

> **Tip:** Multiplying or dividing by 10, 100 or 1000 just moves the digits 1, 2 or 3 places. Check your answer makes sense: 2.45 km must be *more* than 1000 m.

## Derived units: cm² and km/h

A **derived unit** is built from other units.

**Area units.** 1 m = 100 cm, so a square 1 m by 1 m is 100 cm by 100 cm:

$1 \text{ m}^2 = 100 \times 100 = 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2$, not 100 cm².

Likewise 1 cm² = 10 × 10 = 100 mm², and 1 km² = 1000 × 1000 = 1 000 000 m².

**Speed units.** 1 km/h means 1000 m in 3600 seconds. That gives a handy shortcut:

- m/s → km/h: **× 3.6**
- km/h → m/s: **÷ 3.6**

### Example

A car travels at 72 km/h. In m/s: $72 \div 3.6 = 20$ m/s. (Check: 20 m every second is $20 \times 3600 = 72\,000$ m, or 72 km, every hour.)

## Time counts in 60s

Time is not decimal: 60 seconds make a minute and 60 minutes make an hour. This is **base 60**.

- 2.5 h = 2 h 30 min, because 0.5 of 60 minutes is 30 minutes.
- 1 h 15 min = 1.25 h, because 15 ÷ 60 = 0.25.
- To find a duration such as 9:47 am to 11:25 am, count up: 13 min to 10:00, 60 min to 11:00, then 25 min. Total: 98 minutes.

Race times often mix both systems. A swim time of 1:05.37 means 1 minute and 5.37 seconds, which is $60 + 5.37 = 65.37$ seconds. The part after the decimal point is hundredths of a second.

> **Watch out:** 1.5 hours is **not** 1 hour 50 minutes. It is 1 hour 30 minutes.

## Precision and accuracy

**Precision** is how finely something is measured: to the nearest metre, the nearest centimetre, or the nearest 0.01 s. **Accuracy** is how close a measurement is to the true value. A broken scale can give very precise readings that are not accurate at all.

The key rule: **an answer cannot be more precise than the least precise measurement used.**

### Example

Two planks are measured as 2.4 m (to the nearest 0.1 m) and 1.35 m (to the nearest 0.01 m). The calculator says the total is 3.75 m. The first plank is only known to 0.1 m, so give the total to 0.1 m: **3.8 m**.

The same goes for calculator answers. If a runner covers 125 m in 14 s, the calculator shows 8.928571429 m/s. Writing all those digits pretends you measured far more precisely than you did. About 8.9 m/s is sensible.

## Exam tips

- Write the conversion fact first (for example, 1 km = 1000 m), then decide: smaller unit → multiply, bigger unit → divide.
- Area units square the factor: 1 m² = 10 000 cm². (Volume units would cube it.)
- Speed: × 3.6 for m/s → km/h, and ÷ 3.6 for km/h → m/s.
- For time, never treat minutes as decimals. Convert with 60.
- Round your final answer to match the least precise measurement, and always include the unit.
