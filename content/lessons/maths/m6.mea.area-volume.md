How much carpet covers the classroom floor? How much sand fills the sandpit? The first question is about **area** and the second is about **volume**. In this lesson you will find both by multiplying.

## Area of a rectangle

**Area** is the amount of flat space a shape covers. We measure it in squares.

- A **square centimetre** (cm²) is a square 1 cm long and 1 cm wide. It is about the size of your fingernail.
- A **square metre** (m²) is a square 1 m long and 1 m wide.


This rectangle has 3 rows of 5 squares. Instead of counting every square, multiply: $3 \times 5 = 15$. The area is 15 cm².

**Area of a rectangle = length × width**

### Example

A vegetable garden is 6 m long and 4 m wide. Its area is $6 \times 4 = 24$ m².

> **Watch out:** Area is not the same as perimeter. Perimeter is the distance around the edge, in cm or m. Area is the space inside, in cm² or m².

## Area of a right-angled triangle

A **right-angled triangle** has one right angle (a square corner).

```figure

```

Look at the dashed rectangle. Its diagonal cuts it into two triangles that are exactly the same. So the triangle is **half** of the rectangle.

**Area of a right-angled triangle = length × width ÷ 2**

Use the two sides that meet at the right angle. We call them the **base** and the **height**.

### Example

The triangle has a base of 6 cm and a height of 4 cm.

- Rectangle: $6 \times 4 = 24$ cm².
- Triangle: $24 \div 2 = 12$ cm².

> **Watch out:** Don't use the long sloping side. It does not help you find the area.

## Volume of a rectangular prism

**Volume** is the amount of space a 3D object takes up.

- A **cubic centimetre** (cm³) is a cube with edges 1 cm long. It is about the size of a small dice.
- A **cubic metre** (m³) is a cube with edges 1 m long. Two or three students could squeeze inside one.

A **rectangular prism** is a box shape. All of its faces are rectangles.


This block is made of 1 cm cubes. The top layer has $4 \times 3 = 12$ cubes. There are 2 layers, so there are $12 \times 2 = 24$ cubes. The volume is 24 cm³.

**Volume of a rectangular prism = length × width × height**

A **cube** has all its edges the same length. A cube with 3 cm edges has a volume of $3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$ cm³.

## Shapes made of rectangles

Some shapes can be split into rectangles. Find the area of each rectangle, then add them.


### Example

- Rectangle A is 6 m by 3 m: $6 \times 3 = 18$ m².
- Rectangle B is 10 m by 5 m: $10 \times 5 = 50$ m².
- Total area: $18 + 50 = 68$ m².

How did we know B is 5 m high? The whole shape is 8 m high, and A takes up 3 m of that: $8 - 3 = 5$ m.

**Finding a missing side:** a rectangle has an area of 42 m² and a length of 7 m. What times 7 makes 42? $42 \div 7 = 6$, so the width is 6 m.

## Estimating and comparing

Benchmarks help you check that an answer makes sense.

| Benchmark | About |
|---|---|
| Your fingernail | 1 cm² |
| A sheet of A4 paper | 600 cm² |
| A classroom floor | 60 m² |
| A small dice | 1 cm³ |
| A school lunchbox | 1500 cm³ |

### Comparing boxes

Box A is 6 cm × 5 cm × 2 cm. Box B is 4 cm × 4 cm × 4 cm. Which holds more?

- A: $6 \times 5 \times 2 = 60$ cm³.
- B: $4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$ cm³.

Box B holds more, by 4 cm³, even though it is shorter.

> **Tip:** Always write the units. Lengths are in cm or m, areas in cm² or m², and volumes in cm³ or m³.
