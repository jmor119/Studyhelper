Wheels, plates, clock faces and the rings in a tree trunk are all circles. To talk and calculate about circles you need the right words for their parts, and you need to know how those parts are connected.

## What makes a circle

A **circle** is the set of **all the points that are the same distance from a fixed point**. The fixed point is the **centre**, and the fixed distance is the **radius**.

That is exactly how a compass draws a circle: the point stays on the centre, and the pencil stays the same distance away as it turns. You can do the same thing outside with a peg, a piece of string and a stick to mark out a circular garden.

Because every point on the circle is the same distance from the centre, **all radii of a circle are equal**. This simple fact solves many problems.

## The parts of a circle

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Centre** | The point in the middle, the same distance from every point on the circle. |
| **Radius** ($r$) | A straight line from the centre to the circle. (Plural: radii.) |
| **Diameter** ($d$) | A straight line from one side of the circle to the other, **through the centre**. |
| **Chord** | Any straight line joining two points on the circle. |
| **Arc** | Part of the curved edge of the circle. |
| **Circumference** | The whole curved edge: the distance all the way around. |

A diameter is made of two radii end to end, so:

$$d = 2r \qquad r = \frac{d}{2}$$

A diameter is a special chord: the one that goes through the centre. It is the **longest** chord you can draw in a circle. A diameter also cuts the circle into two **semicircles**.

## Using radius and diameter

### Example 1

Five coins, each with radius 1.2 cm, are laid in a straight row, touching. Each coin takes up its diameter, $2 \times 1.2 = 2.4$ cm, so the row is $5 \times 2.4 = 12$ cm long.

### Example 2

A circle fits exactly inside a square with sides of 18 cm. The circle touches two opposite sides, so its diameter is 18 cm and its radius is 9 cm.

### Example 3

O is the centre of a circle, and A and B are points on the circle. OA and OB are both radii, so they are equal. That makes triangle OAB **isosceles**, so its two base angles (at A and B) are equal. If angle AOB is 40°, each base angle is $(180° - 40°) \div 2 = 70°$.

## Exam tips

- Learn the six words: centre, radius, diameter, chord, arc, circumference. Questions often ask you to name a part on a diagram.
- Radius or diameter? Read carefully, and remember $d = 2r$.
- Every radius in the same circle is the same length. If two sides of a triangle are both radii, the triangle is isosceles.
- Two circles that touch on the outside have centres that are $r_1 + r_2$ apart.
