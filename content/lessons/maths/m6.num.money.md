Money maths helps you every time you buy lunch, save pocket money or look at a sale. This lesson covers dollars and cents, adding prices, working out change, finding 10%, 25% and 50% of a price, and checking whether you have enough money.

## Dollars and cents

There are 100 **cents** in 1 **dollar**. Money uses place value with two decimal places.

| tens of dollars | dollars | . | 10 cents | 1 cent |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 3 | 4 | . | 7 | 5 |

So \$34.75 is 34 dollars and 75 cents.

Cents always take **two** places after the point:
- \$4.05 is 4 dollars and 5 cents (405 cents).
- \$4.50 is 4 dollars and 50 cents (450 cents).

To change dollars to cents, multiply by 100: \$7.20 is 720 cents. To change cents to dollars, divide by 100: 365 cents is \$3.65.

New Zealand coins are 10c, 20c, 50c, \$1 and \$2. Notes are \$5, \$10, \$20, \$50 and \$100.

## Adding prices

The **total** is what everything costs added together. Add the dollars, add the cents, then regroup 100 cents as \$1.

### Example

A pie costs \$4.80, a juice costs \$2.50 and a muffin costs \$3.70.

- Dollars: $4 + 2 + 3 = 9$ dollars.
- Cents: $80 + 50 + 70 = 200$ cents, which is \$2.
- Total: $9 + 2 = 11$, so the total is **\$11.00**.

You can also use the column method. Line up the decimal points:

$$
\begin{array}{rr} & 4.80 \\ & 2.50 \\ + & 3.70 \\ \hline & 11.00 \end{array}
$$

> **Watch out:** \$2.50 + \$3.70 is not \$5.120. 120 cents is \$1.20, so the answer is \$6.20.

Change = money you hand over − total cost

### Example

A sandwich costs \$7.40. You pay with a \$20 note.

- \$7.40 up to \$8 is 60c.
- \$8 up to \$10 is \$2.
- \$10 up to \$20 is \$10.

Change: 60c + \$2 + \$10 = **\$12.60**. Check by subtracting: $20.00 - 7.40 = 12.60$ ✓

## Percentages of prices

A **percentage** is an amount out of 100. Some percentages are easy fractions:
- 50% is one half, so divide by 2.
- 25% is one quarter, so halve and then halve again.
- 10% is one tenth, so divide by 10.

**Examples:** 50% of \$280 is $280 \div 2 = 140$, so \$140. 25% of \$36: half is \$18, and half again is **\$9**. 10% of \$45 is $45 \div 10 = 4.5$, so **\$4.50**.

A **discount** is an amount taken off a price. A \$60 jacket has 25% off:
- Discount: 25% of \$60 is \$15.
- Sale price: \$60 − \$15 = **\$45**.

## Budgets and best buys

A **budget** is a plan for how much money you can spend. To check if you have enough, find the total and compare it with your money.

### Example

Mere has \$20. She wants felt pens for \$8.50, a pencil case for \$6.90 and a muffin for \$4.20.

- Estimate: about $9 + 7 + 4 = 20$ dollars. That is close, so work it out exactly.
- Dollars: $8 + 6 + 4 = 18$. Cents: $50 + 90 + 20 = 160$ cents, which is \$1.60. Total: **\$19.60**.
- \$19.60 is less than \$20, so Mere has enough, with **40c** left over.

**Best buy:** muesli bars cost \$6.00 for 4 or \$8.40 for 6. Find the price of one bar in each pack. $600 \div 4 = 150$ cents, so \$1.50 each. $840 \div 6 = 140$ cents, so \$1.40 each. The pack of 6 is better value.

> **Tips:** Always write money with a \$ sign and two decimal places (\$3.50, not \$3.5). When the numbers are close, an estimate isn't enough: work it out exactly.
