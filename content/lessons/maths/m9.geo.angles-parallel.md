Geometry questions often show a diagram with a few angles given and ask you to find another one, **giving reasons**. In this lesson you'll use the basic angle facts, then the three special angle pairs made when a line crosses parallel lines. You'll also learn to prove that two lines are parallel.

## Basic angle facts

| Fact | Short reason to write |
|---|---|
| Angles on a straight line add to 180°. | ∠s on a line |
| Angles around a point add to 360°. | ∠s at a point |
| **Vertically opposite** angles (opposite each other where two lines cross) are equal. | vert opp ∠s |
| The angles in a triangle add to 180°. | ∠ sum of triangle |
| The base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal. | base ∠s isos triangle |

Two angles that add to 180° are called **supplementary**.

### Example

Two angles on a straight line are 128° and $x$. So $x = 180° - 128° = 52°$ (∠s on a line).

## Parallel lines and a transversal

**Parallel** lines never meet. On diagrams they are marked with matching arrows. A **transversal** is a line that crosses them. It makes eight angles, and they come in special pairs:

| Pair | Where they are | Rule | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Corresponding** | Same position at each crossing | Equal | F |
| **Alternate** | Between the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal | Equal | Z |
| **Co-interior** | Between the parallel lines, on the same side of the transversal | Add to 180° | C (or U) |

These rules **only** work when the lines are parallel.

> **Tip:** Trace the letter with your finger. The angles in the corners of an F are equal, the angles in the corners of a Z are equal, and the two angles inside a C add to 180°.

## Finding angles with reasons

In exams, the reason is worth marks. For each step, write the angle, its size, and the rule you used.

### Example

AB is parallel to CD. A transversal crosses AB at P and CD at Q. The angle above AB, to the right of the transversal at P, is 72°. Find the angle below CD, to the left of the transversal at Q.

1. The angle above CD, to the right at Q, is in the same position as the 72° angle, so it is also **72°** (corresponding ∠s, AB ∥ CD).
2. The angle we want is vertically opposite that one, so it is **72°** (vert opp ∠s).

> **Watch out:** "Because they look the same" is never a reason. Diagrams are often **not to scale**. Use a rule.

## Proving lines are parallel

The rules also work in reverse. Two lines **are** parallel if a transversal makes:

- corresponding angles that are equal, **or**
- alternate angles that are equal, **or**
- co-interior angles that add to 180°.

If the angles don't fit the rule, the lines are **not** parallel.

### Example

A transversal crosses lines AB and CD. Two co-interior angles are 104° and 76°. Since $104° + 76° = 180°$, AB is parallel to CD (co-interior ∠s add to 180°).

If they had been 104° and 74°, they would add to 178°, so the lines would not be parallel. They would slowly get closer together.

## Exam tips

- Look for the parallel arrows first. No arrows means you can't use the F, Z or C rules unless you're told the lines are parallel.
- Give a reason for every angle you find, using the correct name: corresponding, alternate, co-interior, vertically opposite, angles on a line, angles at a point, angle sum of a triangle.
- With expressions such as $(3x + 10)°$, decide on the rule first, then write an equation and solve it.
- Check your answers: the angles on a line, and the angles in a triangle, should add to 180°.
