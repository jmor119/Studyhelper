Every digit in a number has a job, and its job depends on where it sits. Knowing this helps you read big numbers, compare them, round them and estimate answers.

## Place value up to millions

A **digit** is one of the symbols 0 to 9. **Place value** is the value a digit has because of its place in a number.

Our number system is **base 10**. Each place is worth 10 times as much as the place to its right. Ten ones make a ten, ten tens make a hundred, and so on. A **million** is a thousand thousands: 1,000,000.

### Example

In 3,482,615 the 4 is in the hundred thousands place, so it is worth 400,000. The 1 is in the tens place, so it is worth 10.

To read a big number, split it into groups of three digits from the right: **3** million, **482** thousand, **615**. We say "three million, four hundred and eighty-two thousand, six hundred and fifteen".

> **Watch out:** Use zeros to hold empty places. Four million, sixty thousand and five is written 4,060,005.

## Comparing and ordering

To compare two whole numbers:

1. **Count the digits.** The number with more digits is bigger. 102,345 has 6 digits and 98,765 has 5, so 102,345 is bigger.
2. **Same number of digits?** Compare the digits from the left until you find two that are different.

### Example

Compare 405,312 and 450,132. Both have 6 digits. The hundred thousands digits are both 4. The ten thousands digits are 0 and 5, and 5 is bigger. So 450,132 is the bigger number.

We write $405{,}312 < 450{,}132$. The symbol $<$ means "is less than" and $>$ means "is greater than". The pointy end always points to the smaller number.

> **Watch out:** Don't just look at the first digit. 98,765 starts with a 9, but it is smaller than 102,345.

## Rounding whole numbers

To **round** a number means to change it to a nearby number that is easier to work with.

1. Find the two numbers it sits between. For the nearest hundred, these are two hundreds, like 3,400 and 3,500.
2. Look at the digit just to the right of the place you are rounding to.
3. If that digit is **5 or more, round up**. If it is **4 or less, round down**.

### Examples

- 3,487 to the nearest hundred: it is between 3,400 and 3,500. The tens digit is 8, so round up to **3,500**.
- 6,250,000 to the nearest million: it is between 6,000,000 and 7,000,000. The hundred thousands digit is 2, so round down to **6,000,000**.
- 2,961,000 to the nearest hundred thousand: it is between 2,900,000 and 3,000,000. The ten thousands digit is 6, so round up to **3,000,000**.

## Tenths and hundredths

**To round to the nearest whole number,** look at the tenths digit. 14.67 has 6 tenths, so it rounds up to **15**.

**To round to the nearest tenth,** look at the hundredths digit. 14.67 is between 14.6 and 14.7. The hundredths digit is 7, so it rounds up to **14.7**.

> **Watch out:** Only look at one digit. 6.48 rounds to **6** as a whole number, not 7. Don't round to 6.5 first and then round again.

## Estimating

An **estimate** is a close answer that you work out quickly with rounded numbers. Estimates help you check that an exact answer makes sense.

### Examples

- $4{,}870 + 3{,}120$: round each number to the nearest thousand. $5{,}000 + 3{,}000 = 8{,}000$. The exact answer is 7,990, which is close.
- $48 \times 31$: round each number to the nearest ten. $50 \times 30 = 1{,}500$.

**Too big or too small?** If you round every number up, your estimate will be too big. If you round every number down, it will be too small.

> **Tip:** If your exact answer is a long way from your estimate, check your working.
