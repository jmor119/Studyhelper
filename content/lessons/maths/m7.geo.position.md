Maps, orienteering courses and computer games all use directions, turns and coordinates to describe where things are and how to get there. In this lesson you will use the eight compass points, turns in degrees, and coordinates.

## The eight compass points

The four main **compass points** are north (N), east (E), south (S) and west (W). Halfway between each pair is another point:

- **north-east** (NE) is halfway between north and east
- south-east (SE) is halfway between south and east
- **south-west** (SW) is halfway between south and west
- north-west (NW) is halfway between north and west

The names of the halfway points always start with north or south.

## Turns and degrees

A **turn** can be measured in **degrees**.

| Turn | Degrees |
|---|---|
| quarter turn | 90° |
| half turn | 180° |
| three-quarter turn | 270° |
| full turn | 360° |

**Clockwise** is the way clock hands move: N → NE → E. **Anticlockwise** is the other way: N → NW → W.

To work out a turn on the compass, count steps of 45°.

Aroha faces east and turns 135° anticlockwise. That is $135° \div 45° = 3$ steps. Counting anticlockwise from E: NE, N, NW. She now faces **north-west**.

## Coordinates on a map

## Describing a pathway

A **pathway** is a route made of steps. You can describe it in two ways.

- **With compass directions:** "Walk 3 squares north, then 4 squares east."
- **With turns:** "Face north. Walk 3 squares. Turn 90° clockwise. Walk 4 squares."

After a turn, you walk the way you are now facing, so keep track of your direction after every turn.

## Putting it together

- North is at the top of most maps. Look for a north arrow.
- Neighbouring compass points are 45° apart, so NE is 45° east of north.
- Count turns in steps of 45°: 90° is two steps, 135° is three steps and 180° is four steps.
- To describe a route, give a distance and a direction for each part, or a turn and then a distance.
- Check a route by following it backwards to the start.

> **Tip:** If each grid square stands for 100 m, then "3 squares east" means 300 metres east.

> **Watch out:** NE and NW are easy to mix up. North-**east** is on the **east** side of north, to the right of N on the compass.
