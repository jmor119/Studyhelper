Shapes are all around us: windows, kites, road signs and roofs. In this lesson you will name four-sided shapes and triangles, describe the angles inside them, find rotational symmetry, and compare prisms and pyramids.

## Quadrilaterals

A **quadrilateral** is a flat shape with four straight sides. Lines that are **parallel** always stay the same distance apart, like railway tracks.

Maths drawings use marks to show facts. Tick marks show sides that are the same length. Arrows show parallel sides. A small square shows a right angle (90°).

> **Watch out:** A square standing on one corner is still a square. Turning a shape never changes its name.

## Triangles and interior angles

An **interior angle** is an angle inside a shape, where two sides meet. A triangle has three interior angles and a quadrilateral has four.

You can name a triangle by its sides. An **equilateral** triangle has three equal sides. An **isosceles** triangle has two equal sides. A **scalene** triangle has no equal sides.

A rectangle has four interior angles, and they are all right angles. A parallelogram with no right angles has two acute angles and two obtuse angles. The two acute angles are opposite each other.

## Rotational symmetry

Imagine tracing a shape, then turning the tracing around its centre. If the tracing fits exactly on top of the shape before you have turned all the way round, the shape has **rotational symmetry**.

The **order of rotational symmetry** is the number of times the shape looks exactly the same during one full turn (360°).

### Example

An equilateral triangle looks the same after a turn of 120°, after 240° and after 360°. That is 3 times, so its order is 3. Notice that $360° \div 3 = 120°$.

A shape that only fits back on itself after a full turn has order 1. A regular shape (all sides and angles equal) with 6 sides has order 6.

## Prisms and pyramids

A **prism** is a 3D shape with two matching ends joined by rectangles. A **pyramid** has one base, and triangles that meet at a point called the apex. Both are named after the shape of their base.

3D shapes have **faces** (flat surfaces), **edges** (where two faces meet) and **vertices** (corners).

## Putting it together

- Use the marks on a drawing: tick marks for equal sides, arrows for parallel sides and small squares for right angles.
- Choose the **most exact** name. A rectangle with four equal sides is best called a square.
- Turning a shape doesn't change its name, its sides or its angles.
- For rotational symmetry, count how many times the shape fits onto itself in one full turn. Count the end of the turn, but not the start.
- For a prism: faces = sides of the base + 2, edges = 3 × sides of the base, and vertices = 2 × sides of the base.
- For a pyramid: faces = sides of the base + 1, edges = 2 × sides of the base, and vertices = sides of the base + 1.
