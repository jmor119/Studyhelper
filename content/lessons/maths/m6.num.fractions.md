Fractions describe equal parts of a whole, like half a pizza or three quarters of an hour. In this lesson you will find equivalent fractions, write fractions in simplest form, change between mixed numbers and improper fractions, and compare, add and subtract fractions.

## Equivalent fractions

In the fraction $\frac{3}{4}$:

- the **denominator** (4) is the number of equal parts the whole is split into
- the **numerator** (3) is how many of those parts you have.

**Equivalent fractions** are fractions that have the same value. Look at the bars below. If you cut each quarter in half, 3 quarters become 6 eighths. The shaded amount stays the same.

To make an equivalent fraction, **multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same number**:

$$\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 2} = \frac{6}{8}$$

### Example
Find the missing number: $\frac{2}{5} = \frac{\square}{20}$. The denominator was multiplied by 4 ($5 \times 4 = 20$), so multiply the numerator by 4 too: $2 \times 4 = 8$. So $\frac{2}{5} = \frac{8}{20}$.

## Simplest form and comparing

A **common factor** is a number that divides exactly into two numbers. 3 is a common factor of 12 and 18.

A fraction is in **simplest form** when the numerator and the denominator have no common factor except 1. To simplify, divide the top and the bottom by a common factor. Keep going until you can't divide any more.

### Example
$\frac{12}{18} = \frac{12 \div 6}{18 \div 6} = \frac{2}{3}$

**Comparing fractions.** If one denominator is a multiple of the other, change one fraction so they have the same denominator. Then compare the numerators.

### Example
Which is bigger, $\frac{3}{4}$ or $\frac{5}{8}$? Change quarters to eighths: $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{8}$. 6 eighths is more than 5 eighths, so $\frac{3}{4} > \frac{5}{8}$.

> **Watch out:** A bigger denominator means *smaller* pieces. $\frac{1}{8}$ is smaller than $\frac{1}{4}$.

## Mixed numbers and improper fractions

An **improper fraction** has a numerator that is bigger than (or equal to) its denominator, like $\frac{11}{4}$. A **mixed number** is a whole number and a fraction together, like $2\frac{3}{4}$.

**Improper to mixed:** divide the numerator by the denominator. $11 \div 4 = 2$ remainder 3, so $\frac{11}{4} = 2\frac{3}{4}$.

**Mixed to improper:** each whole has 4 quarters, so 2 wholes have 8 quarters. Add the 3 extra quarters: $8 + 3 = 11$. So $2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$.

A quick way: whole number × denominator + numerator. $3\frac{2}{5} = \frac{3 \times 5 + 2}{5} = \frac{17}{5}$

## Adding and subtracting fractions

You can only add or subtract fractions when they have the **same denominator**. Then add or subtract the numerators, and keep the denominator.

$$\frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$$

If one denominator is a multiple of the other, change one fraction first.

### Example
$\frac{1}{3} + \frac{5}{12}$. Change thirds to twelfths: $\frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{12}$. Then $\frac{4}{12} + \frac{5}{12} = \frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}$.

### Example
$2\frac{1}{2} + 1\frac{3}{4}$. Add the wholes: $2 + 1 = 3$. Add the fractions: $\frac{2}{4} + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{5}{4} = 1\frac{1}{4}$. Total: $3 + 1\frac{1}{4} = 4\frac{1}{4}$.

For subtracting mixed numbers, it often helps to change them to improper fractions first: $3\frac{1}{4} - 1\frac{1}{2} = \frac{13}{4} - \frac{6}{4} = \frac{7}{4} = 1\frac{3}{4}$.

> **Watch out:** Never add the denominators. $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4}$ is $\frac{3}{4}$, not $\frac{2}{6}$.

## Putting it together

- **Equivalent fractions:** multiply or divide the top and bottom by the same number.
- **Simplest form:** divide by common factors until the only one left is 1.
- **Compare** by giving the fractions the same denominator.
- **Mixed and improper:** whole × denominator + numerator. Divide to go back the other way.
- **Add and subtract** only when the denominators are the same. Simplify your answer at the end.
- **Sense check:** $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4}$ must be more than $\frac{1}{2}$, so $\frac{2}{6}$ can't be right.
