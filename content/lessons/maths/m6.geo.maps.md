Maps help us find places and plan how to get there. In this lesson you will use grid references, compass directions, turns and map scales to describe where things are and how to reach them.

## Grid references

Many maps have a grid drawn over them. On our maps, the **rows** are labelled with letters (A, B, C …) down the side. The **columns** are labelled with numbers (1, 2, 3 …) along the top.

A **grid reference** names one square. Write the **letter first**, then the **number**.

## Compass directions

A **compass** shows the four main directions: **north** (N), **east** (E), **south** (S) and **west** (W). On most maps, north is at the top. So south is at the bottom, east is to the right and west is to the left.

To remember the order going clockwise from north, try: **N**aughty **E**lephants **S**quirt **W**ater.

### Example

On the town map, the Dairy is in A6 and the School is in A2. They are in the same row, and the Dairy is further to the right. So the Dairy is **east** of the School. That also means the School is **west** of the Dairy.

## Turns

When you describe a turn, say how far and which way. **Clockwise** is the way clock hands move. **Anticlockwise** is the other way.

| Turn | Degrees |
|---|---|
| Quarter turn | 90° |
| Half turn | 180° |
| Three-quarter turn | 270° |
| Full turn | 360° |

## Map scales

A map is much smaller than the real place. A **map scale** tells you what real distance each distance on the map stands for. For example, **1 cm = 100 m** means every 1 cm on the map stands for 100 m on the ground.

### Example

On a map with a scale of 1 cm = 250 m, the track to the beach is 6 cm long. In real life it is $6 \times 250 = 1500$ m. There are 1000 m in 1 km, so that is 1.5 km.

To go the other way, divide. On the same map, a real distance of 1000 m is $1000 \div 250 = 4$ cm.

## Describing a pathway

To describe a route, give a starting point, then the directions, distances and turns in order.

### Example

Each square on the town map is 100 m wide. Start at the School (A2), facing south. Walk 3 squares to D2. Turn 90° anticlockwise, so you now face east. Walk 2 squares, and you reach the Hall in D4. You walked 5 squares, which is $5 \times 100 = 500$ m.
