How long will the drive to the beach take? How fast was that sprinter? Speed links distance and time, so if you know any two of the three you can find the third. You'll also learn to read speed from a distance–time graph.

## Speed is a rate

**Speed** tells you how much **distance** is covered in each unit of **time**. It is a **rate**:

$$\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$$

The units of speed come straight from the units you divide:

- kilometres ÷ hours → **km/h** (kilometres per hour)
- metres ÷ seconds → **m/s** (metres per second)

Most journeys speed up and slow down, so the answer is the **average speed**: the steady speed that would cover the same distance in the same time.

### Example

A bus travels 180 km in 3 hours. Average speed $= 180 \div 3 = 60$ km/h.

## Finding distance or time

Rearrange the formula:

$$\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time} \qquad \text{time} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$$

A formula triangle can help you remember: **D** on top, **S** and **T** underneath. Cover the one you want. D = S × T, S = D ÷ T, T = D ÷ S.

### Example

A cyclist rides at 18 km/h for 3 hours. Distance $= 18 \times 3 = 54$ km.

A ferry travels 96 km at 32 km/h. Time $= 96 \div 32 = 3$ hours.

## Mixing units

The units must match. If the speed is in km/h, the time must be in **hours**.

- Minutes → hours: divide by 60. So 45 min $= 45 \div 60 = 0.75$ h.
- Hours → minutes: multiply by 60. So 0.4 h $= 0.4 \times 60 = 24$ min.
- m/s → km/h: multiply by 3.6. km/h → m/s: divide by 3.6.

### Example

Tama drives 30 km in 20 minutes. What is his speed in km/h?

20 min $= 20 \div 60 = \frac{1}{3}$ h, so speed $= 30 \div \frac{1}{3} = 90$ km/h.

Or think: 20 minutes is a third of an hour, so in a whole hour he'd go 3 times as far: $3 \times 30 = 90$ km.

> **Watch out:** 1 hour 30 minutes is 1.5 h, **not** 1.3 h. Always divide minutes by 60.

## Average speed over a whole journey

For a journey in several parts:

$$\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}$$

Include any stops in the total time if the question says so.

### Example

Mere cycles 20 km to the lake at 20 km/h (1 hour) and back again at 10 km/h (2 hours). Her average speed is $40 \div 3 \approx 13.3$ km/h.

> **Watch out:** It is **not** the average of the two speeds, $(20 + 10) \div 2 = 15$ km/h. She spends longer going at the slower speed, so the slow part counts for more.

## Distance–time graphs

A **distance–time graph** (also called a position–time graph) shows how far something is from its starting point at each moment.

- The **gradient** (steepness) of the line is the **speed**: rise ÷ run = distance ÷ time.
- A **steeper** line means a **faster** speed.
- A **flat** (horizontal) line means the distance is not changing, so the object has **stopped**.
- A line sloping **down** means it is coming **back** towards the start.

### Example

A line goes from $(0, 0)$ to $(2, 50)$, with time in hours and distance in km. Speed $= 50 \div 2 = 25$ km/h.

## Exam tips

- Write the formula you are using, then substitute.
- Make the units match before you calculate (hours with km/h, seconds with m/s).
- Convert answers in hours to hours and minutes using × 60, not by reading the decimal digits.
- For average speed, find the **total distance** and the **total time** first.
- On graphs, pick two points where the line crosses grid lines exactly, then work out rise ÷ run.
