Every digit in a number has a value that depends on where it sits. In this lesson you will write place values as powers of 10, write numbers in expanded form, round numbers, and use estimates to check your answers.

## Place values are powers of 10

**Place value** is the value a digit has because of its place in a number. Each place is 10 times bigger than the place to its right.

We can write the places as **powers of 10**. The small raised number tells you how many 10s are multiplied together.

| Place | Value | Power of 10 |
|---|---|---|
| ten thousands | 10,000 | $10^4$ |
| thousands | 1,000 | $10^3$ |
| hundreds | 100 | $10^2$ |
| tens | 10 | $10^1$ |
| ones | 1 | |

$10^4 = 10 \times 10 \times 10 \times 10 = 10{,}000$. Notice that $10^4$ has 4 zeros.

The places carry on forever. To the left they keep getting 10 times bigger: $10^5$, $10^6$, … To the right of the decimal point they keep getting 10 times smaller: tenths, hundredths, thousandths, …

### Example

Which is bigger, $10^5$ or 90,000? $10^5 = 100{,}000$, which has 6 digits. 90,000 has only 5 digits, so $10^5$ is bigger.

## Expanded form

**Expanded form** shows a number as each digit times its place value, all added together.

### Example

$34{,}506 = 3 \times 10^4 + 4 \times 10^3 + 5 \times 10^2 + 6$

There are 0 tens, so that part is left out, because $0 \times 10 = 0$.

To go the other way, work out each part and add:

$7 \times 10^4 + 2 \times 10^2 + 9 \times 10 = 70{,}000 + 200 + 90 = 70{,}290$

> **Watch out:** A missing place needs a 0. There are no thousands and no ones in $70{,}290$, so those digits are 0.

## Rounding

To **round** a number means to swap it for a nearby number that is simpler. You can round to the nearest ten, hundred, thousand, or any power of 10.

1. Find the digit in the place you are rounding to.
2. Look at the digit just to its right.
3. If it is 5 or more, round **up**. If it is less than 5, round **down**.

### Example

Round 3,487 to the nearest hundred ($10^2$). The hundreds digit is 4. The next digit is 8, which is 5 or more, so round up to **3,500**.

**Decimals** work the same way. To round 6.483 to the nearest tenth, look at the hundredths digit, 8. It is 5 or more, so the answer is **6.5**. To the nearest whole number, 6.483 rounds to **6**, because the tenths digit is 4.

> **Watch out:** 3.96 rounded to the nearest tenth is 4.0. The 9 tenths round up to 10 tenths, which carries 1 into the ones.

## Estimating and benchmarks

An **estimate** is a rough answer you can work out quickly. Round the numbers first, then calculate.

### Example

Estimate $387 \times 42$. Round to $400 \times 40$. Since $4 \times 4 = 16$, and there are three zeros to put back, the estimate is **16,000**. If you worked out the exact answer and got 1,625, you would know something had gone wrong.

A **benchmark** is a friendly number you compare with, such as 0, 1, 10 or 100.

Each number in $0.73 + 0.8 + 0.999$ is less than the benchmark 1. So the total must be less than $1 + 1 + 1 = 3$. You don't need the exact answer to know that.

> **Tip:** Estimate before you calculate. Then check that your answer is close to your estimate.

## Putting it together

- Each place is a power of 10: $10^1 = 10$, $10^2 = 100$, $10^3 = 1{,}000$, …
- For a power of 10, the exponent tells you how many zeros there are.
- In expanded form, leave out places with a 0 digit. When you write the number back, put a 0 in each missing place.
- To round, look at the digit to the right of the place you are rounding to. 5 or more rounds up.
- Estimate first, then check that your exact answer is close.
