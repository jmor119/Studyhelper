Algebra lets you write rules that work for any number, not just one. Before you can solve equations or use formulas, you need to be able to tidy up expressions: simplifying, expanding and factorising them.

## The language of algebra

A **variable** is a letter that stands for a number that can change, like $x$ or $n$.

An expression such as $5x + 3y - 7$ is made of **terms**, separated by $+$ and $-$ signs. Its terms are $5x$, $3y$ and $-7$. Each term carries the sign in front of it.

- The **coefficient** is the number multiplying the variable. In $5x$ the coefficient is 5. In $-y$ it is $-1$.
- A term with no variable, like $-7$, is called a **constant**.

Some shorthand to remember:

| Written | Means |
|---|---|
| $3x$ | $3 \times x$ |
| $ab$ | $a \times b$ |
| $\frac{x}{4}$ | $x \div 4$ |
| $x^2$ | $x \times x$ |

> **Watch out:** $2x$ and $x^2$ are different. If $x = 5$, then $2x = 10$ but $x^2 = 25$.

## Properties of operations

These rules work for every number, so they work for variables too.

| Property | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Commutative** | Order doesn't matter for $+$ and $\times$ | $a + b = b + a$, $\;ab = ba$ |
| **Associative** | Grouping doesn't matter for $+$ and $\times$ | $(2 \times a) \times 3 = 2 \times (a \times 3)$ |
| **Distributive** | Multiplying a bracket multiplies every term in it | $3(x + 2) = 3x + 6$ |
| **Identity** | Adding 0 or multiplying by 1 changes nothing | $a + 0 = a$, $\;1 \times a = a$ |
| **Inverse** | Opposites undo each other | $a + (-a) = 0$, $\;a \times \frac{1}{a} = 1$ |

> **Watch out:** subtraction and division are **not** commutative: $5 - 2 \neq 2 - 5$.

### Example: multiplying terms

Simplify $3a \times 4b$.

Using the commutative and associative properties, rearrange it as $3 \times 4 \times a \times b = 12ab$.

## Collecting like terms

**Like terms** have exactly the same letter part. You can add or subtract like terms by combining their coefficients.

- $4a$ and $-2a$ are like terms.
- $3xy$ and $5yx$ are like terms, because $yx = xy$.
- $x$ and $x^2$ are **not** like terms, and neither are $a$ and $ab$.

### Example

Simplify $5a + 3b - 2a + 4b - 6$.

1. Group the like terms, keeping each term's sign: $5a - 2a + 3b + 4b - 6$
2. Combine them: $3a + 7b - 6$

The $-6$ has no like term, so it stays as it is.

### Example

Simplify $3x^2 + 5x - x^2 - 8x$.

$x^2$ terms: $3x^2 - x^2 = 2x^2$. $\;x$ terms: $5x - 8x = -3x$. Answer: $2x^2 - 3x$.

> **Tip:** Circle or underline each kind of term in a different way before you start. Don't forget the sign in front of each term.

## Expanding brackets

To **expand** a bracket, multiply every term inside it by the term outside. This is the **distributive** property.

Think of $3(x + 4)$ as the area of a rectangle 3 wide and $x + 4$ long. It splits into two parts: $3 \times x = 3x$ and $3 \times 4 = 12$. So $3(x + 4) = 3x + 12$.

### Example

Expand $-2(3x - 5)$.

- $-2 \times 3x = -6x$
- $-2 \times (-5) = +10$

So $-2(3x - 5) = -6x + 10$.

### Example: expand and simplify

$4(x + 2) + 3x = 4x + 8 + 3x = 7x + 8$

### Example: a letter outside

$2a(3b - 1) = 6ab - 2a$

> **Watch out:** the most common mistake is only multiplying the first term: $3(x + 4) \neq 3x + 4$. Another is losing a negative sign: in $5x - 2(x - 3)$, the $-2$ multiplies the $-3$ to give $+6$.

## Factorising

**Factorising** is the reverse of expanding: you put an expression *into* brackets by taking out a **common factor**.

1. Find the **highest common factor (HCF)** of all the terms.
2. Write it in front of a bracket.
3. Divide each term by the HCF to find what goes inside.
4. Check by expanding.

### Example

Factorise $12x + 18$.

The HCF of 12 and 18 is 6. $\;12x \div 6 = 2x$ and $18 \div 6 = 3$.

So $12x + 18 = 6(2x + 3)$. Check: $6 \times 2x + 6 \times 3 = 12x + 18$ ✓

**Factorise fully** means use the *highest* common factor. $2(6x + 9)$ is factorised, but not fully, because $6x + 9$ still has a common factor of 3.

### Example: a letter in the common factor

Factorise $6xy + 9x$. The HCF of the numbers is 3, and $x$ is in both terms, so the HCF is $3x$:

$6xy + 9x = 3x(2y + 3)$

> **Watch out:** if a term equals the HCF, a 1 is left behind: $7x + 7 = 7(x + 1)$, not $7(x)$.

## Exam tips

- **Read the instruction word.** *Simplify* means collect like terms. *Expand* means remove brackets. *Factorise* means put into brackets.
- **Signs belong to the term after them.** In $7 - 3x$, the term is $-3x$.
- **Check factorising by expanding.** It only takes a few seconds and catches most mistakes.
- **Factorise fully:** after factorising, look inside the bracket. If the terms still share a factor, you haven't used the HCF.
- **Don't combine unlike terms.** $3x + 2$ can't be simplified to $5x$, and $x + x^2$ isn't $2x^2$ or $x^3$.
- **Typing answers in this app:** write $3x$ as `3x` and $x^2$ as `x^2`. Brackets are fine, e.g. `4(2x + 3)`.
