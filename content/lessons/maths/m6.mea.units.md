We measure things every day: how tall you are, how much milk is left, how heavy your school bag is. New Zealand uses the **metric** system. All the metric units are linked by 10, 100 or 1000, so changing from one unit to another is easy.

## Metric units and prefixes

These are the units you will use most.

| Measuring | Units |
|---|---|
| Length | **millimetre** (mm), **centimetre** (cm), **metre** (m), **kilometre** (km) |
| Mass (how heavy) | **gram** (g), **kilogram** (kg) |
| Capacity (how much it holds) | **millilitre** (mL), **litre** (L) |

The start of each name is a **prefix**. It tells you how big the unit is.

- **milli-** means 1000 times smaller. 1 mm is one thousandth of a metre.
- **centi-** means 100 times smaller. 1 cm is one hundredth of a metre.
- **kilo-** means 1000 times bigger. 1 km is 1000 metres.

So 10 mm = 1 cm, 100 cm = 1 m, 1000 m = 1 km, 1000 g = 1 kg and 1000 mL = 1 L.

## Converting between units

To **convert** means to change a measurement into a different unit.

- Changing to a **smaller** unit? You need **more** of them, so **multiply**.
- Changing to a **bigger** unit? You need **fewer** of them, so **divide**.

### Example 1

Convert 3.5 m to centimetres. Centimetres are smaller, so multiply by 100.

$3.5 \times 100 = 350$, so 3.5 m = 350 cm.

### Example 2

Convert 2400 g to kilograms. Kilograms are bigger, so divide by 1000.

$2400 \div 1000 = 2.4$, so 2400 g = 2.4 kg.

> **Tip:** Multiplying by 100 moves every digit two places to the left. Dividing by 1000 moves every digit three places to the right.

> **Watch out:** Check that your answer makes sense. There should be lots of centimetres in a few metres, not the other way round.

## Mixed units

Sometimes a measurement uses two units, like 3 m 45 cm or 10 kg and 500 g. You can write it in one unit.

**In the smaller unit:** 3 m 45 cm = 300 cm + 45 cm = 345 cm.

**In the bigger unit:** 500 g is half of 1000 g, so it is 0.5 kg. That means 10 kg and 500 g = 10.5 kg.

### Example

Write 2 kg 50 g in kilograms.

- 50 g = $50 \div 1000 = 0.05$ kg.
- So 2 kg 50 g = **2.05 kg**.

> **Watch out:** 50 g is not 0.5 kg. 0.5 kg is 500 g. Use a zero to hold the tenths place: 2.05 kg.

## Reading scales

We use a ruler for length, kitchen scales for mass, a measuring jug for capacity and a thermometer for **temperature**. Temperature is measured in **degrees Celsius** (°C). Water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C.

To read a scale:

1. Find two labelled numbers next to each other.
2. Count the spaces between them.
3. Work out what each small mark is worth.
4. Count on from the labelled number.

## Estimating with benchmarks

To **estimate** is to make a good guess using what you know. A **benchmark** is something you already know the size of. Compare with it.

| Benchmark | About |
|---|---|
| Width of your little finger | 1 cm |
| Height of a classroom door | 2 m |
| A paper clip | 1 g |
| A 1 L bottle of milk | 1 kg |
| A teaspoon of water | 5 mL |
| A warm classroom | 20 °C |

### Example

About how tall is the classroom ceiling? It looks about one and a half doors high. One door is about 2 m, so the ceiling is about 3 m high.

> **Tip:** When you choose a unit, think about the size. Use mm or cm for small things, m for rooms and fields, and km for long trips.
