Powers are a short way to write repeated multiplication. They let you write very big numbers (like the distance to the Sun) and very small numbers (like the width of a blood cell) without long rows of zeros.

## Index notation

In $2^5$, the **base** is 2 and the **exponent** is 5. The exponent is also called the **index** or **power**. It tells you how many lots of the base to multiply together:

$2^5 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 32$

Learn the powers of 2 up to $2^{10}$. Each one is double the one before:

| $2^1$ | $2^2$ | $2^3$ | $2^4$ | $2^5$ | $2^6$ | $2^7$ | $2^8$ | $2^9$ | $2^{10}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 256 | 512 | 1024 |

Powers of 10 are 1 followed by that many zeros: $10^4 = 10\,000$.

> **Watch out:** $3^4$ means $3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 81$. It is not $3 \times 4 = 12$.

## Exponents of 1, 0, and negative exponents

Look at the pattern as the exponent goes down by 1. Each time, you **divide by the base**:

| $3^3$ | $3^2$ | $3^1$ | $3^0$ | $3^{-1}$ | $3^{-2}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 27 | 9 | 3 | 1 | $\frac{1}{3}$ | $\frac{1}{9}$ |

This shows three rules:

- **Exponent 1:** $a^1 = a$. One lot of the number is just the number.
- **Exponent 0:** $a^0 = 1$ for any number $a$ except 0. (Divide the base by itself.)
- **Negative exponents** mean repeated division, which gives a unit fraction: $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$. For example, $3^{-2} = \frac{1}{3^2} = \frac{1}{9}$ and $10^{-3} = \frac{1}{1000} = 0.001$.

> **Watch out:** A negative exponent does **not** make the number negative. $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}$, which is positive.

## Square roots and perfect squares

A **perfect square** is a whole number multiplied by itself: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225, …

The **square root** of a number is what you multiply by itself to get that number: $\sqrt{49} = 7$ because $7 \times 7 = 49$.

If a number isn't a perfect square, its square root lies between two whole numbers. Find the perfect squares either side.

### Example

Where is $\sqrt{30}$? We know $5^2 = 25$ and $6^2 = 36$, and 30 is between 25 and 36, so $\sqrt{30}$ is between 5 and 6. 30 is closer to 25 than to 36, so $\sqrt{30}$ is closer to 5. (In fact $\sqrt{30} \approx 5.48$.)

On a number line, $\sqrt{30}$ sits between 5 and 6, a bit left of the middle.

## Scientific notation

**Scientific notation** writes a number as $a \times 10^k$, where $1 \le a < 10$ and $k$ is an integer.

**Big numbers** have a positive power of 10. The Earth is about 150 000 000 km from the Sun:

$150\,000\,000 = 1.5 \times 10^8$ km (the decimal point moves 8 places)

**Small numbers** (less than 1) have a negative power of 10. A red blood cell is about 0.000007 m wide:

$0.000007 = 7 \times 10^{-6}$ m

To change back, move the decimal point: right for a positive power, left for a negative power. $3.2 \times 10^4 = 32\,000$ and $3.2 \times 10^{-4} = 0.00032$.

> **Watch out:** $45 \times 10^3$ has the right value but is **not** scientific notation, because 45 is not between 1 and 10. Write it as $4.5 \times 10^4$.

## Comparing and ordering in scientific notation

To compare numbers in scientific notation:

1. **Compare the powers of 10 first.** The bigger the power, the bigger the number. Remember that $-2$ is bigger than $-5$, so $10^{-2}$ is bigger than $10^{-5}$.
2. **If the powers are the same**, compare the front numbers.

### Example

Order $3.1 \times 10^{4}$, $9.8 \times 10^{3}$, $2.5 \times 10^{4}$ and $7 \times 10^{-2}$ from smallest to largest.

- $7 \times 10^{-2}$ has the smallest power (it is 0.07).
- $9.8 \times 10^{3}$ comes next (9800).
- $2.5 \times 10^4$ and $3.1 \times 10^4$ have the same power, and 2.5 < 3.1.

Order: $7 \times 10^{-2}$, $9.8 \times 10^{3}$, $2.5 \times 10^{4}$, $3.1 \times 10^{4}$.

> **Tip:** If you're unsure, write each number out as an ordinary number and compare.

## Exam tips

- **Exponent = how many lots of the base**, not something to multiply by.
- **$a^0 = 1$**, and **negative exponents give fractions**, not negative numbers.
- **Square roots:** list the perfect squares near the number to find the two whole numbers either side.
- **Scientific notation check:** is the front number at least 1 and less than 10? Is the power positive for a big number and negative for a small one?
- **Calculators and spreadsheets** may show $4.7 \times 10^{-5}$ as 4.7E-05. It means $4.7 \times 10^{-5} = 0.000047$.
