Shopping, saving and getting paid all use percentages, ratios and rates. This lesson covers discounts, mark-ups, GST and simple interest: the money maths you will use for the rest of your life. A calculator is allowed for this topic.

## Discounts

A **discount** is an amount taken off a price.

**Discount amount:** find the percentage of the original price. 25% off \$80: $0.25 \times 80 = 20$, so the discount is \$20.

**Sale price:** take the discount off, or use a multiplier. After 25% off you pay 75% of the price:

$80 \times 0.75 = 60$, so the sale price is \$60.

**Percentage discount:** divide the discount by the **original** price.

A hoodie is reduced from \$50 to \$42. The discount is \$8, and $\frac{8}{50} \times 100 = 16$, so it is a 16% discount.

> **Watch out:** Two discounts in a row don't add. 20% off, then a further 10% off, is $0.8 \times 0.9 = 0.72$. That is 28% off altogether, not 30%.

## Mark-ups

Shops buy goods at a **cost price** and sell them at a higher **selling price**. The amount added on is the **mark-up**. It is often given as a percentage of the cost price.

### Example

A shop buys a jacket for \$40 and adds a 30% mark-up.

- Mark-up: $0.3 \times 40 = 12$, so \$12.
- Selling price: $40 + 12 = 52$, or in one step $40 \times 1.3 = 52$. So the selling price is \$52.

**Percentage mark-up:** divide the mark-up by the **cost price**. A café buys muffins for \$2.50 and sells them for \$4.00. The mark-up is \$1.50, and $\frac{1.50}{2.50} \times 100 = 60$, so it is a 60% mark-up.

## GST

**GST** (goods and services tax) is a tax added to most things you buy in New Zealand. The rate is **15%**. Prices in shops normally *include* GST. Businesses often quote prices "plus GST" or "excluding GST".

| To… | Do this | Example |
|---|---|---|
| find the GST on a price without GST | × 0.15 | GST on \$80 is $80 \times 0.15 = 12$ |
| add GST | × 1.15 | \$80 + GST is $80 \times 1.15 = 92$ |
| remove GST from a GST-inclusive price | ÷ 1.15 | $92 \div 1.15 = 80$ |
| find the GST in a GST-inclusive price | inclusive price − price without GST | $92 - 80 = 12$ |

> **Watch out:** To remove GST, divide by 1.15. Taking 15% off \$92 gives \$78.20, which is wrong, because the 15% was worked out on \$80, not \$92.

**Shortcut:** the GST in a GST-inclusive price is $\frac{3}{23}$ of it, because $\frac{0.15}{1.15} = \frac{3}{23}$. For example, $92 \times \frac{3}{23} = 12$.

## Simple interest

When you save money in a bank, the bank pays you **interest**. When you borrow, you pay interest. The **principal** is the amount invested or borrowed. The **interest rate** is a percentage per year (often written "p.a.", for *per annum*).

With **simple interest**, you earn the same amount of interest every year:

$$I = P \times r \times t$$

- $I$ is the interest.
- $P$ is the principal.
- $r$ is the interest rate per year, written as a decimal.
- $t$ is the time in years.

### Example

\$2000 is invested at 4% p.a. simple interest for 3 years.

$I = 2000 \times 0.04 \times 3 = 240$, so the interest is \$240. The total in the account at the end is $2000 + 240 = 2240$, which is \$2240.

> **Watch out:** The time must be in **years**. 18 months is $18 \div 12 = 1.5$ years.

## Rates and ratios with money

Money problems often use rates and ratios too.

- **Hourly pay** is a rate: \$24.50 per hour for 6 hours is $24.50 \times 6 = 147$, so \$147.
- **Time-and-a-half** means 1.5 times the normal hourly rate. At \$24 per hour, overtime pays $24 \times 1.5 = 36$, so \$36 per hour.
- **Commission** is a percentage of sales. 5% commission on \$20 000 of sales is $0.05 \times 20\,000 = 1000$, so \$1000.
- **Sharing in a ratio:** three friends earn \$180 at a car wash and share it in the ratio of the hours they worked, $2 : 3 : 4$. One share is $180 \div 9 = 20$, so they get \$40, \$60 and \$80.

## Exam tips

- **Use multipliers:** 25% off is × 0.75, a 30% mark-up is × 1.3, adding GST is × 1.15.
- **Divide by the right amount.** Percentage discounts divide by the original price; percentage mark-ups divide by the cost price.
- **Remove GST by dividing by 1.15.** Never take 15% off a GST-inclusive price.
- **Simple interest:** $I = Prt$, with $r$ as a decimal and $t$ in years. Read whether the question asks for the interest or the total amount.
- **Money answers** go to 2 decimal places (cents) unless they are whole dollars, and they need a \$ sign.
