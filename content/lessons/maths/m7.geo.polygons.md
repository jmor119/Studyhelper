A **polygon** is a flat, closed shape with straight sides. In this lesson you will find out what the angles inside any polygon add up to, and use that to find missing angles.

## Polygons and their interior angles

Polygons are named by how many sides they have.

| Sides | Name |
|---|---|
| 3 | triangle |
| 4 | quadrilateral |
| 5 | **pentagon** |
| 6 | **hexagon** |
| 8 | **octagon** |
| 10 | decagon |

An **interior angle** is an angle inside the shape, at one of its corners.

You already know that the angles in a triangle add up to 180°. A line from one corner of a quadrilateral to the opposite corner cuts it into two triangles. So the angles of a quadrilateral add up to $2 \times 180° = 360°$.

A quadrilateral has angles of 85°, 95° and 110°. The fourth angle is $360° - 85° - 95° - 110° = 70°$.

## The angle sum of any polygon

Pick one corner of a polygon. Draw lines from it to every other corner you can reach. The polygon splits into triangles, and a polygon with $n$ sides always splits into $n - 2$ triangles.

```figure
{"svg":"<svg xmlns=\"http://www.w3.org/2000/svg\" viewBox=\"0 0 380 265\" width=\"380\" height=\"265\" font-family=\"Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif\" font-size=\"14\"><rect width=\"100%\" height=\"100%\" fill=\"#ffffff\"/><polygon points=\"125.73,225 220.53,225 301.2,109.8 264.49,31.08 148.38,25 78.8,124.37\" fill=\"#dbeafe\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"2\"/><line x1=\"125.73\" y1=\"225\" x2=\"301.2\" y2=\"109.8\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"2\" stroke-dasharray=\"5 4\"/><line x1=\"125.73\" y1=\"225\" x2=\"264.49\" y2=\"31.08\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"2\" stroke-dasharray=\"5 4\"/><line x1=\"125.73\" y1=\"225\" x2=\"148.38\" y2=\"25\" stroke=\"#1f2937\" stroke-width=\"2\" stroke-dasharray=\"5 4\"/><text x=\"215.82\" y=\"186.6\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"16\" fill=\"#2563eb\" font-weight=\"bold\">1</text><text x=\"230.47\" y=\"121.96\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"16\" fill=\"#2563eb\" font-weight=\"bold\">2</text><text x=\"179.53\" y=\"93.69\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"16\" fill=\"#2563eb\" font-weight=\"bold\">3</text><text x=\"117.64\" y=\"124.79\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"16\" fill=\"#2563eb\" font-weight=\"bold\">4</text><text x=\"190\" y=\"250\" text-anchor=\"middle\" dominant-baseline=\"middle\" font-size=\"13\" fill=\"#1f2937\">Four triangles: 4 × 180° = 720°</text></svg>","alt":"A hexagon with dashed lines from one corner to three other corners, making four triangles numbered 1 to 4."}
```

So the **angle sum** (the total of all the interior angles) is:

$$\text{angle sum} = 180° \times (n - 2)$$

| Shape | Sides | Triangles | Angle sum |
|---|---|---|---|
| triangle | 3 | 1 | 180° |
| quadrilateral | 4 | 2 | 360° |
| pentagon | 5 | 3 | 540° |
| hexagon | 6 | 4 | 720° |
| octagon | 8 | 6 | 1080° |

### Example

A hexagon has 6 sides, so it splits into $6 - 2 = 4$ triangles. Its angles add up to $180° \times 4 = 720°$.

Imagine walking all the way around the edge of a polygon. At each corner you turn through the exterior angle. When you get back to the start, you have turned through one full turn. So **the exterior angles of any polygon add up to 360°**, however many sides it has.

### Example

Four of the exterior angles of this pentagon are 80°, 60°, 85° and 70°. The fifth one is $x = 360° - 80° - 60° - 85° - 70° = 65°$.

## Regular polygons

A **regular polygon** has all its sides equal and all its angles equal. A square and an equilateral triangle are regular polygons.

In a regular polygon with $n$ sides:

- each exterior angle $= 360° \div n$
- each interior angle $= 180° - \text{exterior angle}$

```

### Example

A regular octagon has 8 sides.

- Each exterior angle $= 360° \div 8 = 45°$
- Each interior angle $= 180° - 45° = 135°$

Check with the angle sum: $8 \times 135° = 1080°$ ✓

You can also work backwards. If each exterior angle is 24°, the polygon has $360 \div 24 = 15$ sides.

> **Tip:** Stop signs are regular octagons, so each corner of a stop sign is 135°.

## Putting it together: missing angles

To find a missing interior angle:

1. Count the sides and work out the angle sum.
2. Add up the angles you know.
3. Subtract from the angle sum.

```

### Example

This pentagon has angles of 100°, 120°, 95° and 115°, and a missing angle $x$.

- Angle sum of a pentagon: $180° \times 3 = 540°$
- Angles you know: $100° + 120° + 95° + 115° = 430°$
- $x = 540° - 430° = 110°$

> **Watch out:** A pentagon's angles add up to $180° \times 3$, not $180° \times 5$. Always take 2 away from the number of sides.

> **Tip:** If two missing angles are equal, take the known angles away from the angle sum, then share what is left between them.
