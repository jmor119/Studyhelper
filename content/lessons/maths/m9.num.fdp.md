$\frac{1}{4}$, 0.25 and 25% are three ways of writing the same number. Being able to switch between them quickly makes comparing, ordering and calculating much easier, and it is the foundation for percentages and probability.

## Three ways to write the same number

A **percentage** is a number out of 100: 25% means $\frac{25}{100}$. Learn these common ones by heart:

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| $\frac{1}{2}$ | 0.5 | 50% |
| $\frac{1}{4}$ | 0.25 | 25% |
| $\frac{3}{4}$ | 0.75 | 75% |
| $\frac{1}{5}$ | 0.2 | 20% |
| $\frac{1}{10}$ | 0.1 | 10% |
| $\frac{1}{8}$ | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| $\frac{1}{3}$ | 0.333… | $33\frac{1}{3}$% |
| $\frac{2}{3}$ | 0.666… | $66\frac{2}{3}$% |

Percentages can be more than 100%: $1\frac{1}{2} = 1.5 = 150\%$. More than 100% means more than one whole.

## Converting between forms

| From → to | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| fraction → decimal | divide the top by the bottom, or make the denominator 10, 100 or 1000 | $\frac{7}{20} = \frac{35}{100} = 0.35$ |
| decimal → percentage | × 100 | $0.35 \times 100 = 35\%$ |
| percentage → decimal | ÷ 100 | $8\% = 0.08$ |
| decimal → fraction | use place value, then simplify | $0.125 = \frac{125}{1000} = \frac{1}{8}$ |
| percentage → fraction | write it over 100, then simplify | $45\% = \frac{45}{100} = \frac{9}{20}$ |

### Example

Write $\frac{3}{8}$ as a percentage. First $3 \div 8 = 0.375$, then $0.375 \times 100 = 37.5\%$.

> **Watch out:** 0.5% is not 0.5. It is $0.5 \div 100 = 0.005$, which is one two-hundredth.

## Comparing, ordering and number lines

To compare numbers written in different forms, change them all to the **same form**. Decimals are usually easiest.

### Example

Order $\frac{2}{3}$, 0.6, 65% and $\frac{5}{8}$ from smallest to largest.

As decimals: $0.666\ldots$, $0.6$, $0.65$, $0.625$. So the order is 0.6, $\frac{5}{8}$, 65%, $\frac{2}{3}$.

A **number line** shows the order and size of numbers. To read one, first work out what each small division is worth.

- From 0 to 1 split into 5 equal parts, each part is $\frac{1}{5} = 0.2 = 20\%$. The third mark is 0.6 or 60%.
- From 2 to 3 split into 4 equal parts, each part is 0.25. The first mark after 2 is 2.25.

Negative numbers go to the left of 0: halfway between −1 and 0 is −0.5.

> **Tip:** When you place numbers on a line, put the benchmarks in first: 0, $\frac{1}{2}$, 1. Then decide which side of each benchmark your number goes.

## Terminating and recurring decimals

A **terminating decimal** stops: $\frac{3}{8} = 0.375$.

A **recurring decimal** repeats forever: $\frac{1}{3} = 0.333\ldots$ and $\frac{5}{11} = 0.454545\ldots$ We show the repeating part with dots. Put a dot over a single repeating digit, or over the first and last digits of a repeating block:

- $0.333\ldots = 0.\dot{3}$
- $0.1666\ldots = 0.1\dot{6}$
- $0.454545\ldots = 0.\dot{4}\dot{5}$

**Which fractions terminate?** Write the fraction in simplest form and look at the denominator. If its only prime factors are 2 and 5, the decimal terminates. Otherwise it recurs. For example, $\frac{7}{20}$ terminates ($20 = 2 \times 2 \times 5$), but $\frac{7}{12}$ recurs ($12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$).

## Rational numbers, and the numbers in between

A **rational number** is any number that can be written as a fraction of two integers (with a denominator that isn't 0). Integers, fractions, terminating decimals and recurring decimals are all rational: $-4 = \frac{-4}{1}$, $0.375 = \frac{3}{8}$, $0.\dot{3} = \frac{1}{3}$.

Between any two numbers there are **infinitely many** rational numbers. You can always find one by going halfway:

- Halfway between 0.4 and 0.5 is $(0.4 + 0.5) \div 2 = 0.45$.
- Halfway between 0.4 and 0.45 is 0.425.
- Halfway between 0.4 and 0.425 is 0.4125, and so on forever.

With fractions, use a common denominator: halfway between $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$ is $\left(\frac{2}{6} + \frac{3}{6}\right) \div 2 = \frac{5}{6} \div 2 = \frac{5}{12}$.

## Exam tips

- **Same form first.** Change everything to decimals before comparing or ordering.
- **Use enough decimal places.** Write $\frac{1}{3}$ as 0.3333…, not 0.3, when comparing it with 0.33.
- **Simplify fractions** in your final answer unless the question says otherwise.
- **Percent means ÷ 100.** 12.5% = 0.125 and 150% = 1.5.
- **Recurring or terminating?** Simplify the fraction, then check the denominator's prime factors: only 2s and 5s means it terminates.
