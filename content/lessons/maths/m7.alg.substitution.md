An expression like $3n + 2$ doesn't have one value. Its value depends on what $n$ is. In this lesson you will put numbers in place of letters, use formulae, and change a formula around.

## Substitution

To **substitute** means to replace a letter with a number. Then you work out the **value** of the expression.

### Example

Find the value of $w + 12$ when $w = 4$.

Replace $w$ with 4: $w + 12 = 4 + 12 = 16$.

Remember that a number next to a letter means multiply. So when $a = 5$:

- $3a = 3 \times 5 = 15$ (not 35)
- $a^2 = 5 \times 5 = 25$ (not 10)
- $\frac{a}{5} = 5 \div 5 = 1$

With two letters, replace each one. When $p = 3$ and $q = 7$, $pq = 3 \times 7 = 21$.

> **Tip:** Write the × signs back in when you substitute. It stops you from writing $3a$ as 35.

## Using the order of operations

After you substitute, use the order of operations (GEMA): brackets (grouped) first, then exponents (powers), then × and ÷, then + and −.

### Example 1

Find $2 + 3x$ when $x = 4$.

$2 + 3 \times 4 = 2 + 12 = 14$. Multiply before you add. The answer is not $5 \times 4 = 20$.

### Example 2

Find $4(y + 2)$ when $y = 6$.

Brackets first: $y + 2 = 8$. Then $4 \times 8 = 32$.

### Example 3

Find $a^2 + b$ when $a = 3$ and $b = 10$.

Powers first: $a^2 = 9$. Then $9 + 10 = 19$.

## Formulae

A **formula** is a rule that connects different amounts. The plural of formula is **formulae**. Here are some formulae you will use:

| Shape | Formula |
|---|---|
| Area of a rectangle | $A = lw$ |
| Perimeter of a rectangle | $P = 2(l + w)$ |
| Perimeter of a square | $P = 4l$ |
| Volume of a rectangular prism | $V = lwh$ |

### Example

For the rectangle above, $A = lw = 8 \times 5 = 40$, so the area is 40 cm². The perimeter is $P = 2(8 + 5) = 2 \times 13 = 26$ cm.

You can also use a formula to find a missing value. A rectangle has an area of 40 cm² and a length of 8 cm. Then $40 = 8 \times w$, so $w = 40 \div 8 = 5$ cm.

## Rearranging formulae

The **subject** of a formula is the letter on its own, on one side of the equals sign. In $A = lw$, the subject is $A$.

To **rearrange** a formula means to change which letter is the subject. Use **inverse operations** (opposites): + and − undo each other, and × and ÷ undo each other. Do the same thing to both sides.

### Example 1: one step

Make $w$ the subject of $A = lw$.

$w$ is multiplied by $l$, so divide both sides by $l$: $w = \frac{A}{l}$.

### Example 2: two steps

Make $n$ the subject of $c = 5n + 3$.

Undo the last step first. Subtract 3 from both sides: $c - 3 = 5n$.

Then divide both sides by 5: $n = \frac{c - 3}{5}$.

## Putting it together

- **Substitute** by replacing each letter with its number. Write the × signs back in.
- $3a$ means $3 \times a$, $ab$ means $a \times b$, and $a^2$ means $a \times a$.
- Use the order of operations: brackets, powers, × and ÷, then + and −.
- Write the formula first, then substitute. Include units in your answer (cm, cm², cm³).
- To find a missing value, put in what you know and use inverse operations.
- To rearrange, undo the steps in reverse order, doing the same to both sides.
- Check by substituting your answer back in.
