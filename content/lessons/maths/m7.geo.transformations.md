A **transformation** moves a shape to a new position. The starting shape is the **object**, and the shape after the move is the **image**. In this lesson you will translate, reflect and rotate shapes on a coordinate grid, and work out the coordinates of the image.

## Three kinds of transformation

- A **translation** slides a shape. It doesn't turn or flip.
- A **reflection** flips a shape over a **mirror line**. The image is a mirror image.
- A **rotation** turns a shape around a fixed point, called the **centre of rotation**.

In all three, the image is exactly the same shape and size as the object. Only its position (and sometimes the way it faces) changes.

We label the image with a small dash called "prime". The image of point $A$ is $A'$, which you say as "A prime".

## Translations

In a translation, every point moves the same distance in the same direction.

- Moving **right** adds to the $x$-coordinate. Moving **left** takes away from it.
- Moving **up** adds to the $y$-coordinate. Moving **down** takes away from it.

### Example

Triangle $ABC$ has corners $A(1, 2)$, $B(4, 2)$ and $C(1, 4)$. Translate it 3 units left and 5 units down.

- $A(1, 2) \to A'(1 - 3, 2 - 5) = A'(-2, -3)$
- $B(4, 2) \to B'(1, -3)$
- $C(1, 4) \to C'(-2, -1)$

## Reflections

In a reflection, each point of the image is the same distance from the mirror line as the matching point of the object, but on the other side. Measure straight across to the line.

- Reflecting in the **$x$-axis**: $x$ stays the same and $y$ changes sign. $(3, 2) \to (3, -2)$
- Reflecting in the **$y$-axis**: $y$ stays the same and $x$ changes sign. $(3, 2) \to (-3, 2)$

For other mirror lines, such as $x = 1$, count the squares to the line.

### Example

Reflect triangle $ABC$ in the line $x = 1$. $A(3, 1)$ is 2 units to the right of the line, so $A'$ is 2 units to the left of it, at $(-1, 1)$. In the same way, $B(5, 1) \to B'(-3, 1)$ and $C(5, 4) \to C'(-3, 4)$.

## Rotations

To describe a rotation you need three things: the **centre of rotation**, the angle (such as 90° or 180°), and the direction. **Clockwise** is the way clock hands move. **Anticlockwise** is the other way.

A good way to rotate a shape is with tracing paper. Trace the shape, hold the paper down at the centre with your pencil point, turn the paper, then mark the new corners.

For rotations about the origin $(0, 0)$:

- 180°: both coordinates change sign. $(2, 3) \to (-2, -3)$
- 90° clockwise: $(2, 3) \to (3, -2)$
- 90° anticlockwise: $(2, 3) \to (-3, 2)$

### Example

Rotate triangle $ABC$ 90° clockwise about the origin. $A(1, 1) \to A'(1, -1)$, $B(4, 1) \to B'(1, -4)$ and $C(1, 3) \to C'(3, -1)$.

## Putting it together

When you describe a transformation, give all the details.

| Transformation | What to say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Translation | how far across, and how far up or down | 4 units right and 2 units down |
| Reflection | the mirror line | reflection in the line $y = -1$ |
| Rotation | the centre, the angle and the direction | 90° anticlockwise about $(0, 0)$ |

To work out which transformation it is:

- Is the image a mirror image of the object? Then it is a **reflection**.
- Is it the same way round, but turned to face a new direction? Then it is a **rotation**.
- Is it the same way round and facing the same direction? Then it is a **translation**.

> **Tip:** Always check your answer with one corner. Does the matching corner of the image end up in the right place?
