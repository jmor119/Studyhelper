An **angle** is the amount of turn between two lines that meet at a point. Builders, sailors and netball players all think about angles. In this lesson you will name, measure and work out angles.

## Types of angles

Angles are measured in **degrees** (°). A full turn is 360°. Half a turn is 180°, and a quarter turn is 90°.

The point where the two lines meet is the **vertex**. The two lines are the **arms**.

| Type | Size |
|---|---|
| **Acute** | less than 90° |
| **Right angle** | exactly 90° (marked with a small square) |
| **Obtuse** | more than 90°, less than 180° |
| **Straight** | exactly 180° |
| **Reflex** | more than 180°, less than 360° |

### Example

An angle of 200° is more than a straight line, so it is a reflex angle.

> **Tip:** Compare with a square corner, like the corner of a book. Smaller is acute, bigger is obtuse.

## Measuring with a protractor

A **protractor** measures angles. It has two scales, one going each way round.

1. Put the centre of the protractor on the vertex.
2. Line up the 0 line with one arm.
3. Use the scale that starts at **0 on that arm**. Count round to the other arm.

> **Watch out:** Check your answer. This angle is smaller than a right angle, so it can't be 130°.

**Reflex angles:** a protractor only goes to 180°. Measure the smaller angle, then take it away from 360°. If the smaller angle is 50°, the reflex angle is $360 - 50 = 310°$.

**Drawing an angle:** draw one arm with a ruler. Put the protractor's centre on one end. Make a dot at the number you want, then join the dot to the end.

### Example 2

Three angles sit on a straight line: 40°, 75° and $c$. Add the angles you know: $40 + 75 = 115$. Then $c = 180 - 115 = 65°$.

**Angle notation:** we can name an angle with three letters. $\angle ABC$ means the angle at B, between the arms going to A and C. The **middle letter is always the vertex**.

## Putting it together

Many problems use more than one angle fact. Write down the fact you use at each step.

### Example

Three angles at a point are a right angle, 150° and $c$. Find $c$.

- A right angle is 90°.
- Angles at a point add to 360°: $90 + 150 = 240$.
- $c = 360 - 240 = 120°$.

> **Tip:** Diagrams in tests are often **not to scale**. Work the angles out with angle facts. Don't measure them.

> **Watch out:** Use 180° for a straight line and 360° for a full turn. Mixing them up is the most common mistake.

Quick check that your answer makes sense: if the angle looks acute, your answer should be less than 90°.
