**Area** is the amount of flat surface a shape covers, measured in square units such as cm² or m². You'll need it to work out how much paint, carpet or grass seed to buy. Every formula in this lesson comes from one idea: the area of a rectangle.

## Rectangles and square units

A rectangle 5 cm long and 3 cm wide can be covered by 3 rows of 5 one-centimetre squares, so its area is $5 \times 3 = 15$ cm².

$$A_{\text{rectangle}} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$$

Area units are **square** units: mm², cm², m², km². Both lengths must be in the same unit before you multiply.

> **Watch out:** Perimeter and area are different. Perimeter is the distance around the edge (cm). Area is the space inside (cm²). Two shapes can have the same perimeter but different areas.

## Triangles

Draw a rectangle around a triangle, using the same base and height. The triangle fills exactly **half** of it. So:

$$A_{\text{triangle}} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{perpendicular height}$$

The **perpendicular height** is the distance from the base to the opposite corner, measured at a **right angle** to the base. It is usually drawn as a dashed line with a small square at the bottom. It is not a sloping side.

For an obtuse triangle, the perpendicular height can fall **outside** the triangle. You still use it, measured to the extended base line.

### Example

A triangle has base 12 cm, perpendicular height 7 cm, and a sloping side of 9 cm.

$A = \frac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 7 = 42$ cm². The 9 cm side is not needed.

## Parallelograms

A **parallelogram** has two pairs of parallel sides. Cut the triangle off one end and slide it to the other end, and the parallelogram becomes a rectangle with the same base and height.

$$A_{\text{parallelogram}} = \text{base} \times \text{perpendicular height}$$

### Example

A parallelogram has base 15 cm, perpendicular height 8 cm and slanted sides of 10 cm.

$A = 15 \times 8 = 120$ cm². The slanted side (10 cm) is a distractor.

## Trapeziums and kites

A **trapezium** has one pair of parallel sides, $a$ and $b$. Two identical trapeziums, one turned upside down, fit together to make a parallelogram with base $a + b$. The trapezium is half of that:

$$A_{\text{trapezium}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b) \times h$$

A **kite** has two pairs of equal sides next to each other, and its diagonals cross at right angles. Draw a rectangle around the kite using the diagonals as its length and width. The kite fills exactly half of the rectangle:

$$A_{\text{kite}} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2$$

### Example

A trapezium has parallel sides 6 m and 10 m and height 4 m: $A = \frac{1}{2} \times (6 + 10) \times 4 = 32$ m².

A kite has diagonals 9 cm and 14 cm: $A = \frac{1}{2} \times 9 \times 14 = 63$ cm².

## Composite shapes

A **composite shape** is made from simpler shapes. There are two strategies:

- **Add:** split it into rectangles, triangles and so on, find each area, and add them.
- **Subtract:** find the area of a big shape, then take away the part that is missing (such as a corner cut out, or a pond in the middle of a lawn).

### Example

A lawn is a 20 m by 12 m rectangle with a 4 m by 3 m garden bed in the middle. Lawn area $= 20 \times 12 - 4 \times 3 = 240 - 12 = 228$ m².

If grass seed costs \$2 per square metre, seeding the lawn costs 228 × \$2 = \$456.

## Exam tips

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | $l \times w$ |
| Triangle | $\frac{1}{2} \times b \times h$ |
| Parallelogram | $b \times h$ |
| Trapezium | $\frac{1}{2}(a + b) \times h$ |
| Kite | $\frac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2$ |

- $h$ is always the **perpendicular** height. Ignore sloping sides.
- Write the formula, substitute, then calculate. That earns method marks even if you slip.
- Check your units are the same, and give the answer in **square** units.
