Time helps us plan our day. When does the bus come? How long until lunch? When is Matariki this year? In this lesson you will change between units of time, use 12-hour and 24-hour time, work out how long things take, and read timetables.

## Units of time

| | |
|---|---|
| 60 **seconds** (s) | = 1 **minute** (min) |
| 60 minutes | = 1 **hour** (h) |
| 24 hours | = 1 day |
| 7 days | = 1 week |

To change hours into minutes, multiply by 60. To change minutes into hours, divide by 60.

### Example 1

3 hours = $3 \times 60 = 180$ minutes.

### Example 2

150 minutes = 120 minutes + 30 minutes = 2 hours 30 minutes.

> **Watch out:** Time does not work in tens. Half an hour is 30 minutes, so 1.5 hours is 1 hour 30 minutes, not 1 hour 50 minutes.

## 12-hour and 24-hour time

In **12-hour time** we use **am** for times from midnight to midday, and **pm** for times from midday to midnight.

In **24-hour time** the hours go from 00 to 23, so you don't need am or pm. We always write four digits, like 07:15 or 19:40.

- **am to 24-hour:** keep the hour and write it with two digits. 7:15 am = 07:15.
- **pm to 24-hour:** add 12 to the hour. 7:40 pm = 19:40.

## Elapsed time

**Elapsed time** (or **duration**) is how long something takes, from start to finish.

A good way to work it out is to **count on**: first to the next o'clock, then on from there.


### Example 1

From 2:53 pm to 3:28 pm:

- 2:53 pm → 3:00 pm is 7 minutes.
- 3:00 pm → 3:28 pm is 28 minutes.
- $7 + 28 = 35$ minutes.

### Example 2

From 9:45 am to 1:20 pm:

- 9:45 am → 10:00 am is 15 minutes.
- 10:00 am → 1:00 pm is 3 hours.
- 1:00 pm → 1:20 pm is 20 minutes.
- Total: 3 hours and 35 minutes.

> **Watch out:** Don't just subtract the numbers. 3:20 − 2:50 is not 70 minutes, because an hour has 60 minutes, not 100.

## Reading timetables

A **timetable** shows when buses, trains or events happen. Here is part of a bus timetable.

| Stop | Bus 1 | Bus 2 | Bus 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Library | 07:30 | 08:00 | 08:30 |
| Town Centre | 07:42 | 08:12 | 08:42 |
| Hospital | 07:51 | 08:21 | 08:51 |
| Beach Road | 08:05 | 08:35 | 09:05 |

Each **column** is one bus. Each **row** is one stop. Read down a column to follow one bus on its trip.

### Example

How long does Bus 2 take from the Library to Beach Road?

Bus 2 leaves the Library at 08:00 and gets to Beach Road at 08:35. That is **35 minutes**.

> **Tip:** To find the latest bus that gets you somewhere in time, look along the row for that stop. Find the last time that is not too late, then read up the column.

## Calendars

A **calendar** shows the days, weeks and months of a year. Different calendars follow different things in the sky.

- **Solar calendars** follow the Sun. The **Gregorian calendar**, which we use for school terms and birthdays, has 365 days, or 366 days in a leap year. It is based on the time Earth takes to go around the Sun.
- **Lunar calendars** follow the Moon. The Moon goes through all its phases in about 29 and a half days.

The **maramataka** is the Māori lunar calendar. Each night of the Moon's cycle has a name. The maramataka has long been used to plan activities such as planting, fishing and gathering kai, and different iwi have their own maramataka. The Chinese calendar also uses the Moon for its months, which is why Lunar New Year falls on a different date each year. (It is really a mix of both kinds: some years get an extra month so the calendar keeps in step with the Sun.)

> **Tip:** The Matariki public holiday is on a different date each year, because it is set using the maramataka, not the Gregorian calendar.
