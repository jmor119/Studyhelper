Percentages are used for test scores, sale prices, weather forecasts and sports statistics. In this lesson you will change between fractions, decimals and percentages, compare them, find percentages of amounts, and work back to the whole.

## What is a percentage?

**Per cent** means "out of 100". A **percentage** is a fraction with a denominator of 100, written with the % sign.

35 out of 100 squares are shaded. That is $\frac{35}{100}$, or 0.35 as a **decimal**, or 35%. All three are **equivalent**: they show the same amount.

The **whole** is always 100%. If 35% of the grid is shaded, then $100\% - 35\% = 65\%$ is not shaded.

Percentages help you compare amounts with the whole. A percentage can even be more than 100%. If a school raised 150% of its fundraising goal, it raised one and a half times the goal.

## Converting between fractions, decimals and percentages

- **Percentage to decimal:** divide by 100. $45\% = 0.45$ and $7\% = 0.07$.
- **Decimal to percentage:** multiply by 100. $0.6 = 60\%$ and $1.25 = 125\%$.
- **Fraction to percentage:** make the denominator 100. $\frac{3}{20} = \frac{15}{100} = 15\%$. Or divide: $\frac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8 = 0.375 = 37.5\%$.
- **Percentage to fraction:** write it over 100 and simplify. $35\% = \frac{35}{100} = \frac{7}{20}$.

These are worth knowing by heart:

| Fraction | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{4}$ | $\frac{3}{4}$ | $\frac{1}{5}$ | $\frac{1}{10}$ | $\frac{1}{8}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decimal | 0.5 | 0.25 | 0.75 | 0.2 | 0.1 | 0.125 |
| Percentage | 50% | 25% | 75% | 20% | 10% | 12.5% |

> **Watch out:** $0.6$ is 60%, not 6%. Six tenths is the same as sixty hundredths.

## Comparing and ordering

To compare a fraction, a decimal and a percentage, change them all into the same form. Percentages are usually easiest.

### Example
Put $\frac{2}{5}$, 0.38 and 41% in order from smallest to largest.

- $\frac{2}{5} = \frac{40}{100} = 40\%$
- $0.38 = 38\%$
- $41\%$

So the order is 0.38, $\frac{2}{5}$, 41%.

### Example
Which is bigger, 0.5 or 45%? Change 0.5 into a percentage: $0.5 \times 100 = 50\%$. So 0.5 is bigger.

> **Watch out:** Don't compare the digits you can see. 0.5 has fewer digits than 45%, but 50% is more than 45%.

## Finding a percentage of an amount

Build percentages from easy ones:
- **50%** is half. **25%** is a quarter.
- **10%** is a tenth: divide by 10.
- **5%** is half of 10%. **1%** is a hundredth: divide by 100.

### Example
Find 35% of 260.
- 10% of 260 is 26.
- 30% is $3 \times 26 = 78$.
- 5% is half of 10%: 13.
- 35% is $78 + 13 = 91$.

### Example
Find 40% of 85. 10% is 8.5, so 40% is $4 \times 8.5 = 34$.

**Putting it together:**
- Percentages, decimals and fractions are three ways to write the same amount.
- Change everything into one form before you compare.
- Use 10%, 5% and 1% as building blocks.
- To find the whole, find an easy percentage first, then build up to 100%.
