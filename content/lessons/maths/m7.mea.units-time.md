We measure things every day: how long, how heavy, how much a bottle holds, and how long a trip takes. Choosing a sensible unit, and changing between units, makes measurements easy to use. You will also learn to read timetables like the ones at a bus stop.

## Choosing a sensible unit

Metric units are made from a **base unit** and a **prefix**.

- The base units are the **metre** (m) for length, the **gram** (g) for mass and the **litre** (L) for capacity.
- A **prefix** goes at the front of the base unit. It tells you the size of the unit.

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **kilo-** (k) | 1000 times bigger | 1 km = 1000 m, 1 kg = 1000 g |
| **centi-** (c) | 100 times smaller | 100 cm = 1 m |
| **milli-** (m) | 1000 times smaller | 1000 mm = 1 m, 1000 mL = 1 L |

Choose the unit that gives a sensible number. A pencil is about 15 cm long. In kilometres that is 0.00015 km, which is very hard to picture!

> **Tip:** Use benchmarks. A centimetre is about the width of your little fingernail. A kilogram is about the mass of a 1 litre bottle of water. A teaspoon holds about 5 mL.

## Converting metric units

To change units, multiply or divide by 10, 100 or 1000.

- Changing to a **smaller** unit? You need more of them, so **multiply**.
- Changing to a **bigger** unit? You need fewer of them, so **divide**.

### Examples

- 3.5 kg in grams: 1 kg = 1000 g, so $3.5 \times 1000 = 3500$ g.
- 250 cm in metres: 100 cm = 1 m, so $250 \div 100 = 2.5$ m.
- 2 L 350 mL in litres: 350 mL is 0.35 L, so the total is 2.35 L.

> **Watch out:** 4 L 50 mL is 4.05 L, not 4.5 L. 50 mL is only $\frac{50}{1000}$ of a litre.

## Units of time

Time does not work in tens. Learn these facts:

- 60 seconds = 1 minute
- 60 minutes = 1 hour
- 24 hours = 1 day, and 7 days = 1 week

To find a **fraction of an hour**, find that fraction of 60 minutes.

### Examples

- $\frac{3}{4}$ of an hour: $60 \div 4 = 15$, and $3 \times 15 = 45$ minutes.
- $\frac{2}{5}$ of an hour: $60 \div 5 = 12$, and $2 \times 12 = 24$ minutes.
- $1\frac{1}{2}$ hours $= 60 + 30 = 90$ minutes.
- 135 minutes $= 120 + 15$ minutes. That is 2 hours 15 minutes, or $2\frac{1}{4}$ hours.

> **Watch out:** 1.5 hours is 1 hour **30** minutes, not 1 hour 50 minutes. Half an hour is 30 minutes.

## Durations and 24-hour time

A **duration** is how long something takes. To find a duration, **count on**. Go to the next o'clock first, then count the whole hours, then the minutes that are left.

From 9:45 am to 11:20 am is $15 + 60 + 20 = 95$ minutes. That is 1 hour 35 minutes.

**24-hour time** counts the hours from midnight, 00:00, up to 23:59. It always uses four digits.

- Morning times keep the same hour: 7:30 am is 07:30.
- From 1:00 pm onwards, add 12 to the hour: 4:15 pm is 16:15.
- Midday is 12:00 and midnight is 00:00.

> **Watch out:** Don't subtract times as if they were decimals. From 9:45 to 11:20 is not $11.20 - 9.45 = 1.75$ hours. There are 60 minutes in an hour, not 100.

Ana needs to be at the Pool by 16:10. Bus B gets there at 16:01. Bus C gets there at 16:35, which is too late. So the latest bus Ana can catch is **Bus B**, leaving School at 15:40.

> **Tips for timetable questions:**
> - Find the right row (stop) and the right column (bus) first.
> - Count on to work out durations.
> - "The latest bus" means the last bus that still arrives in time.
