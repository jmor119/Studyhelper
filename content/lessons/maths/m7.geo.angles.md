When lines meet or cross, the angles they make follow a few simple rules. If you know the rules, you can work out a missing angle without a protractor, and explain how you know.

## Complementary and supplementary angles

**Adjacent** angles sit side by side. They share a corner (the vertex) and one arm.

- Two angles are **complementary** if they add up to 90°. Together they make a right angle.
- Two angles are **supplementary** if they add up to 180°. Together they make a straight line.

Two lines that meet at a right angle are **perpendicular**. A small square in the corner shows a right angle.

## Vertically opposite angles

When two straight lines cross, they make four angles. The angles directly across from each other are **vertically opposite** angles. Vertically opposite angles are always equal.

### Example

Angle $c$ is opposite the 70° angle, so $c = 70°$.

Angle $b$ sits next to the 70° angle on a straight line, so $b = 180° - 70° = 110°$.

Angle $d$ is opposite $b$, so $d = 110°$ too.

Check: $70° + 110° + 70° + 110° = 360°$, which is a full turn. ✓

## Parallel lines and transversals

**Parallel** lines go the same way and never meet. On a diagram, matching arrows show that lines are parallel. A **transversal** is a line that crosses them.

When a transversal crosses two parallel lines:

- **Corresponding angles** are in the same position at each crossing. They are equal. Look for an **F** shape.
- **Alternate angles** are between the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal. They are equal. Look for a **Z** shape.

## Using equations to find angles

Sometimes angles are written with a letter, like $x°$ or $2x°$. Use an angle fact to write an **equation**, then solve it.


### Example

The angles $x°$, $2x°$ and 30° sit on a straight line, so they add to 180°.

1. Write the equation: $x + 2x + 30 = 180$
2. Collect like terms: $3x + 30 = 180$
3. Subtract 30 from both sides: $3x = 150$
4. Divide both sides by 3: $x = 50$
5. Check: $50 + 100 + 30 = 180$ ✓

So the three angles are 50°, 100° and 30°.

## Giving reasons

In a test you will often be asked to **give reasons**. For each step, write the angle fact you used.

| Fact | Reason to write |
|---|---|
| They add to 90° | angles in a right angle (complementary) |
| They add to 180° | angles on a straight line (supplementary) |
| They add to 360° | angles around a point |
| They are equal | vertically opposite angles |
| They are equal | corresponding angles (parallel lines, F shape) |
| They are equal | alternate angles (parallel lines, Z shape) |

For example: "$x = 65°$ because corresponding angles on parallel lines are equal."

> **Tip:** Diagrams are often marked "Not to scale". Work the angles out instead of measuring them.

> **Watch out:** Corresponding (F) and alternate (Z) angles are both equal, but they are different reasons. Name the right one.
