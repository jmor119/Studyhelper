Have you heard a weather report say "minus three degrees" on a frosty morning? Numbers less than zero are everywhere: in cold weather, in freezers, and when someone owes money.

## What are negative numbers?

A **negative number** is less than zero. We write it with a negative sign: −4 is said "negative four". A **positive number** is greater than zero. **Zero** is neither positive nor negative.

On a **number line** that goes across, negative numbers are to the **left** of 0.

- Start at −6 and count back in 2s: −6, −8, −10, −12, …

When you count forward, the numbers get bigger, so negative numbers get closer to 0. When you count back, the numbers get smaller.

> **Tip:** Say the numbers out loud as you cross 0: "two, one, zero, negative one, negative two".

## Temperature and money

**Temperature:** when the temperature goes past 0, count to 0 first.

It is −3 °C at 7 am and it warms up by 8 degrees. Count up 3 degrees to reach 0 °C, then 5 more. It is now **5 °C**.

To find the difference between −4 °C and 6 °C, count from −4 up to 0 (4 degrees), then from 0 up to 6 (6 degrees). The difference is $4 + 6 = 10$ degrees.

**Money:** a **debt** is money you owe. If Kai owes \$5, we can write this as −\$5. If Kai then earns \$8 and pays back what he owes, he has \$3 left. On a number line, −5 goes up 8 to 3.
