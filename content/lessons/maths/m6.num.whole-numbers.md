You add, subtract and multiply all the time: scores, prices, crowds and kai for camp. This lesson shows quick ways to work in your head, how the column method works, how to multiply by a two-digit number, and how to check your answer with an estimate.

## Adding and subtracting in your head

The answer to an adding question is the **sum**. The answer to a subtracting question is the **difference**.

You can often work in your head. Choose the most **efficient** way, which means the quickest way that you can do correctly.

**Partition** (split) numbers into tens and ones:

$67 + 58$: tens $60 + 50 = 110$, ones $7 + 8 = 15$, so the sum is $110 + 15 = 125$.

## The column method

For bigger numbers, use the **column method**. Line up the digits by place value: ones under ones, tens under tens. Always start with the ones.

### Example: $4{,}582 + 2{,}739$

$$
\begin{array}{rr} & 4{,}582 \\ + & 2{,}739 \\ \hline & 7{,}321 \end{array}
$$

- Ones: $2 + 9 = 11$. Write 1 and **regroup** the 10 ones as 1 ten in the tens column.
- Tens: $8 + 3 + 1 = 12$. Write 2 and regroup 1 hundred.
- Hundreds: $5 + 7 + 1 = 13$. Write 3 and regroup 1 thousand.
- Thousands: $4 + 2 + 1 = 7$.

### Example: $5{,}204 - 1{,}867$

In the ones, 4 is smaller than 7. There are 0 tens, so rename 1 hundred as 10 tens, then 1 ten as 10 ones. Now you have 14 ones and 9 tens: $14 - 7 = 7$ and $9 - 6 = 3$. In the hundreds, 1 is smaller than 8, so rename 1 thousand: $11 - 8 = 3$. Then $4 - 1 = 3$. The answer is **3,337**.

> **Watch out:** Never swap the digits round to take the smaller from the bigger. Rename from the next column instead.

## Multiplying

The answer to a multiplying question is the **product**. Know your times tables up to $12 \times 12$. Use them for bigger numbers too: $7 \times 8 = 56$, so $7 \times 80 = 560$.

**Partition** to multiply by a one-digit number:

$347 \times 6 = 300 \times 6 + 40 \times 6 + 7 \times 6 = 1{,}800 + 240 + 42 = 2{,}082$

The written method does the same thing. For $542 \times 12$:

$$
\begin{array}{rr} & 542 \\ \times & 12 \\ \hline & 1{,}084 \\ + & 5{,}420 \\ \hline & 6{,}504 \end{array}
$$

The first line is $542 \times 2$. The second line is $542 \times 10$, so it ends in a 0.

## Estimate to check

An **estimate** is a quick, rough answer. Round the numbers to friendly ones, then work it out.

- $4{,}812 + 3{,}179$ is about $5{,}000 + 3{,}000 = 8{,}000$. (The exact answer is 7,991.)
- $46 \times 23$ is about $50 \times 20 = 1{,}000$. So an answer of 1,058 makes sense.

Estimates help you catch mistakes. Suppose Tama works out $46 \times 23$ and gets 230. That is much smaller than 1,000, so it can't be right. (Tama forgot that the 2 in 23 means 2 tens.)

> **Tip:** Estimate first, then calculate. If the two are far apart, check your working.

## Putting it together

In a word problem, first decide which operation to use.

| Words like… | Usually mean… |
|---|---|
| altogether, total, sum | add |
| how many more, difference, left | subtract |
| each, rows of, lots of | multiply |

### Example

School camp costs \$145 for each student, and 27 students are going. The school has collected \$2,850. How much more does it need?

1. Total cost: $145 \times 27$. Work out $145 \times 7 = 1{,}015$ and $145 \times 20 = 2{,}900$, then add: $1{,}015 + 2{,}900 = 3{,}915$. So camp costs \$3,915.
2. Still needed: $3{,}915 - 2{,}850 = 1{,}065$. The school needs **\$1,065** more.
3. Check with an estimate: $150 \times 30 = 4{,}500$, so \$3,915 is a sensible total.

> **Tip:** Write down each step. If one step goes wrong, you can still get marks for the others.
