An equation like $2x + 3 = 11$ has one answer. An **inequality** like $2x + 3 < 11$ has a whole range of answers. Inequalities describe limits in real life, such as a lift that holds at most 600 kg or a budget you mustn't go over.

## Inequality symbols

| Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| $<$ | **less than** | $x < 4$: $x$ is less than 4 |
| $>$ | **greater than** | $x > -2$: $x$ is greater than $-2$ |
| $\le$ | less than or equal to | $x \le 7$: $x$ is 7 or less |
| $\ge$ | greater than or equal to | $x \ge 0$: $x$ is 0 or more |

The **solution set** is all the values that make the inequality true. For $x < 4$ it includes 3, 0, $-10$ and 3.99, but not 4 itself. There are infinitely many solutions, including decimals and fractions.

> **Tip:** The symbol points to the smaller side, and the open end faces the bigger side. $3 < 8$ reads "3 is less than 8".

> **Watch out:** $x > 5$ and $5 < x$ mean the same thing. Read the inequality from the letter outwards.

## Showing solutions on a number line

A number line shows the whole solution set at once.

- An **open circle** (not filled in) means the number is **not** included: used for $<$ and $>$.
- A **filled circle** means the number **is** included: used for $\le$ and $\ge$.
- The arrow shows which way the solutions go: right for greater, left for less.

For example, $x \ge -2$ has a filled circle at $-2$ and an arrow pointing right. $x < 3$ has an open circle at 3 and an arrow pointing left.

## Solving inequalities

Solve an inequality the same way as an equation: do the same thing to both sides using inverse operations. Adding, subtracting, or multiplying and dividing by a **positive** number keeps the inequality sign the same.

### Example

Solve $3x + 4 < 19$.

1. Subtract 4 from both sides: $3x < 15$
2. Divide both sides by 3: $x < 5$

Check with a value in the solution set, such as $x = 4$: $3 \times 4 + 4 = 16$, and $16 < 19$ ✓

### Example: unknowns on both sides

Solve $5x - 2 \ge 2x + 10$.

Subtract $2x$: $3x - 2 \ge 10$. Add 2: $3x \ge 12$. Divide by 3: $x \ge 4$.

### Whole-number answers

Sometimes the answer must be a whole number. If $4k \le 30$, then $k \le 7.5$, so the greatest whole number is 7.

> **Watch out:** with $<$, the boundary isn't included. The largest integer with $x < 5$ is 4, not 5.

## Multiplying or dividing by −1

Start with a true statement: $2 < 5$. Multiply both sides by $-1$ and you get $-2$ and $-5$. But $-2$ is to the **right** of $-5$ on a number line, so $-2 > -5$.

When you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, **reverse the inequality sign**: $<$ becomes $>$, and $\le$ becomes $\ge$.

### Example

Solve $-x > 4$.

Multiply both sides by $-1$ and reverse the sign: $x < -4$.

Check with $x = -5$: $-(-5) = 5$, and $5 > 4$ ✓

### Example

Solve $10 - x \le 3$.

Subtract 10: $-x \le -7$. Multiply by $-1$ and reverse the sign: $x \ge 7$.

**Another way** that avoids negatives: add $x$ to both sides to get $10 \le 3 + x$, then subtract 3: $7 \le x$, which is the same as $x \ge 7$.

## Exam tips

- **Keep the inequality sign in every line** of working. Don't switch to $=$ halfway through.
- **Check with a test value** from your solution set. Substitute it into the original inequality.
- **Open or filled?** $<$ and $>$ use open circles; $\le$ and $\ge$ use filled circles.
- **Only multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the sign.** Adding or subtracting a negative number does not.
- **Word problems:** words like "at most" mean $\le$, and "at least" means $\ge$. Then think about whether the answer has to be a whole number.
