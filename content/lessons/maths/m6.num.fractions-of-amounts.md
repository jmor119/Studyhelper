How many lollies is $\frac{3}{4}$ of a bag of 28? How many minutes is $\frac{2}{3}$ of an hour? In this lesson you will find fractions of numbers, and work backwards to find the whole when you only know a part.

## Unit fractions of an amount

A **unit fraction** has 1 as its numerator, like $\frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{1}{4}$ or $\frac{1}{10}$.

The word **of** tells you to find that part of the **whole**. To find a unit fraction of an amount, share the amount into equal parts. That means you **divide by the denominator**.

### Example
$\frac{1}{4}$ of 20: share 20 into 4 equal parts. $20 \div 4 = 5$.

## Non-unit fractions of an amount

A **non-unit fraction** has a numerator bigger than 1, like $\frac{3}{4}$ or $\frac{2}{5}$.

To find a non-unit fraction of an amount:

1. Find one part: **divide by the denominator**.
2. Take as many parts as the numerator says: **multiply by the numerator**.

### Example
$\frac{3}{4}$ of 28. One quarter is $28 \div 4 = 7$. Three quarters is $3 \times 7 = 21$.

$\frac{2}{3}$ of 240. One third is $240 \div 3 = 80$. Two thirds is $2 \times 80 = 160$.

## Finding the whole

Sometimes you know the part and need to find the whole. Work backwards.

### Example
$\frac{3}{4}$ of a set of cards is 90 cards. How many cards are in the whole set?

The shaded parts are labelled 90 and the whole bar has a question mark.","caption":"3 parts are 90, so 1 part is 30 and 4 parts are 120."}

- 3 parts are worth 90, so 1 part is $90 \div 3 = 30$.
- The whole has 4 parts: $4 \times 30 = 120$.

So there are 120 cards in the set. Check: $\frac{3}{4}$ of 120 is $120 \div 4 \times 3 = 90$ ✓

With a unit fraction it is just one step. If $\frac{1}{5}$ of a number is 9, the whole is $5 \times 9 = 45$.

## Problems with quantities and measures

Fractions of amounts are useful for measures. First change the whole into a smaller unit.

| Fraction | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| $\frac{3}{4}$ of an hour | $60 \div 4 \times 3$ | 45 minutes |
| $\frac{2}{5}$ of a kilogram | $1000 \div 5 \times 2$ | 400 g |
| $\frac{3}{10}$ of a metre | $100 \div 10 \times 3$ | 30 cm |

**How many are left?** Hemi has 40 lollies. He gives $\frac{3}{8}$ of them away. $\frac{1}{8}$ of 40 is 5, so he gives away $3 \times 5 = 15$. He has $40 - 15 = 25$ left.

Another way: he keeps $\frac{5}{8}$ of them, and $5 \times 5 = 25$.

## Putting it together

- **Unit fraction of an amount:** divide by the denominator.
- **Non-unit fraction:** divide by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator.
- **Finding the whole:** divide the part by the numerator to find one part, then multiply by the denominator.
- **Draw a bar model.** Split a bar into equal parts. It shows which part you know and which part you need.
- **Check it makes sense.** A fraction less than 1 of an amount is always smaller than the amount.
