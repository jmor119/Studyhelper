Decimals show parts of a whole. You see them on price tags, on measuring jugs and in sports results, like a 100 m race won in 12.35 seconds. In this lesson you will read, compare, multiply, divide, add and subtract decimals with up to three decimal places.

## Tenths, hundredths and thousandths

A **decimal** is a number with a **decimal point**. The decimal point separates the whole numbers from the parts of a whole.

- Split 1 whole into 10 equal parts. Each part is one **tenth**: $\frac{1}{10} = 0.1$
- Split a tenth into 10 equal parts. Each part is one **hundredth**: $\frac{1}{100} = 0.01$
- Split a hundredth into 10 equal parts. Each part is one **thousandth**: $\frac{1}{1000} = 0.001$. That is the same as dividing 1 by 1000.

Each place after the decimal point is called a **decimal place**.

| Ones | . | Tenths | Hundredths | Thousandths |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 4 | . | 3 | 0 | 7 |

So $4.307$ is 4 ones, 3 tenths, 0 hundredths and 7 thousandths. The 0 holds the empty hundredths place.

### Example
Write $\frac{47}{1000}$ as a decimal. Thousandths need three decimal places, so it is $0.047$.

A number line helps you see decimals. Between $0.3$ and $0.4$ there are 10 hundredths.

## Comparing and ordering decimals

To compare decimals:

1. Line up the decimal points.
2. Add zeros on the end so the numbers have the same number of decimal places. Adding zeros on the end does not change a decimal: $0.5 = 0.50 = 0.500$.
3. Compare the digits from the left: ones first, then tenths, then hundredths.

### Example
Which is bigger, $0.6$ or $0.45$? Write $0.6$ as $0.60$. 60 hundredths is more than 45 hundredths, so $0.6 > 0.45$.

> **Watch out:** More digits does not mean a bigger number. $0.45$ has more digits than $0.6$, but it is smaller.

### Example
Order $2.35$, $2.305$, $2.5$ and $2.053$ from smallest to largest. With three places they are $2.350$, $2.305$, $2.500$ and $2.053$. So the order is $2.053$, $2.305$, $2.35$, $2.5$.

## Multiplying and dividing by 10, 100 and 1000

When you multiply by 10, every digit becomes 10 times bigger, so it moves **one place to the left**. Multiplying by 100 moves the digits two places left, and multiplying by 1000 moves them three places left.

Dividing moves the digits to the **right** in the same way, so the number gets smaller.

| Calculation | Digits move | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| $1.3 \times 10$ | 1 place left | $13$ |
| $0.45 \times 100$ | 2 places left | $45$ |
| $2.5 \times 1000$ | 3 places left | $2500$ |
| $36 \div 10$ | 1 place right | $3.6$ |
| $27.5 \div 100$ | 2 places right | $0.275$ |
| $6 \div 1000$ | 3 places right | $0.006$ |

> **Tip:** Use zeros to fill empty places. In $2.5 \times 1000$, the 2 moves to the thousands place and the 5 to the hundreds place, so zeros fill the tens and ones: $2500$.

## Adding and subtracting decimals

1. Write the numbers in columns with the decimal points **lined up**.
2. Fill any empty places with zeros.
3. Add or subtract like whole numbers. Put the decimal point in the answer straight below the others.

### Example
$4.5 + 0.275$

$$\begin{array}{r} 4.500 \\ +\ 0.275 \\ \hline 4.775 \end{array}$$

### Example
$6 - 2.48$. Write 6 as $6.00$.

$$\begin{array}{r} 6.00 \\ -\ 2.48 \\ \hline 3.52 \end{array}$$

> **Watch out:** Line up the decimal points, not the last digits. If you line up the 5 in 4.5 with the 5 in 0.275, you are adding tenths to thousandths.

## Putting it together

- **Name the places:** tenths, hundredths, thousandths. Zeros hold empty places, as in $0.047$.
- **Compare** by giving every number the same number of decimal places.
- **× 10, 100, 1000** moves digits 1, 2 or 3 places left. **÷** moves them right.
- **Add and subtract** with the decimal points lined up.
- **Check your answer makes sense.** If you add $0.275$ to $4.5$, the answer should be a bit more than $4.5$.

### Example
Mia has a 2 L bottle of water. She pours out $0.35$ L and then $0.6$ L. How much is left?

Poured out: $0.35 + 0.60 = 0.95$ L. Left: $2.00 - 0.95 = 1.05$ L.
