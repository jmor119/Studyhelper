How much sand fills a sandpit? How much water fits in a fish tank? These are volume questions. In this lesson you will find the volume of cubes and rectangular prisms, work backwards to missing lengths, and link volume to litres.

## What is volume?

**Volume** is the amount of space a 3D object takes up. Volume is three-dimensional (it has length, width and height), so it is measured in **cubic units**.

- A **cubic centimetre** (cm³) is the space inside a cube with edges of 1 cm.
- A **cubic metre** (m³) is the space inside a cube with edges of 1 m.

You can find a volume by counting cubes in layers.

## Volume formulas

A **rectangular prism** is a box shape. Its three **dimensions** are its length, width and height.

$V = l \times w \times h$, which is often written $V = lwh$.

A **cube** has all its edges the same length, so $V = l \times l \times l = l^3$.

### Example 1

For the prism above: $V = 6 \times 4 \times 5 = 24 \times 5 = 120$ cm³.

### Example 2

A cube has edges of 3 m. $V = 3^3 = 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$ m³.

> **Tip:** You can multiply in any order, so choose an easy pair first. $25 \times 7 \times 4 = 25 \times 4 \times 7 = 100 \times 7 = 700$.

## Working backwards

If you know the volume, you can find a missing length.

**A missing height.** A box has a volume of 180 cm³. It is 6 cm long and 5 cm wide. The base is $6 \times 5 = 30$ cm², so the height is $180 \div 30 = 6$ cm.

**The edge of a cube.** A cube has a volume of 64 cm³. Find the number that multiplies by itself three times to make 64. $4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$, so each edge is 4 cm.

It helps to know the first few cube numbers:

| Edge | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of the cube | 1 | 8 | 27 | 64 | 125 | 1000 |

```check

    "Which number times itself, times itself again, makes 125?"
  ],
  "solution": "$5 \\times 5 \\times 5 = 125$, so each edge is 5 m."
}
```

### Example

- Tall part: $4 \times 5 \times 4 = 80$ cm³

> **Watch out:** Each box needs three lengths. Here the depth, 4 cm, is the same for both parts.

## Capacity

**Capacity** is how much a container can hold. It is often measured in millilitres (mL) and litres (L).

- 1 cm³ holds 1 mL.
- 1000 cm³ holds 1 L.
- 1 m³ holds 1000 L.


### Example

A fish tank is 50 cm long, 30 cm wide and 40 cm high. Its volume is $50 \times 30 \times 40 = 60{,}000$ cm³. Divide by 1000 to get litres: the tank holds 60 L.

> **Putting it together:**
> - Volume = length × width × height. Units like cm³ or m³.
> - To work backwards, divide (or find the cube's edge).
> - Split a composite solid into boxes and add.
> - 1 cm³ = 1 mL, and 1000 cm³ = 1 L.
