An **equation** says two things are equal. Solving it means finding the value of the unknown that makes it true. Think of a set of balance scales: whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other.

## Undoing operations

To get $x$ on its own, undo whatever has been done to it, using **inverse operations**:

| If the equation has… | …undo it by |
|---|---|
| $+ 7$ | subtracting 7 |
| $- 7$ | adding 7 |
| $\times 3$ (written $3x$) | dividing by 3 |
| $\div 4$ (written $\frac{x}{4}$) | multiplying by 4 |

### Example

Solve $x + 9 = 4$.

Subtract 9 from both sides: $x = 4 - 9 = -5$.

> **Check:** $-5 + 9 = 4$ ✓. Always substitute your answer back in. It takes five seconds and catches most mistakes.

## Two-step equations

When there are two operations, undo them in **reverse order**. In $3x + 5 = 20$, the $x$ was multiplied by 3 and *then* had 5 added, so undo the $+5$ first, then the $\times 3$.

### Example

Solve $3x + 5 = 20$.

1. Subtract 5 from both sides: $3x = 15$
2. Divide both sides by 3: $x = 5$

> **Check:** $3 \times 5 + 5 = 20$ ✓

> **Watch out:** With a negative coefficient, like $-2x = 8$, divide by $-2$ (not $2$): $x = -4$.

## Brackets and unknowns on both sides

**Brackets:** either expand first, or divide both sides by the number outside the bracket.

$5(x - 2) = 30 \;\Rightarrow\; x - 2 = 6 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 8$

**Unknowns on both sides:** move the $x$ terms to one side first. It's usually easiest to move the smaller $x$ term.

### Example

Solve $7x - 4 = 3x + 12$.

1. Subtract $3x$ from both sides: $4x - 4 = 12$
2. Add 4: $4x = 16$
3. Divide by 4: $x = 4$

## Equations with fractions

When the unknown is divided, multiply both sides to **clear the fraction**.

### Example

Solve $\dfrac{x + 4}{3} = 5$.

1. Multiply both sides by 3: $x + 4 = 15$
2. Subtract 4: $x = 11$

When $x$ is multiplied by a fraction, multiply by the **reciprocal**: $\frac{2}{3}x = 8 \Rightarrow x = 8 \times \frac{3}{2} = 12$.

> **Tip:** The curriculum prefers improper fractions like $\frac{7}{2}$ over mixed numbers in algebra. Both are fine as final answers here.

## Forming equations from words

Many exam questions describe a situation. Turn it into an equation:

1. Choose a letter for the unknown and say what it means.
2. Write the equation.
3. Solve it, then answer the question in a sentence with units.

### Example

Mere buys 3 drink bottles and a $\$12$ lunchbox. She spends $\$39$ altogether. How much is one bottle?

Let $b$ = price of one bottle. Then $3b + 12 = 39$, so $3b = 27$ and $b = 9$.

Each bottle costs **\$9**.
