Algebra uses letters to stand for numbers. Once you know how to write things the algebra way, you can make long expressions short and tidy. This lesson shows you how.

## Letters that stand for numbers

A **variable** is a letter that stands for a number. It can be:

- an **unknown** number we want to find, like $a$ in $3a = 18$
- a number that can **change**, like the cost of $n$ pies
- a value in a **formula**, like $l$ and $w$ in $A = lw$

An **expression** is a group of numbers and letters joined by operations, like $3b + 2$. It has no equals sign.

A **term** is one part of an expression. Terms are separated by $+$ and $-$ signs. The expression $3b + 2$ has two terms: $3b$ and $2$.

### Example

Kai has $k$ lollies. Hana has 4 more than Kai. We can write Hana's lollies as the expression $k + 4$. If Kai has 10 lollies, Hana has 14.

## Writing it the algebra way

**Algebraic notation** is the short way of writing things in algebra. We leave out the × sign, write the number first, and put letters in alphabetical order.

| Instead of | We write |
|---|---|
| $b + b + b$ | $3b$ |
| $3 \times b$ or $b \times 3$ | $3b$ |
| $1b$ | $b$ |
| $a \times b$ or $b \times a$ | $ab$ |
| $a \times a$ | $a^2$ |
| $a \times a \times a$ | $a^3$ |
| $a \div 4$ | $\frac{a}{4}$ |

The number in front of a letter is called the **coefficient**. In $7y$ the coefficient is 7. In $y$ the coefficient is 1.

> **Watch out:** $a^2$ is not the same as $2a$. If $a = 5$, then $a^2 = 5 \times 5 = 25$, but $2a = 2 \times 5 = 10$.

## Like terms

**Like terms** have exactly the same letter part. The coefficients can be different.

- $3a$ and $5a$ are like terms.
- $3a$ and $3b$ are not like terms (different letters).
- $a$ and $a^2$ are not like terms ($a^2$ means $a \times a$).
- $4$ and $4a$ are not like terms (one has no letter).

To **simplify** an expression, collect the like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients:

$3a + a + a = 5a$ and $3b - 2b = b$

### Example

Simplify $5p + 4q - 2p + q$.

- $p$ terms: $5p - 2p = 3p$
- $q$ terms: $4q + q = 5q$

So the answer is $3p + 5q$. Keep each sign with the term just after it.

You can also simplify when terms are multiplied or divided: $3 \times 4a = 12a$, $2a \times 3b = 6ab$ and $12m \div 4 = 3m$.

## Writing expressions from words

Look for key words to choose the operation.

| Words | Operation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| more than, increased by, sum | add | 5 more than $n$: $n + 5$ |
| less than, decreased by, take away | subtract | 5 less than $n$: $n - 5$ |
| times, lots of, product, double | multiply | 3 times $n$: $3n$ |
| divided by, shared by, half of | divide | $n$ divided by 2: $\frac{n}{2}$ |

> **Watch out:** "5 less than $n$" means start with $n$ and take away 5. It is $n - 5$, not $5 - n$.

### Example

Pies cost $p$ dollars each. Mere buys 4 pies and a drink that costs \$3. The total cost in dollars is $4p + 3$.

## Putting it together

- A **variable** is a letter that stands for a number.
- Leave out × signs. Write the number first and letters in alphabetical order: $3ab$, not $ba3$.
- Write $b$, not $1b$. And remember $a^2$ means $a \times a$, not $2a$.
- Only **like terms** can be collected. Check that the letter parts match exactly.
- Keep each $+$ or $-$ sign with the term after it.
- Test your answer: put in a number for the letter and check that both versions give the same value.
