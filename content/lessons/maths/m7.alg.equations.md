An equation is like a puzzle: something equals something else, and one number is missing. In this lesson you will find the missing number, check your answer, and decide whether number sentences are true.

## Equations and solutions

An **equation** has an equals sign. Both sides have the same value, like $t + 7 = 12$. The letter is the **unknown**.

The **solution** is the number that makes the equation true. To **solve** an equation means to find the solution.

You can always **check** a solution by substituting it back in.

### Example

Is $x = 4$ the solution of $3x + 2 = 14$?

Check: $3 \times 4 + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14$ ✓. Both sides are 14, so yes.

**Trial and error** (guess, check, then improve your guess) also works. But it can be slow, especially with big numbers. Inverse operations are faster.

## One-step equations

Use the **inverse operation** to get the letter on its own. Inverse operations undo each other.

| If the letter has been… | Undo it by… |
|---|---|
| added to (+ 7) | subtracting 7 |
| subtracted from (− 7) | adding 7 |
| multiplied (× 4) | dividing by 4 |
| divided (÷ 3) | multiplying by 3 |

Always do the same thing to **both sides**, so the equation stays balanced.

### Examples

- $t + 7 = 12$: subtract 7 from both sides, so $t = 5$.
- $4m = 28$: divide both sides by 4, so $m = 7$.
- $\frac{k}{3} = 6$: multiply both sides by 3, so $k = 18$.
- $x + 9 = 4$: subtract 9 from both sides, so $x = 4 - 9 = -5$. On a number line, start at 4 and move 9 to the left.

### Example

Solve $2x + 3 = 11$.

1. Undo the + 3: subtract 3 from both sides. $2x = 8$.
2. Undo the × 2: divide both sides by 2. $x = 4$.
3. Check: $2 \times 4 + 3 = 11$ ✓

### Example

Solve $5s - 4 = 26$.

1. Undo the − 4: add 4 to both sides. $5s = 30$.
2. Undo the × 5: divide both sides by 5. $s = 6$.
3. Check: $5 \times 6 - 4 = 26$ ✓

## Forming equations

To **form** an equation, use a letter for the number you don't know. Then write the facts as an equation.

### Example

Sione buys some packets of seeds at \$3 each and a trowel for \$8. He spends \$29 altogether. How many packets did he buy?

Let $n$ be the number of packets. The packets cost $3n$ dollars, plus \$8 for the trowel:

$3n + 8 = 29$

Subtract 8: $3n = 21$. Divide by 3: $n = 7$. Sione bought 7 packets.

Check: $7 \times 3 + 8 = 29$ ✓

### Example

"I think of a number, double it and add 5. I get 17." The equation is $2n + 5 = 17$, so $2n = 12$ and $n = 6$.

## Number sentences and inequalities

A **number sentence** compares two amounts. It can be true or false. Work out each side, then compare.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $=$ | is equal to |
| $<$ | is less than |
| $>$ | is greater than |
| $\le$ | is less than or equal to |
| $\ge$ | is greater than or equal to |

A sentence with $<$, $>$, $\le$ or $\ge$ is an **inequality**.

### Example

Is $0.8 \times 12 \le 8 \times 0.5 + 8$ true or false?

- Left: $0.8 \times 12 = 9.6$ (because $8 \times 12 = 96$).
- Right: $8 \times 0.5 + 8 = 4 + 8 = 12$.

Is 9.6 less than or equal to 12? Yes, so it is **true**.

> **Tip:** Always check your solution by substituting it back in. If both sides match, you're right.
