Energy is what makes things happen: it moves cars, lights screens, warms houses and keeps you alive. It can't be made from nothing or destroyed, but it changes from one form to another all the time. This topic covers the forms of energy, how energy is transformed, where Aotearoa's energy comes from, and how heat moves.

## Forms of energy

Energy is measured in **joules (J)**. 1 kilojoule (kJ) = 1,000 J.

| Form | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Kinetic** | Energy of anything that moves | A netball flying through the air |
| **Gravitational potential** | Stored energy of an object raised up high | Water held behind a dam |
| **Elastic potential** | Stored energy of something stretched or squashed | A stretched bungy cord |
| **Chemical** | Stored in the bonds of substances | Food, petrol, batteries |
| **Thermal** (heat) | Energy of hot objects (moving particles) | A hot cup of tea |
| **Electrical** | Carried by an electric current | Power to a toaster |
| **Light** | Given out by light sources | The Sun, a lamp |
| **Sound** | Carried by vibrations | A speaker |
| **Nuclear** | Stored in the nucleus of atoms | Released in the Sun |

**Potential** (stored) energies: gravitational, elastic, chemical and nuclear.

## Energy transformations

An **energy transformation** is a change from one form of energy to another. We show them with **energy-flow diagrams**:

**Torch:** chemical (battery) → electrical → **light** (useful) + heat (wasted)

**Hydro power station:** gravitational potential (water in the lake) → kinetic (falling water) → kinetic (spinning turbine) → **electrical**

**Bungy jump** (like the Kawarau Bridge near Queenstown): gravitational potential → kinetic (falling) → elastic potential (the cord stretches)

More examples:
- **Car:** chemical (petrol) → kinetic + heat + sound
- **Solar panel:** light → electrical
- **Wind turbine:** kinetic (wind) → electrical
- **Speaker:** electrical → sound
- **Photosynthesis:** light → chemical (stored in glucose)

> **Tip:** Start a diagram from where the energy is first **stored** (battery, fuel, food, a high lake). Show the useful output and the wasted output.

## Conservation of energy and efficiency

The **law of conservation of energy**: energy **cannot be created or destroyed**. It can only be transformed from one form to another, or transferred from place to place. The total amount of energy stays the same.

So where does energy "go"? In every transformation, some of it becomes energy we don't want, usually **heat** (thermal energy) spreading into the surroundings. It comes from friction and from electric currents warming wires. This energy isn't destroyed, but it is so spread out that we can't use it again. That's why we call it **wasted**.

**Efficiency** tells you how much of the input energy ends up as useful output:

$$\text{efficiency} = \frac{\text{useful energy out}}{\text{total energy in}} \times 100\%$$

### Example

An electric motor is supplied with 500 J and gives out 400 J of kinetic energy.

Efficiency $= \dfrac{400}{500} \times 100\% = 80\%$. The other 100 J is wasted as heat and sound.

No machine is 100% efficient. An old-style light bulb is only about 5–10% efficient: most of its energy becomes heat, not light.

## Energy sources in Aotearoa

**Renewable** sources are naturally replaced and won't run out. **Non-renewable** sources took millions of years to form and will run out.

| Renewable | In Aotearoa |
|---|---|
| **Hydro** (falling water) | Our biggest source of electricity, more than half. Rivers such as the Waikato, Waitaki and Clutha. |
| **Geothermal** (heat from underground) | The central North Island, e.g. Wairākei near Taupō |
| **Wind** | Wind farms in windy places, e.g. around Palmerston North |
| **Solar** | Rooftop panels and new solar farms |

| Non-renewable | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Coal** | Burned at Huntly Power Station; releases lots of carbon dioxide |
| **Natural gas** | From Taranaki; used for power, heating and cooking |
| **Oil** | Made into petrol and diesel for transport |

Most of New Zealand's electricity, usually more than 80%, comes from renewable sources. But renewables have downsides too. Hydro depends on rain and can run short in dry years, wind doesn't always blow, and dams change rivers. Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide, which adds to climate change. New Zealand has no nuclear power stations.

## Heat transfer

Heat always flows from **hotter** places to **colder** places, in three ways.

**Conduction:** heat passes through a material as particles vibrate and pass energy to their neighbours. It happens best in solids. **Metals** are good conductors. Wood, plastic, wool, polystyrene and air are poor conductors, called **insulators**.

**Convection:** in liquids and gases, warm fluid expands, becomes **less dense** and **rises**. Cooler, denser fluid sinks to take its place, making a **convection current**. This is how a heater warms a whole room and how water heats in a pot.

**Radiation:** heat travels as **infrared radiation**, which needs **no particles**. That's how the Sun's heat crosses empty space to reach Earth. Dark, dull surfaces absorb and give out radiation well; light, shiny surfaces reflect it.

**Keeping heat in:** ceiling and wall insulation and double glazing trap air (a poor conductor). A vacuum flask has a vacuum between its walls (no particles, so no conduction or convection), shiny walls (reflect radiation) and a stopper (stops convection).

## Exam tips

- Learn the nine forms of energy and an example of each.
- Draw energy-flow diagrams with arrows, from the **stored** energy to the **useful** output, and show **wasted heat**.
- Never say energy is "used up" or "lost forever". Say it is **transformed**, usually into heat that spreads into the surroundings.
- Efficiency = useful out ÷ total in × 100%. Useful + wasted = total in.
- Renewable: hydro, geothermal, wind, solar. Non-renewable: coal, gas, oil. Give a New Zealand example.
- Conduction needs touching particles (solids); convection needs a fluid that can flow; radiation needs no particles at all.
