Plants don't eat. They make their own food from air, water and sunlight. This process, **photosynthesis**, feeds almost every food chain on Earth and puts oxygen into the air. In this lesson you'll learn how photosynthesis works, how a leaf is built for it, and how plants move water and sugars around.

## Photosynthesis

Plants are **producers**: they make their own food by photosynthesis.

$$\text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \xrightarrow{\text{light energy, chlorophyll}} \text{glucose} + \text{oxygen}$$

- It happens in **chloroplasts**, mostly in the leaves.
- The green pigment **chlorophyll** absorbs the light energy.
- **Carbon dioxide** comes from the air, through the stomata.
- **Water** comes from the soil, through the roots and xylem.

The **glucose** is used for:
- respiration, to release energy
- making **starch** for storage (e.g. in kūmara and potatoes)
- making **cellulose** for cell walls
- making other sugars, which are carried around the plant
- making proteins, together with nitrates from the soil

The **oxygen** is released into the air through the stomata.

## Respiration in plants

Like every living thing, plants respire to release energy from glucose:

$$\text{glucose} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \;(+\text{ energy released})$$

| | Photosynthesis | Respiration |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Chloroplasts (green cells only) | Mitochondria (every living cell) |
| When | Only in light | All the time, day and night |
| Uses | Carbon dioxide and water | Glucose and oxygen |
| Makes | Glucose and oxygen | Carbon dioxide and water |
| Energy | Stores light energy in glucose | Releases energy from glucose |

The two reactions are opposites: the products of one are the reactants of the other. In daylight, photosynthesis is usually faster than respiration, so overall a plant takes in carbon dioxide and gives out oxygen. At night it only respires, so it takes in oxygen and gives out carbon dioxide.

> **Watch out:** Plants respire **all the time**, not just at night. At night, respiration is simply the only process going on.

## Leaf structure

| Layer | Job |
|---|---|
| **Waxy cuticle** | Waterproof coating; reduces water loss |
| Upper epidermis | Thin and transparent, so light passes through |
| **Palisade mesophyll** | Tall cells packed with chloroplasts; most photosynthesis happens here |
| **Spongy mesophyll** | Loosely packed cells with air spaces, so gases can diffuse |
| Lower epidermis | Contains most of the **stomata** |
| **Guard cells** | Open and close each stoma |
| Veins | Contain xylem (brings water) and phloem (takes sugars away) |

A leaf is adapted for photosynthesis because it is:
- **broad and flat**, which gives a large surface area to absorb light
- **thin**, so carbon dioxide only has a short distance to diffuse
- full of **chloroplasts**, especially in the palisade layer near the top, where light is brightest
- supplied by **veins**, which bring water and carry sugars away

## Moving water and sugars

Plants have two transport tissues, found together in the veins:

| | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Carries | Water and dissolved minerals | Dissolved sugars |
| Direction | Up, from the roots to the leaves | From the leaves to where sugars are needed (up or down) |
| Cells | Dead, hollow tubes with strong walls | Living cells |

**Water's journey:** root hair cells absorb water from the soil by osmosis. It travels up the **xylem** to the leaves. In the leaves, it evaporates from the cells into the air spaces and escapes through the **stomata**. This loss of water vapour is called **transpiration**, and it pulls more water up the xylem, like sucking on a straw.

On hot, dry or windy days, plants lose water faster. To reduce water loss, guard cells can close the stomata, but this also lets less carbon dioxide in.

## Investigating photosynthesis

**Testing a leaf for starch:** leaves store the glucose they make as starch, so starch in a leaf shows that photosynthesis has happened.

1. Boil the leaf in water for about a minute.
2. Put it in hot ethanol, heated in a water bath, to remove the chlorophyll. Ethanol is flammable, so never heat it over a flame.
3. Rinse it in warm water to soften it.
4. Add iodine solution. **Blue-black = starch**; yellow-brown = no starch.

To check that a factor is needed, first **destarch** the plant by keeping it in the dark for about two days. Then:
- Cover part of a leaf with foil: only the uncovered part makes starch, so **light** is needed.
- Test a variegated (green and white) leaf: only the green parts make starch, so **chlorophyll** is needed.

**Measuring the rate:** pondweed gives off bubbles of oxygen. Count the bubbles per minute with a lamp at different distances. Brighter light gives more bubbles, up to a point.

The rate of photosynthesis depends on **light intensity**, **carbon dioxide concentration** and **temperature**.

## Exam tips

- Learn both word equations, and say where each reaction happens (chloroplasts vs mitochondria).
- Never write that plants only respire at night.
- "Xylem: water, up. Phloem: food (sugars), up or down."
- In leaf questions, link the structure to its job, e.g. "air spaces → carbon dioxide can diffuse to the palisade cells".
- In practical questions, explain each step of the starch test. The ethanol removes chlorophyll so the colour change shows up.
