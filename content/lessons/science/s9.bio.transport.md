Cells are constantly swapping substances with their surroundings: oxygen and glucose go in, and carbon dioxide and wastes come out. Everything has to cross the **cell membrane**. In this lesson you'll learn the three ways substances move: diffusion, osmosis and active transport. You'll also see why size matters.

## Diffusion

Particles in liquids and gases move about randomly. Because of this, they spread out from where there are lots of them to where there are fewer. This is **diffusion**: the net movement of particles from an area of **high concentration** to an area of **low concentration**.

- The difference in concentration is called the **concentration gradient**. Particles diffuse *down* the gradient.
- Diffusion is **passive**: the cell doesn't have to use energy.

Examples: oxygen diffusing from the alveoli into the blood; carbon dioxide diffusing into a leaf; the smell of a hāngī spreading through the air.

**Diffusion is faster when:**

| Factor | Why |
|---|---|
| The temperature is higher | Particles move faster |
| The concentration difference is bigger | More particles move down a steeper gradient |
| The distance is shorter | Particles have less far to travel |
| The surface area is bigger | More particles can cross at once |

## Osmosis

**Osmosis** is a special kind of diffusion: the movement of **water** across a **partially permeable membrane**, from a **dilute** solution (lots of water) to a more **concentrated** solution (less water). A partially permeable membrane lets small water molecules through, but not bigger molecules such as sugar.

**Plant cells**
- In pure water, water enters by osmosis. The vacuole swells and pushes on the cell wall, and the cell becomes firm (**turgid**). The strong wall stops it bursting.
- In a concentrated salt or sugar solution, water leaves. The cell goes floppy (**flaccid**), and the plant wilts.

**Animal cells** have no cell wall. In pure water, a red blood cell swells and may **burst**. In a concentrated solution, it **shrinks**.

> **Watch out:** In osmosis, it is always the **water** that moves, not the salt or sugar. Write "water moves out of the cell", not "salt moves in".

## Active transport

Sometimes a cell needs to take in a substance that is already more concentrated inside the cell than outside. Diffusion would carry it the wrong way, so the cell uses **active transport**: moving particles **against** the concentration gradient (from low to high), using **energy from respiration**. Carrier proteins in the cell membrane do the moving.

Examples:
- **Root hair cells** take in mineral ions such as nitrate, even when the soil has fewer ions than the root.
- The **small intestine** absorbs glucose, even when there is less glucose left in the gut than in the blood.

| | Diffusion | Osmosis | Active transport |
|---|---|---|---|
| What moves | Any particles | Water only | Particles, e.g. mineral ions |
| Direction | High → low | Dilute → concentrated solution | Low → high |
| Energy from the cell? | No | No | Yes |

## Surface area to volume ratio

Substances enter a cell through its **surface**, but they are used throughout its **volume**. The **surface area to volume ratio (SA:V)** compares the two.

For a cube with side length $L$: surface area $= 6L^2$ and volume $= L^3$.

| Side | Surface area | Volume | SA : V |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 cm | 6 cm² | 1 cm³ | 6 : 1 |
| 2 cm | 24 cm² | 8 cm³ | 3 : 1 |
| 3 cm | 54 cm² | 27 cm³ | 2 : 1 |

As an object gets **bigger, its SA:V ratio gets smaller**.

- **Cells are small**, so they have a large SA:V ratio and short distances. Diffusion can supply the whole cell quickly.
- **Large organisms** have a small SA:V ratio, and most of their cells are far from the surface. They need **exchange surfaces** with a huge surface area (alveoli, villi, root hairs) and a **transport system** (blood, or xylem and phloem) to reach every cell.
- Small animals also lose heat faster, because they have more surface for their size.

## Exam tips

- Learn the definitions word for word. They are easy marks: diffusion (high → low), osmosis (**water**, across a **partially permeable membrane**), active transport (low → high, **needs energy**).
- In osmosis questions, first decide which side is more concentrated. Water moves **towards** the more concentrated solution.
- In potato experiments, a gain in mass means water moved **in**, and a loss means water moved **out**. Percentage change = change ÷ starting mass × 100.
- For SA:V, show your working: surface area, volume, then SA ÷ V.
- When you explain why an organism needs lungs, gills or roots, link it to its **small SA:V ratio** and the long distances diffusion would have to cover.
