Every cell in your body needs a constant supply of food and oxygen to stay alive. Three body systems work together to deliver them: the **digestive**, **respiratory** and **circulatory** systems. In this lesson you'll learn how each one works and how they link up.

## Why cells need supplies

Your cells release energy from food by **respiration**, which happens in the mitochondria:

$$\text{glucose} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \;(+\text{ energy released})$$

To keep respiring, every cell needs a supply of glucose and oxygen, and its carbon dioxide must be taken away.

| System | Its job in this |
|---|---|
| Digestive | Breaks food down into small molecules such as glucose, and absorbs them into the blood |
| Respiratory | Takes oxygen into the blood and removes carbon dioxide |
| Circulatory | Carries glucose, oxygen and carbon dioxide between the organs and every cell |

> **Watch out:** Respiration is **not** breathing. Breathing just moves air in and out of the lungs. Respiration is the chemical reaction inside cells.

## The circulatory system

The **heart** is a muscular pump with four chambers. The two **atria** (top) receive blood, and the two **ventricles** (bottom) pump it out. Valves inside the heart stop blood flowing backwards.

- The **right side** pumps oxygen-poor blood to the lungs to pick up oxygen.
- The **left side** pumps oxygen-rich blood to the rest of the body. Its wall is thicker, because this trip needs more pressure.

| Vessel | Carries blood | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Artery** | Away from the heart | Thick, muscular, stretchy walls; high pressure |
| **Vein** | Back to the heart | Thinner walls; valves stop blood flowing backwards; low pressure |
| **Capillary** | Between arteries and veins, through the tissues | Walls one cell thick, so substances diffuse in and out |

**Blood** is made of plasma (the liquid, carrying dissolved glucose, carbon dioxide and wastes), **red blood cells** (carry oxygen using haemoglobin), **white blood cells** (fight infection) and **platelets** (help blood clot).

> **Tip:** **A**rteries carry blood **a**way from the heart.

## The respiratory system

Air travels: **nose or mouth → trachea (windpipe) → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli**.

The **alveoli** are millions of tiny air sacs at the ends of the bronchioles. Here, **gas exchange** happens by diffusion: oxygen moves from the air into the blood, and carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the air. Alveoli are adapted to make this fast:

- a huge total surface area
- walls one cell thick, so gases only travel a short distance
- a moist lining, where gases dissolve
- a rich supply of capillaries

**Breathing in:** the **diaphragm** contracts and flattens, and the muscles between the ribs pull the ribs up and out. The chest volume increases, the pressure inside drops, and air flows in. **Breathing out** is the reverse.

| Gas | Air breathed in | Air breathed out |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | about 21% | about 16% |
| Carbon dioxide | about 0.04% | about 4% |

Breathed-out air turns limewater cloudy much faster than room air does, because it contains more carbon dioxide.

## The digestive system

Food is made of large molecules, such as starch and protein, that are too big to get into the blood. **Digestion** breaks them into small, soluble molecules that can be absorbed.

- **Mechanical digestion:** breaking food into smaller pieces, e.g. chewing, and the stomach churning.
- **Chemical digestion:** **enzymes** breaking large molecules into small ones.

| Organ | What happens there |
|---|---|
| Mouth | Teeth chew; saliva contains amylase, which starts digesting starch |
| Oesophagus | A muscular tube that squeezes food down to the stomach |
| Stomach | Churns food; acid kills most germs; protease starts digesting protein |
| Liver and gall bladder | The liver makes **bile** (stored in the gall bladder), which breaks fat into tiny droplets |
| Pancreas | Makes enzymes that flow into the small intestine |
| Small intestine | Digestion finishes; small molecules are absorbed into the blood through the **villi** |
| Large intestine | Absorbs water from what is left |
| Rectum and anus | Store and then remove faeces |

The **villi** are millions of tiny finger-like folds. They give the small intestine a huge surface area, with thin walls and a rich blood supply, so absorption is fast.

## Enzymes

**Enzymes** are proteins that speed up chemical reactions without being used up. Each enzyme only works on one type of substance.

| Enzyme | Breaks down | Into |
|---|---|---|
| Amylase | Starch | Sugars |
| Protease | Proteins | Amino acids |
| Lipase | Fats | Fatty acids and glycerol |

Temperature matters. Human enzymes work best at about **37 °C**, body temperature. Colder than that, they work slowly. If they get too hot, their shape is damaged and they stop working: they are **denatured**.

> **Watch out:** Bile is not an enzyme. It doesn't break any chemical bonds. It just breaks fat into small droplets, which gives lipase more surface to work on.

## Putting it together

Follow the supplies to a leg muscle during a run:

1. Food is digested into **glucose**, which is absorbed into the blood in the small intestine.
2. **Oxygen** diffuses from the alveoli into the blood in the lungs.
3. The heart pumps the blood through arteries to the leg. In the capillaries, glucose and oxygen diffuse into the muscle cells.
4. The cells respire and release energy. **Carbon dioxide** diffuses into the blood, and veins carry it back to the heart and lungs to be breathed out.

During exercise, the muscles respire faster, so the **heart rate** and **breathing rate** both increase. This delivers oxygen and glucose faster and removes carbon dioxide faster.

> **Exam tips:**
> - Learn the respiration word equation. It links all three systems.
> - Use the word **diffuse** for gases moving in the alveoli and capillaries.
> - For an "adapted for" question, name the feature **and** say how it helps, e.g. "thin walls → short distance for diffusion".
> - To get beats per minute from a 15-second count, multiply by 4.
